# Devices, board, power and heat

Code: `src/features/board/sim` (import from `src/features/board/sim`). No DOM imports. **All numbers are placeholders** for a balance pass.

## Specs (`specs.ts`)

Devices are plain records in `DEVICE_SPECS` (`getSpec(id)`, `specsOfKind(kind)` gives the tier ladder). Common fields: `id, kind, tier, name, slot` (slot type needed; for a PSU the power connector), `price, idleW, loadW` (W at base clock), `heatFactor` (fraction of power that becomes heat), `clock {min, base, max}` (ticks per base tick; absent for PSU and cooler), `thermal {passive, mass, throttleC, unstableC, damageC}`, `visual` (opaque renderer key).

| Kind | Extra fields | Catalog (tier: clock base) |
|---|---|---|
| cpu | `engine: EngineConfig` | cpu0 unpipelined (1), cpu1 in-order no forwarding (2), cpu2 in-order + forwarding, 16-line cache (3) |
| ram | `sizeBytes, latency` (RAM ticks per access) | 16K lat 200, 32K lat 12 (2x clock), 64K lat 4 (3x clock) |
| psu | `watts` | 40 W and 100 W (`atx`), 250 W (`atx-plus`) |
| cooler | `coolingPerC` (W per degree C, CPU only), `vrmAirPerC` (airflow over the VRM) | stock 0.35 / 0.05, tower 0.8 / 0.25, liquid 1.8 / **0** |
| fan | `size` (mm), `airflow` (1 = a basic fan) | 40, 92, 120 and 140 mm, basic and high-airflow. Fits a case mount or a heatsink's spot of its size, see [`ASSEMBLY.md`](../cpu/ASSEMBLY.md) "Fan parts" |
| display | `writeLatency` | CLI monitor |
| vrm-sink | `coolingPerC` | vsink0 fins (0.25), vsink1 big fins (0.5), vsink2 fan fins (1.0 with a `fan40` turning, half of it without). Bolts onto the board VRM, slot `vrm-sink`, see [`VRM.md`](VRM.md) |
| storage | `capacityBytes, latencyTicks, blockTicks, enduranceBlocks, queueDepth, seekByDistance?, mechBadRate?, mirror?` | hdd0 HDD 96K (SATA, clock 1), ssd1 SATA SSD 64K (SATA, clock 2), nvme2 NVMe SSD 128K (M.2, clock 4), raid2 RAID 1 of two NVMe (M.2, mirror, level 16). A block device on the bus, see [`OS.md`](OS.md) |

Slot types: `cpu dimm pcie sata m2 usb cooler display atx atx-plus vrm-sink ram-sink tpm`. Accelerators ([`ACCEL.md`](ACCEL.md)): device kinds `gpu`, `npu`, `tpm`; the `tpm` header is one slot on Mainstream and Enthusiast and absent on Starter and Basic; GPU and NPU cards use `pcie` slots (competing with NICs); `PsuSpec.pcieLeads` (0, 0, 1 for `psu0..2`) is how many PCIe power leads a PSU has; a card drawing more than `PCIE_SLOT_W` (75 W) takes `cardLeads` PCIe leads (one per 75 W beyond the slot): `install` refuses it on Starter (no PCIe connector) and when the cards together need more leads than the PSU has (`pcieLeadsNeeded`). The card and TPM specs themselves come with T69. **Board tiers are a four-step ladder with a VRM each: Starter, Basic, Mainstream, Enthusiast (table, VRM and heatsinks in [`VRM.md`](VRM.md))**. The Starter board has 1 DIMM, 1 SATA, 1 USB, 1 display, ATX power, no PCIe, no M.2, no ethernet; slots a tier lacks are absent from `boardSlots` and from `snapshot.slots` (the renderer draws them unpopulated on one shared layout). `BoardTierSpec` = `{tier, name, price, slots, features: {ethernetMbps, usbPorts, displayPorts}, vrm: VrmSpec, visual}`; `features.ethernetMbps` (0, 100, 100, 1000) is a field only, the network phase will read it. The text below that mentions "tier 0 / tier 1" slot counts is the old two-tier layout: the old tier 0 is now Basic, the old tier 1 Mainstream. Board tiers (`BOARD_TIERS`): tier 0 = 1 cpu, 2 dimm, 1 sata, 1 usb, 1 atx, 1 cooler, 1 display; tier 1 adds dimms, pcie, a second sata, **1 m2**, `atx-plus` instead of `atx`. **`sata` and `m2` are real, typed and installable** (storage drives; slot ids `sata0`, `sata1`, `m20`; a drive's clock domain is named after its slot). SATA power rule: a SATA drive needs a free sata slot and its `loadW` inside the PSU rated load (no connector count); NVMe needs the m2 slot. Tier ladder and the unlock principle: [`TECHTREE.md`](TECHTREE.md). An `atx-plus` slot also accepts plain `atx` PSUs (`slotAccepts`). `upgradeBoard(board, tier)` moves to a bigger tier (or the same tier again while the board is burnt, which clears the flag) carrying devices over (see GAME.md purchases).

## Board (`board.ts`)

`Board = {tier, nextId, devices: InstalledDevice[], burnt?}` (`burnt`: the VRM burnt out, the board is dead), `InstalledDevice = {id, spec, slot, clock?, burnt?}`. Plain data; slots are derived from the tier (`boardSlots`, ids like `dimm0`).

- `install(board, specId, slotId?)` returns `{ok:true,id,slot}` or `{ok:false,reason}`: wrong slot type, slot used, no free slot, no such slot, or PSU limits. Auto-picks the first free slot of the right type; for a `pcie` card, the narrowest free slot it fits.
- **PCIe widths:** each board tier lists its slots' lane widths in `BoardTierSpec.pcieWidths` (Starter x16; Basic x16, x4; Mainstream x16, x8, x4; Enthusiast x32, x16, x8, x4), and a card spec's `pcieWidth` is the narrowest slot it fits (NICs x4, x4, x8; GPUs x8, x16, x32; NPUs x4, x8). The player reads 32 as `x16 Gen5` (`pcieLabel`): no real slot is wider than x16, and Gen5 doubles the rate. `install` / `placeInto` refuse a narrower slot (`slotRefusal`), `upgradeBoard` moves the widest cards first, and `boardFromJson` does not check widths so an older save keeps its cards. The board art's slot rails are as long as their width.
- PSU rule: a PSU needs a matching connector and `watts >= rated load` (sum of `loadW` at base clocks of all non-PSU parts). With a PSU present, a new part is refused if rated load would exceed it. Overclocking can still exceed the budget at run time (brownout below).
- `uninstall`, `setClock(board, id, clock)` (range checked), `boardToJson` / `boardFromJson` (validates; throws on bad saves).

Serialization shape (this is the board part of the save; temperatures are not saved, they restart at ambient):

```json
{ "tier": 0, "nextId": 6, "devices": [ { "id": "dev1", "spec": "cpu0", "slot": "cpu0", "clock": 1 }, { "id": "dev2", "spec": "ram0", "slot": "dimm0", "clock": 1, "burnt": true } ] }
```

## Power and heat (`thermal.ts`)

`const th = new ThermalModel(machine.prng); th.step(board, {cpu: load, ...})` once per base tick (load 0..1 per kind, default 1). Per device: `r = effClock / baseClock`; `power = idleW*r + (loadW-idleW)*load*r^3`; `T += (heat - (passive + coolerCoeff[CPU only]) * (T - 25)) / mass`. Effective clock = requested * thermal factor * PSU factor. Read `th.state(id)` (tempC, powerW, effClock, throttle, unstable, stress), `th.power`, `th.effectiveClock(id)`.

- **Throttle**: factor 1 up to `throttleC` (CPU 70), falling linearly to 0.8 at `unstableC` (85). Throttling only trims 20%, so heavy overclock or poor cooling still runs away.
- **Instability**: above `unstableC`, `th.errorRoll(id)` returns true with p = 1% + 2%/degree (max 50%) using the machine PRNG. It consumes PRNG state only while unstable. Engines are not wired to it yet; the sim decides what an error does.
- **Damage**: above `damageC` (CPU 95) for 10 accumulated ticks (each cool tick forgives 1) the device gets `burnt = true`: clock 0, no draw, until uninstalled/replaced.
- **Brownout**: total draw > PSU watts: all clocks scaled by `watts/draw` (forced throttle). Draw > 1.5x watts: hard trip, everything off (clock 0) until `th.powerCycle()`. No working PSU: off.
- **Airflow zones** (`airflow.ts`, tested in `airflow.test.ts`): the case's air is cooled and warmed per AREA, not as one lump. Areas: `north` (CPU, RAM, both VRMs), `cards` (GPU, NPU, NIC, TPM), `bay` (drives); a cooler, a sink, the PSU and the monitor use the case average. `FanPower.mounts` lists the case fans that turn (`front0..`, `top0..`, `rear`: own cable and the hub's); each area gets air from the front fans level with it and loses it to the exhaust that draws from it (the rear fan takes what crosses the case, the top fans the hot air over the processor, plus the shell's vents). Air goes through an area only with both an intake and an exhaust (a harmonic mean, so no exhaust or no intake leaves only a little stirring). `th.air[zone]` = `{ fans, flow, riseC }`: `fans` are case-fan equivalents (`CASE_FAN_K` W/C each, to every part in the area; with every fan turning, the old count times the area's `QUALITY`), `flow` 0..1 against the designed case, `riseC` how much the working cards (`CASE_HEAT_C_PER_W`) warm the area, more where the air does not move (up to `MAX_TRAPPED` times). With no `mounts` (a model run alone with `caseFans`) every area gets `caseFans` and the cards' heat, as before. `th.caseRiseC` is the mean of north and bay. **Drive cages:** the main cage stands in the front fans' air. Each drive there blocks a share of the fans level with it (`BAY_SHADE` per case tier, from the 3D models, checked in `fit.test.ts`; a hard disk blocks about four times what an SSD does). `zoneAir`'s `shade` (`cageShade`) takes that share of their air from the `north` and `cards` areas; the bay keeps it. A full cage of hard disks costs about one front fan. The basement's cage under the shroud (`BASE_BAYS`: sata0 and sata1) costs nothing, so the first two drives are free. Snapshot: `board.air` (`AirZoneSnap`). The lens (`room.md`) names a poorly ventilated part (`AIR 40%`). **Card fans:** a graphics or neural card has its own fans (`ThermalSpec.fanPerC`, W/C at full speed). Their speed `DeviceThermal.fan` (0..1, `DeviceSnap.fan`) follows the card's last temperature (`cardFan`: still below `CARD_FAN_START_C`, full at the throttle limit), is 0 when the machine is off or the card burnt, and cools the card by `fan x fanPerC`, less when the `cards` area has no air moving (`CARD_FAN_AIR_MIN` floor). The card view spins its blades by that number. The weights of this section (`QUALITY`, `COUPLE`, `REACH`, `DRAW_*`, `MAX_TRAPPED`, `CARD_FAN_*`) are placeholders, constants at the top of `airflow.ts` and `thermal.ts`; `airflow.test.ts` "the weights" guards the balance when you change them. **Liquid loop** (`thermal.ts`, `loop.test.ts`): a liquid cooler is a heat path, not a constant. The CPU gives heat to the coolant (`ThermalModel.loopC`, mass `LOOP_MASS`) through the block (`LOOP_BLOCK_K`, only with the pump's `cpufan` cable in); the coolant gives it to the air through the pump housing (`pumpPerC`) and the radiator (`FanPower.loopK`: its `coolingPerC` by the share of its fans that turn). A **front** radiator takes room air and gives its heat to it: `loopRejectW` counts like card heat in `zoneAir` (the case warms). A **top** radiator breathes the hot air of the case (`north.riseC`) and blows its heat out. The radiator's turning fans are in `FanPower.mounts` (they move the areas' air) but not in `caseFans`. Snapshot: `board.loop` = `{ coolantC, rejectW, side }`, null with no loop. The VRM and RAM heatsink fans blow along their fins toward the rear exhaust (the lens draws that stream).

## Wiring (`apply.ts`)

- `machineConfigFor(board, seed?)`: `MachineConfig` with total DIMM bytes, initial cpu/display rates, display latency (RAM size is fixed at Machine construction).
- `applyRates(board, machine, thermal?)`: `clock.setRate` for the `cpu` and `display` domains (and `ram` if such a domain exists), using throttled effective clocks; rate 0 for missing or burnt parts. Call after each `th.step`. Clock to rate: `{mul: round(clock*100), div: 100}`.
- `engineConfigFor(board)` / `applyEngine(board, machine, program)`: engine from the CPU spec, `missPenalty = ceil(ram.latency * cpuClock / ramClock)`; with several working RAM sticks the **slowest** one (highest penalty) counts. The game calls `applyEngine` at every power-on with the boot ROM as the program (caches go cold).

## Cables and the boot check (`cables.ts`)

Cables are board connectors that are either plugged or not: `CableId` = `atx` (24-pin, from the PSU), `cpu` (8-pin, from the PSU), `video` (from the monitor's display output). `Cables` is `Record<CableId, boolean>`, owned by the Game (not part of `Board`/`BoardJson`; saved next to it). `connectCable(board, cables, id)` needs the source part installed (a PSU, or the monitor) and says why otherwise; `disconnectCable(cables, id)`; `pruneCables(board, cables)` unplugs cables whose source part is gone (after an uninstall; a swap keeps them). `bootCheck(board, cables)` returns `{noPower, post}`: `noPower` (no PSU, burnt PSU, ATX cable off: the power button does nothing) and `post` (`{reason, short}` for a missing/burnt CPU, 8-pin cable off, or no working RAM: the machine powers on but POST fails). See [`ASSEMBLY.md`](ASSEMBLY.md).

The thermal model's `step(board, loads, powered = true)` takes a `powered` flag: false means switched off (nothing draws, parts cool, the trip latch is untouched).

Other changes for the assembly phase: `ram0` latency 100 to 200 (RAM stays a real choice now that jobs boot first), `Display` has `connected` (no monitor: writes go nowhere) and `setText` (the PCH's own POST error text).

## The VRM and the board ladder

The VRM is a per-tier component, simulated inside `ThermalModel.step` (state in `th.vrm`, maths in `vrm.ts`): its conversion loss is added to the PSU draw (`th.power.vrmLossW` is included in `totalW`), a hot VRM multiplies the CPU clock by a factor in [0.8, 1], overload or heat makes `th.vrmErrorRoll()` fire (PRNG only while unstable), and sustained trouble sets `board.burnt` (a dead board: `bootCheck.noPower = "board dead: replace the board"`, nothing draws). Full model, numbers and the plain-words explanation: [`VRM.md`](VRM.md). Snapshot: `snapshot.board` (tier, name, burnt, features, slots, vrm, vrmSink) and `snapshot.power.vrmLossW`.

Heatsink slot `vrm-sink`: one per board on every tier (the Starter board too). Cooler coupling: an air cooler adds `vrmAirPerC` of cooling to the VRM, a liquid cooler adds none. Board tiers carry the VRM's strength (phases x amps: 24, 36, 60, 120 A). Tests: `src/features/board/sim/vrm.test.ts`.

## Cases

The PC case is part of the machine (`CASE_TIERS` in `specs.ts`, `Board.caseTier`, `snapshot.caseTier` and `snapshot.board.caseName / caseForm`). A case of tier `t` holds boards up to `maxBoard` = `t`: **Mini tower** (mini-ITX, the Starter board; the campaign starts in it), **Micro tower** (micro-ATX), **Mid tower** (ATX) and **Full tower** (E-ATX). Prices are placeholders ($25, 70, 160, 320).

- **A bigger board needs a bigger case first.** `applyOffer`, `Game.install` and the shop's `blocked` text refuse a board the case cannot hold ("the Mini tower is too small for a Basic board: buy a Micro tower first"). Owning the bigger case in the inventory is enough to buy the board; fitting the board needs the case fitted. A dead board of the same tier needs no bigger case.
- **Offers**: the next case tier (`case1`...), also in the shop with its level lock (a case unlocks with its board: levels 2, 5 and 9). A case item installs onto the case (`freeSlots ["case"]`) only with the machine off (`purchase` and `install` say so); every part stays where it is.
- **Not gated**: the sandbox, and a game whose `economy.caseGating` is false (the sim tests): there the case follows the board (`caseFor(tier)`).
- The primitive `upgradeBoard` does not look at the case (the game does); `upgradeCase` only goes up. `createBoard(tier, caseTier?)` puts a board in the smallest case that holds it. `src/features/board/sim/case.test.ts`.

## Compatibility checks (`compat.ts`, `compat.test.ts`)

`checkCompat(board)` reads a build as it stands (no ticks) and returns `CompatIssue[]`: `{ id, severity: error|warn|tip, part, message, value?, limit?, fix? }`, errors first. `compatProblems` keeps the errors and warnings. It has two users: tests (a rig that overloads its own parts fails with a clear message, not a timeout: see `machine()` in `cpu/sim/multicore/threads4.test.ts`) and, later, the player (warnings and tips in the UI: show `message`, pick the icon by `id`). `install` still refuses what cannot be fitted; these are for what fits but runs badly.

Checks: `vrm-overload` (warn, above 1: droop crashes) and `vrm-burn` (error, 1.6): the CPU's current over the regulator rating with every thread busy (`cpuFullLoad`: 1 + `SMT_LOAD_SHARE` per extra thread; SMT parts draw more than their rated load), with `fix.clock` = `safeCpuClock`; `ram-vrm-overload` / `ram-vrm-burn`; `cpu-cooling` (steady temperature with the cooler alone against the throttle and unstable points); `psu-budget`, `psu-missing`, `psu-headroom` (tip), `pcie-leads`; `loop-no-radiator` and `radiator-front-heat` (tips). The VRM ratio equals the thermal model's at full load (tested). A new check: add it to `checkCompat` with a stable id and a test.
