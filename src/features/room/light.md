## Game-time clock

Every animation that belongs to the machine runs on one **game clock** (`gt` in `board.ts`, milliseconds) that advances by `frame time * timeScale` with `timeScale` = 0 when paused (`speed` 0), 1 at 1x, 2 at 4x, 3 at 16x. `Live.frame`, `DieView.frame` (chip tweens, pulses), the LEDs, fans, the VRM glow and the exterior accent strip, platter, head, bus and cable pulses, the PCH bars, the boot bar, the OS progress cue, the done ring and the power LED blink all take `gt` instead of wall time, and snapshot-time holds (bus fade timers) use `gt` too, so a pause freezes them exactly and a resume continues without a jump. Wall time still drives: camera pan/zoom/flights, hover lift and outline, drag ghost and drop-target pulses, tutorial highlight rings, toasts and the slide-in of a new drive. **Flashes** (the VRM crash flash, the DRAM flash on memory traffic, the error blink of the accent strip) fade on wall time, so a paused frame never holds one on screen (about 0.9 s, or the blink). Every other machine animation still freezes with the game clock.

## The room: window, time of day and light

- **The window and what is outside** (`skyline.ts`, `drawSky` in `room.ts`): the glass is 5,600 wide and runs from just under the key rack's top down to the sill (just above the desk top's height), west of the desk, with a cross bar and two transoms. The sky's gradient, the stars, the sun and the moon on their arcs, the clouds and the aircraft are live (`skyBack`); the skyline's static part (`skyCity`: hills and three rows of towers, the lit windows by the night) is redrawn only when the light has moved on by a step; the flickering windows and the red beacons are live (`skyLive`). Towers grow with the glass's height only up to a quarter (`skylineK`).
- **Time of day.** `hourAt(gt)`: a day is `DAY_S` (1800) game seconds (half an hour at 1x: 75 seconds an hour; it was 480), the first view is `START_HOUR` (18.6, dusk), so it pauses with the game and runs 16 times faster at 16x. `nightAt` / `duskAt` drive the sky colours (`skyAt`: a table of keyframes), the stars, the sun and the moon on their arcs, the skyline's lit windows and a blinking beacon, the room's light (`light.ts`: dim at night, a little lifted by day in the dark theme, warm at dusk and dawn), the window's light, the lamp's strength, and the **digital wall clock** above the case (four seven-segment digits and a colon that blinks each second of game time, the same hour as the sky; `segDigit`). **The window light follows the sun and the moon** (`bodiesAt`, `lightsAt`): each body above the horizon throws a shaft that leaves the glass (`shaftSpan`, between the two rays from the glass's corners) in the direction it shines from: the sun rises on the left (east) and sets on the right, so the light runs east in the morning, straight down at noon and west in the evening, the lower the body the longer and flatter the shaft (`slant`, units west per unit down) and the warmer the sun's colour; the moon's is cold and weaker. It is strongest at the glass and fades out by the desk, with a flat patch on the desk top where it lands. It is part of the light layer (see Light), so it also lights whatever stands where it falls (the lamp, the monitor's bezel, the keyboard, the mug), and a wide soft pool in the sun's or the moon's colour round the glass lights the west of the room (`windowGlow`: warm and long at sunrise, a cold pale pool at night). The shaft only reaches the monitor for the low sun at the ends of the day (the geometry is unchanged). The dark theme is dim at night and a little brighter by day, the light theme dims more at night. `roomDebug.hour` pins the hour (`window.__board.room.debug.hour` in dev, tests).
- **Light** (`light.ts`, `RoomView.light`, `lightLayer` in `board.ts`, above the props and below the mail). **Every light is an `Emitter`**: plain data (`emittersOf(LightIn, LightGeo)`: the window, the lamp, the screen, the clock), a list of soft shapes it takes the dark away with (`lit`) and a list it tints with (`tint`; a tint layer with `erase` takes tint away again: the screen over the lamp's warmth), each a `LightShape` (blob, cone, feathered rect, ellipse, polygon) with a strength and a colour, and an optional `clip` (the lamp stays above the desk's front). `LightView` only draws what it is given, so the lights can be changed without touching the drawing: `setGeo(patch)` takes new places for the items they follow (the keyboard, the screen, the lamp's mouth; `RoomView.setLightGeo`), `place(id, dx, dy)` nudges one light from its item (`RoomView.placeLight`), `addLight` / `removeLight` add a light for a new item, and each of these redraws on the next `set`. The monitor's light is soft and flat to what it falls on: the glass (feathered by 45), a big halo on the wall round the bezel, a pool on the desk top in front and a lift over the keyboard (`LightGeo.keyboard` is read from the keyboard's placer), all in a pale green; it is a third as strong by day. One layer lights **everything outside the case**: the monitor, the keyboard, the mug, the props, the wall, the case's shell. It is three small textures (`LIGHT_RES` 0.025 px per world unit over the baked region, redrawn only when `lightKey` changes: night, dusk, the lamp and the screen quantised to a few dozen steps) and rims. The **shade** is the dark of the room (`ambientOf`: a deep blue at night, 0.5 in the dark theme and 0.4 in the light one, none by day) with the lit places **cut out** (blend `erase`): the lamp's **cone** (a fan of 0.74 radians each side of its axis, 19,000 long, clipped at the desk's front: the desk shades what is under it) and its **pool** round the hood, the **screen** (when the machine is on and a display is installed, `RoomView.setScreen`, eased over about 0.4 s) with a halo round it and a patch over the keyboard, the **clock**, and **the inside of the case** (`CASE_IN`: it has its own light, nothing reaches it). The **glow** (blend `screen`, which never over-exposes, on both themes) is what the lights add: the lamp's warm cone, pool, smear along the desk top and reflection on the monitor's glass; the screen's pale green on the keyboard, the desk and a halo on the wall; the clock's orange round it; the sky's lift by day and the warm of the ends of the day (these two are even washes over the whole room, a live Graphics `washG`, not part of the region's texture: on a tall or wide screen the region's edge would show as a change of tone). The window's light is drawn here too (`beamsAt` in `room.ts`: 13 slices stepping down from the glass to the desk, each keeping `fade` of the strength, plus the desk patch, `Beam` in `light.ts`; the sun and the moon pale in `screen` blend; the shafts are a live Graphics (`setBeams`, every frame the sun moves) so they glide, and they are also cut out of the dark texture where they fall, so moonlight shows against the night, with the slant quantised to 1/200 and the strength to 1/200 so the cut-out's edge moves in steps of about 50 units). All of it is cut at the inner rect of the case. Texture edges: the dark is also painted round the region by four plain rectangles of the same colour (a wide or tall screen shows more than the region), and the lamp's pools are capped to the room above the hood so the texture's edge never cuts them. **The focus**: a second dark (`focusAt(view, CASE)`: the share of the view the case covers, 0 at the overview and for a view of the monitor, 1 once the case fills most of it, smooth between) that fades in as an alpha (0.64 at most, drawn once into a texture; only its alpha moves) with the **case, the monitor and the router cut out** with soft edges: zoom in on the machine and the room recedes while the screen and the router stay lit. While the view lies inside the case the room and its light are hidden. **Rims**: `rimArt` draws a thin warm line (brass, a darker bronze in the light theme) along the top and the west side of the lit objects (monitor, case, clock, letterbox, key rack, shelf, socket plate, keyboard, the desk's top edge, the mug's rim, the lamp's hood and arms) above the objects and under the dark, so the night dims them with the rest. **Shadows**: the monitor, the case, the clock, the letterbox, the key rack and the shelf throw soft shadows east and down on the wall (sixteen nested rects each, so no ring shows, baked in the wall art under them: the lamp and the window are west and above). The wall is a little warmer and bluer-violet in the dark theme, and in the light theme cooler (toward the board's mint). Measured in headless Chrome with software GL, 1360x800, a powered machine: the light layer costs about 17 ms at the overview and about 33 ms with the focus on (its three full-screen layers); the room's whole frame is 83 ms at the overview (133 before the light was rebuilt, the old wash and cone being gone). Real GPU not measured.
- **The 3D room** (the default; `?2d` hides it) has its own light: see "3D room light" below.
- **Cost.** The lamp's light used to be 30 big circles, 85 percent of the room's frame time in headless software GL; it is now two textures that are redrawn only when the light changes (see Light). The room's live parts are updated in two rates: **every frame** for what must move smoothly and is cheap (the steam's sprites, the window's shaft, which is a live Graphics `LightView.setBeams` redrawn when the sun has moved by a hair, and the lamp, screen and blind easing), and **about 30 times a second** (`gt / 33` in `RoomView.frame`'s key) for the rest (the sky's stars, clouds, aircraft and city, the clock, the light textures when `lightKey` changes); nothing at all while paused with nothing moving. It was 8 times a second for everything (`gt / 120`), which made the steam and the shaft jump; in software GL the two rates cost the same, so the sky's rate was raised too.

### The owner's call

The light is realistic. No studio map and no work light make the machine read. If the machine is unreadable at night, the room gets more real light: RGB and neon accents, mood light, a few warm practicals. This adds to the earlier calls: uniformity above all (one palette, one level of detail, one light), offline bakes over live tricks, performance second.

**Aligned 2026-11-12 with [`docs/art-style.md`](../../../docs/art-style.md) v3 (the approved direction).** The T149 call (2026-11-05) said "warm and cozy, almost like an Instagram showroom" and "many lamps". That is superseded: the room is a dark neon-led room (§1 "not a cozy warm desk scene"). Warm light is one or two practicals and pools, not the fill (§3, §10 item 1). Neon fixtures (strips, bias light, LED halo) define the room's night light. Kept from the call: realistic light only, no fill or fake work light, RGB accents, and the case lit by the room's light from outside, never from inside. Cutting the warm lamp count is part of rework item 1 (on hold until Khalil's go).

### The eye

The owner (2026-11-06): "the room needs to feel less dark", and the board must read zoomed into the open case, by a cozy overhead light that is "not super bright". They chose an eye that adapts. So the exposure moves, as a camera's does; the lamps do not (`board3d.ts`, `Post.setEye`):

- **The room's views**: a set exposure. `BASE_EXPOSURE` is 2.2 (it was 1.1), and by night it rises by `NIGHT_EYE` (1.4): the room has only its lamps.
- **From the board view in**: metered (`Post.meter`). A 16 x 9 grid of the scene's log luminance before the exposure, weighted to the middle, read back every fourth frame. The exposure shows the mean at `EYE_KEY`, within `EYE_RANGE` of the base, and eases there over about half a second. The two blend in log over the zoom (`roomness`).
- Measured (the meter's mean, then the eye's factor): the open case at 23:00 (`--look ...,8000`) .0016, 11.3; at 14:00 .0077, 2.4; the CPU die at 23:00 .018, 1.0 (the art as it was tuned); the board view at 23:00 .0020, 9.0. The board's middle (rect 900,480,120,120) then reads L .063 at 23:00 and .043 at 14:00; the die .055.
- **The local eye** (T180, `localeye.ts`, a pass of `post.ts` before the tone mapping). The bake gave the room its real light, and the room still read black: its paint is near black (walls .05, furniture .01 to .03), so its bounce is about a thousandth of the sky. A brighter wall at 14:00 by light alone needed about 160 times more. The owner (2026-11-06) kept the dark palette and the real light, and chose an eye that adapts locally, as a person's eye does in a dark room by a bright window, or a phone's HDR photo: the dark parts are lifted and the bright ones held back, each part's own detail kept. A bilateral grid (cells of 32 pixels, a bin a stop) gives each pixel its base, the broad light round it among the pixels of about its brightness, so no halo forms at an edge. The base's distance from the key keeps `SHADOW_C` (.18) of it below and `HIGH_C` (.4) above, at most 9 stops up and 3 down. The key is middle grey by day (`MID`) and `NIGHT_KEY` (.03) at night (`board3d.ts`): at night the room stays low-key, pools of lamp light with the dark between them. Above the key the eye keeps `HIGH_C` (.4) by day, so the bright glass is held back, and `NIGHT_HIGH` (.8) at night (T181, `LocalEye.high`), so the pools stand out of the dark: with .4 the sconce's scallop read L .030 and the city outshone the room. The local eye fades out toward the board view with `roomness`, as the grade does: from the board view in, the metered eye alone adapts and the board keeps its tuned look.

### Units and one white balance

Built in T172:

- **Real falloff.** Every lamp has decay 2 (inverse square) in metres (`M`, 18,700 units a metre, `outside/plan.ts`). Its level comes from its lumens over its cone (`lumens(lm, cone)` in `room3d.ts`), and one lux on a face is `LUX_K` of the scene's light: the camera's exposure for the room. The desk lamp is 500 lm, the bedside lantern 800 lm (a 60 W-equivalent bulb). The screen is an area light on its face at 120 nits (`nits(cd)`), so it lights only what is in front of it. A lamp is as bright by day as at night: only the eye adapts (see "The eye"). An offline bake uses inverse square, so the live lamps now match it. Two lights keep their own falloff: the sun (it is at infinity) and the sky's light `key` (it stands for the whole window, a wide source, on what moves; the bake holds the window's light on the still meshes). The area lights (the LED strips) have no decay: their light falls with the solid angle they fill, so it has no units.
- **One white point**, 4500 K (`WHITE_K`), for every lamp. `kelvin(k)` in `palette.ts` gives a black body's colour against it: 2700 K is a clear amber (the lantern, `LANTERN`), 3000 K a soft warm white (the desk lamp), the noon sky (about 6500 K) a pale blue. Every new lamp takes its colour from `kelvin`. The sky's light (`key`) keeps the sky's colour, paler: that is about the colour a 9000 K sky shows against 4500 K.
- **One glass.** The glass has one neutral tint at every hour (`GLASS_T`, 0.47, in `outside/view.ts`). The view, the key and the sun take it at every hour; `TINT`, `setTint(day)` and `TINT_CUT` are gone. The night city is now as dim through it as the day's, and the room's mirror in the glass (`mirror.ts`, T159) shows more as the outside gets darker than the room.

### The lamp cap

The 4K room is CPU-bound: about 2,000 draws each upload the uniforms of every light in the scene, also a light at 0. The graphics options `lights` (4, 8, 0 = all) and `lightShadows` cut that (`Room3D.setLights`, the pure choice in `roomlights.ts`).

- **The cap counts all the room's lights**, the window's `key` and `sun` included (20 in all; the machine's own lights are not in it). `LIGHT_ORDER` gives the order and the reason for each place. Cap 4: the window, the arc lamp, the desk lamp. Cap 8: also the two lanterns and the sconce's two spots.
- **The choice depends on the cap alone**, not on the hour or a switch. A lamp that is off keeps its place, so the count of lights (and each lit program) holds through the evening. Programs link again only when the option changes.
- **A capped lamp** leaves the scene (`visible = false`). Its fixture still glows, the frame still sets its level, and its baked bounce shows `CAPPED_BOUNCE` (2.5) times over, for the pool it lost. A group with a share of its lights capped takes that share of the lift.
- **`lightShadows` off**: the lamps cast no shadow and their maps are freed. The window's shadows follow `shadows` alone.
- **The bedside lantern casts also with `lightShadows` off** (`ALWAYS_CASTS`), while it is live: with no shadow its light passes the drawer's top onto the wall, the floor and the bed's side under it (pool +5.4%). It takes the smallest map (level 1: a 256 cube), which holds until a caster moves ("When a shadow map is drawn"). A cone that aims up was tried: it darkens the drawer's top.
- Its cost: no map in a still room; six faces of 256 on a frame where a caster in the lantern's range moves (`ShadowCache`: a caster outside a light's range does not redraw its map; the lantern has no `distance`, so its range is its shadow camera's `far`). With `lightShadows` off that `far` is `BEDSIDE_FAR`, 21000, and the map holds only the drawer and the bed, with what stands on them (`BEDSIDE_LAYER`): only they redraw it, nothing else in the room does. They do not move, so in play the map is drawn once. (The drawer alone stopped the leak, but lost the small shadows High shows there: the lantern's stand, the book, the mattress's edge.) It shows the drawer's whole shadow on the wall behind it, the floor and the bed's side under it; past `far` the light is not shadowed, as with no map. With `lightShadows` on: default layers, `far` 130000 again, as before. Also: one point shadow's uniforms and lookups in the lit programs. The other lamps cast no shadow: the desk lamp passes the desk top (about 5% of its pool on the floor under it).
- Measured at cap 4 (Low) against main Low (Reviewer, RTX 5080, 4K, 23:00): shelf strips -56%, desk LEDs -25%, bedside pool -19%, floor pool -7%. `CAPPED_BOUNCE` (2.5) stays a first value: tune it per group later.
- The bake (`exportRoom`) ignores the cap: it bakes every lamp at full power, whatever the tier.
- Tiers: Low caps at 4 lamps with no lamp shadows; Medium and High keep all lamps (Medium with lamp shadows off). The capped-lamp lift is a known limit: shelf strips lose much of their light at cap 4.

### RGB accents

### Offline bakes

The owner prefers offline bakes. The bake gives the bounce (the indirect light); the direct light stays live, with its shadows, so the chair, the keys and the cables still cast and catch light.

- **Light groups.** Light is linear: a bake of each group at unit power, scaled by that group's live power, sums to the bake of them all. The groups: the sky through the glass (one bake, tinted by the hour's sky colour), the sun (a key frame for each hour it comes in: the glass looks north, so only near the day's two ends, about six frames), the city's night glow (the lower part of the glass as an emitter), each lamp, each strip. About 15 bakes.
- **The tool** (`tools/bake/`): export the room's static meshes (glTF, with a stable name and a geometry hash per mesh) from headless Chrome; make the lightmap UVs; bake each group's indirect light in Blender's Cycles (`blender -b -P bake.py`); write HDR lightmaps to `public/bake/room/`. A geometry hash that does not match at run time (the room's code changed, the bake is old) falls back to the probe for that mesh and warns in dev. Blender on the owner's machine is a new dependency: the owner approves it.
- **At run time** a `LightmapMix` (the pattern of the removed `EnvMix`: `git show b6430ea:src/core/render/three/envmix.ts`) sums the groups by their live powers into one lightmap, only when `lightKey` changes. A material patch (like `bakedao.ts`) adds it to the static meshes' indirect diffuse. The probe stays for the reflections and for everything that moves.
- **What replaces `fill` and `hemi`:** the baked bounce on the static meshes, and the room's probe (already there) for what moves. Both lights go when the bake is in. Later, if the moving things need it: a grid of baked probes (spherical harmonics per cell) from the same groups.

Done (T179): `npm run bake` (`tools/bake/bake.mjs`; Blender 5.2 from Steam, or `BLENDER`). It takes about 13 minutes on the RTX 5080.

- **Export** (`scene.ts`, run in the dev game's page): it reads the room as the game built it, after `mergeStatic`. A **receiver** gets a lightmap. It is a plain, shown, opaque mesh with a lit material that does not glow, 0.02 m² or more, and no vertex AO (the props and the plants keep `_ao`). Each receiver carries `meshHash`: FNV-1a over its positions in the room's frame, rounded to whole units, and its triangles. The game finds the receiver by this hash, because a merged mesh has no name. Everything else that is opaque only blocks: the instanced books one by one, the small things, the loaded models, the machine. The glass, the glows and the blinds are left out. The lights are read at full power (23:00, the desk lamp and the screen on). The room goes to Blender as JSON and binary arrays, not glTF: Blender needs only the triangles, the UVs, the materials' colours and the lights.
- **Groups** (`GROUPS`): `desk`, `arc`, `floor`, `bedside`, `strips`, `picture`, `sconce` (up and down), `led` (the three shelf lights), `screen` (the screen and the bias light), `sky`, `city`, and `sun-<hour>` for the key frames (`SUN_HOURS`, every 2 h from 6 to 20). The sun comes in by the glass only at 20:00 (`sunIn`): the manifest lists the other frames as `dark`, with no file. The strips are one group, because the game drives them with one value.
- **UVs** (`atlas.ts`): xatlas packs all receivers in one atlas at 50 texels a metre (2 cm), 4 texels of padding: 991 × 1153. The UVs split at the seams, so the game gets a UV for each corner of each triangle, in the triangles' order. An indexed mesh loses its index first.
- **Bake** (`bake.py`): Cycles on OptiX bakes the indirect diffuse light of one group at a time; the window's groups (`sky`, `city`) bake their direct light too (since T180: the window has no live light on the still meshes). All receivers are in one object, so one group is one Cycles session. The settings: 16384 samples, no adaptive sampling, 8 diffuse bounces, no clamp, no caustics. OIDN denoises the result, and two guides keep the charts apart: each chart's colour and the normals. At 1024 samples the dim groups (the desk lamp's) stayed blotchy after the denoiser; about a minute a group buys the clean result.
- **Calibration** (each run, on one white plane): Blender's lights are set so that the bake gives the game's irradiance, the value its lightmap shader adds. A point light or a spot gets the game's intensity over M² (per metre at decay 2). An area light gets its radiance times its area. The sky and the city give the irradiance for a radiance of 1 over their half of the world (the sky above the horizon, the city below it). The sun gives it for an irradiance of 1. The glass is not in the bake.
- **Output** (`public/bake/room/`): `<group>.png` is RGBM over the square root, 8-bit RGBA, with no colour profile. Decode it as `v = scale · (rgb · a)²`; load it without premultiplied alpha and without colour conversion. A texel no triangle can read (farther than half the padding from every chart: `chartTexels`) is black, as the bake's noise there is a third of the file. `npm run bake -- --pack` writes the files again from the last bake's light, with no Blender. `meshes.bin` holds two Uint16 a corner (0 to 65535 over the atlas, v up: a texture with `flipY`). `manifest.json` holds the atlas size, the groups (`scale`; for a lamp group `lead`, the baked intensity of its first light) and the receivers (`name`, `hash`, `corners`, `offset` into `meshes.bin`).
- **At run time** (T180), a group's weight is: a lamp group, its first light's live intensity over `lead`; `sky` and `city`, their half's radiance times `GLASS_T` and the blinds' opening (by day the city below the horizon is sunlit, `CITY_DAY`); a sun frame, the `sun` light's intensity (it already holds the glass and the blinds), blended between the two frames round the hour.

Done (T180): `LightmapMix` (`src/core/render/three/lightmapmix.ts`) sums the groups by those weights into one half-float lightmap when `lightKey` changes, and `applyLightmap` gives each receiver it finds by hash a copy of its material with the map and a patch: the map is the probe's irradiance (the probe still gives the reflections), and the window's stand-in light `key` (the only light with decay `STAND_IN_DECAY`) is skipped, as the bake holds the window. A mesh whose hash does not match keeps the probe and warns in dev. The copies take the room's reflections with the rest (`RoomEnv.twin`). `fill` and `hemi` are gone. The room still read black (see "The eye"): the local eye followed.
- `--only a,b` bakes again only those groups on the last export (the work dir in the temp folder). `--fast` is 64 samples and no denoiser. `--preview` renders each group's lightmap on the room, from the bed's foot toward the floor lantern's corner (`preview-<group>.png` in the work dir). In `preview-floor.png` the lantern's bounce warms the west wall between the bookcase and the glass, and the bookcase's side.
- **Limits:** thin parts (the door's frame, the drawer fronts, the bookcase's edges) have few texels at 2 cm: they stay a little mottled in a dim group.

## When a shadow map is drawn

`ShadowCache` (`src/core/render/three/shadows.ts`) and `Room3D.redrawShadows` draw a map only when its picture can change:

- **A caster moves** (a door, the chair, a new part; `ShadowCache.scan`): every room light redraws on that frame.
- **A fan's rotor turns** (`userData.spin`): it is no mover. Each `SHADOW_TURN_MS` (400 ms), one light asks for a catch-up (`catchUp`). Its map redraws only if a caster or a rotor changed since its last draw. A still room draws no map.
- **A light at intensity 0** (the lanterns and the room's sun spot by day) draws no map, because no one sees it. Its request waits, and the map is drawn on the first frame the light is lit.
- **The sun moves** with the clock: one map on each step (3 game minutes). The room's sun (`aimSun`) stands still from 20:30 to 6:00 (`sunDir`), so its moon shaft keeps its map through the night.
- **The city's sun at 0** (`outside/sunmap.ts`, from about 20:27 to 6:03) draws no map, and the city is not drawn whole for it. Its step waits, and the map is drawn on its first lit frame, with the sun where it stands then. A jump in the hour or a new map size also asks for a map.

Still menu, phone profile, CPU /4, 300 frames (PERF18, 2026-11-09): at the Low tier, frames with a shadow map went from 45 to 11 (each is the city's sun on a clock step) and shadow calls from 60.8 to 0.7 a frame. `bench:modes` menu at Low: 50.6 to 58.1 fps, 247 to 207 calls.

The same menu with the clock running (PERF18 rest, Low tier): at 23:00, 33 frames with a map (17 the room's sun, 17 the city's) and 34.6 shadow calls a frame went to none. By day (14:30 to 15:15) the city's sun still draws 15 maps of 18 calls, one on each step. Night menu, 10 s: drawn fps did not change (16.5, set by the city's beat), triangles a frame went from 304 k to 190 k, long tasks from 208 to 63 ms.

**Shadows at Low and Off (PERF5, 2026-11-10).** Phone profile, menu by day with the clock running, frame limit lifted, CPU /4, maps counted where they are drawn:
- **Maps a frame.** Low and High draw the same maps: 1 a second, the city's sun on each clock step (Low 16 maps in 15 s, 0.03 a drawn frame; High 12 in 12 s, 0.03). The redraw is set by events in time, not by the level. The old count (Low 0.53, High 0.21) was from before PERF18. `bench:3d`'s `shd` column counts the lights asked for a map, not the maps drawn: a light at 0 keeps its request, so `shd` reads 3.3 at Low and 3.6 at High in a still room.
- **Off against On.** CPU ms per drawn frame (rAF callbacks), 5 runs each: Low 17.5 against Off 15.5 (p50 17.2 against 15.4). The maps cost only 0.05 ms of that. The rest is the lit draws: shadow uniforms and lookups. `bench:3d` in the game at CPU /1: `post.render` 3.8-4.9 ms against 3.1-3.6 ms. So the Low tier has the sun's shadows Off (`tierGraphics`, owner decision); the tier comes from the GPU, not touch. The graphics panel turns them back on.

## What the city costs a frame

- **Detail by distance.** Far from the window, trees (`detailAt` in `outside/trees.ts`) and parked cars lose detail that the eye cannot see. The frame's triangles went from about 9.2 M to 3.6 M.
- **Sweeps.** The buildings (`RINGS` in `outside/towers.ts`) and the trees sit in one mesh per ring of distance, sorted by their bearing from the window; the streets' small things (paint, lamps, tree pits, far kerbs) are one mesh per material, sorted by cell of the ground (`sweptMerge`). When the camera moves, `outside/sweep.ts` cuts each mesh to the run of pieces that the camera sees. The cars out of sight are not placed (`Fleet.park`, `Fleet.update`). On a draw that redraws the sun's shadows, all of it is drawn (never while the sun is at 0). At the touch default the city went from 2.8 M triangles a draw to 0.74 M (PERF16).
- **Cadence.** While the eye holds still (the window's box moves under 1 px), the city is drawn each `EVERY` (3) frames (`view.ts`): about 0.25 M triangles a frame at the Low tier.
- **The traffic.** The traffic moves on only when the city is drawn. Each lane (`outside/traffic.ts`) steps on its own beat (`STEP`, 0.5 s). The beats are spread over the lanes, so a draw steps only some of them. A car is drawn between its lane's last two steps, on the curve the step drove it on (`Traffic.front`). So its place is a function of the clock: it is the same at any frame rate, and a car that comes back into view is where the traffic put it. `Fleet.update` cuts each route to the stretch in the eye's frustum (`reach`: one interval, a test per plane) and places only the cars in it: their soft shadows, their models and, at night, their lamps' pools (a wider stretch, `POOL_R`). It uploads only the instances it wrote. The moving cars cast no shadow in the sun's map, so a shadow draw also places them for the eye only. The parked cars sit in cells of 48 m (`parkCells`): a cell out of the frustum costs one test. Menu, phone profile (412x915, dpr 2.6, CPU /4), share of the main thread (PERF20, 2026-11-09): `cars.ts` went from 5.4 % to 0.6 % (of the busy time, from 11-14 % to 1.4-1.8 %). Under Gallery it was and is 0 (PERF1).
- **The hidden city.** `outside/sight.ts` asks the GPU if the eye sees the window's quad (an occlusion query). While the eye stands still and the window is hidden, the city is not drawn.
- **The room in the glass.** `outside/mirror.ts` draws only what glows, and a plain black in place of each mesh that only hides what is behind it. With a still eye it draws every `MIRROR_EVERY` frames (`view.ts`).
- **The frame limit.** The default is 60 fps, Low too. Only with no focus is it 30 fps (`loopLimit`, `graphics.ts`); time without input lowers nothing.
