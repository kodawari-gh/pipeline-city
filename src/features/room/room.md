# Rendering: the room, props, keys, wall cords

Part of the renderer docs; the hub is [`RENDER.md`](../../../docs/RENDER.md). The room is split in three: this file (what is in it and what answers a click), [`render/room/shell.md`](shell.md) (wall, floor, ceiling, desk, perspective, baking), [`render/room/light.md`](light.md) (window, time of day, light). ## The room

`room.ts` draws the world's setting: a bedroom wall and a compact desk, seen from the front like the rest of the world. It is scenery in `roomLayer`, the first child of the world (behind the interior, the exterior and every prop), rebuilt with the scene (theme and case tier changes).

- **The desk follows the case.** The desk top is `DESK_Y = CASE.y + CASE.h + FOOT` (`layout.ts`; the case feet stand on it), so a taller case sits lower on the same wall; the monitor, the lamp, the keyboard and the mug go with it, the window, the key rack and the digital clock (wall things) do not. **From the west the desk holds: the lamp (the very first thing, 3,600 west of the monitor: **as tall as the monitor and a little more**, a weighted base, a lower arm leaning back to the west, an elbow, an upper arm reaching forward over the desk and a hood pointing down and east whose cone lights the whole desk: `LAMP` in `room.ts`), the 18.5 inch monitor over the **keyboard** (real size against the monitor: 6,200 wide, a box turned a few degrees on the desk top, its front, the end that faces the vanishing point and a top of five rows of keycaps (each a little proud, the space bar in the front row), a pair of lamps at the back (`peripherals.ts`); on a desk mat; drawn on the `over` layer, so it stands in front of the monitor's foot) with the **mouse** (just its basic shape for now (rings of its outline at rising heights, each band lighter; low and slim at the front, the crest three quarters of the way back, flat sides, a dark skirt; no buttons, seam, wheel or cord)) east of it, the **mug** (`mug.ts`, in the gap between the monitor and the case: seen from a little above, so every circle of it (the rim, the coffee, the red band with its skyline of little towers, the base, the coaster) is the same ellipse squashed by `MUG.e` (0.12) and the band and the towers follow the body's curve; a tapered ceramic body, a handle that leaves its right side at two heights, coffee in it; sized so its handle stays west of the cables' trunks). **The steam** (`steam.ts`): 18 soft puffs (an uneven cloud texture made of overlapping soft discs) that rise 2,100 units over a life of 6.4 game seconds, widen and stretch as they go, each on its own phase and sway with a slow drift of wind, at most 0.14 opaque and fading in and out (`puffAt`, a pure function of the game clock, so it freezes with the pause), and the PC, the last thing on the desk: the desk ends 500 east of the case** (`roomGeo().desk`). A side panel at the west end and a **drawer pedestal at the east end** under the PC (three drawers) stand on the floor **in front of** the baseboard (see "The room's perspective" in [render/room/shell.md](shell.md)); the wall under the desk is in its shadow. There is nothing on the wall east of the machine but the clock. The **key rack** (`TRAY` in `layout.ts`, `geo.keyRack`): **two wooden rails of hooks on the wall above the monitor, five grey pegs a rail, seen end-on as dots (a hook's bend did not read at that distance)** (10: the queue cap), left of the router's shelf and with its lowest stick end clear of the monitor's top; it follows the monitor (so the case tier), as a wall thing. Each stick **hangs from its hook on its own lanyard** (a flat two-strand cord in the colour of its workload, ending in a ring on the stick's tail; `trayArt` in `props.ts`, the lanyard is part of the stick's `node`, so it lifts with it): straight down by `LANYARD` (380), upright, its plug at the bottom and its lettering reading down it (the stick is `keyStick` rotated a quarter turn). The lower rail is `ROW_DROP` (700) under the upper and staggered half a hook pitch (`HOOK_PITCH`, 660), so its sticks hang between the upper rail's; keys fill the upper rail first (`trayHook(i)`, `trayKeyCentre(i)`). `room.ts` draws the rails and the hooks; `trayArt` the `KEYS` heading and the state note on the upper rail and the sticks. Dragging a stick off its hook works as before (a flat stick follows the pointer). **Every hanging key sways**: its `swing` container (inside the node, pivoting at the hook) turns by a damped pendulum (`SWAY` in `mail.ts`, a period of about 3 s, lightly damped) plus a slow always-there breath of about 1.7 degrees, each key on its own phase; kicks come from a key landing, a key leaving the rack (its neighbours rock away from it), a key gliding to another hook, and the carry. It runs on the game clock, so it freezes with the pause. **A new key arrives in a letter** (`board.ts` `keysFrame`, the poses in `mail.ts`, `MAIL_S` 3.6 game seconds, shown in slow motion with `window.__board.mailSlow(0.14)` in dev): a key id the rack has not seen (not the keys of a loaded game, not a key put back) starts a letter, at most 3 in flight (the rest just hang there, swinging), one on the stage at a time (`mailStage`: the open wall above the monitor, west of the rack). The envelope (`buildLetter`: its inside in `mailBack` under the keys, its pocket and flap in `mailFront` over them) flies in from the upper west, settles, the flap turns over (and goes behind the key), the key rises out of the pocket with its lanyard uncoiling (`lan.scale.y`), the envelope drifts down and fades, and the key is carried by the end of its lanyard, up and over to its hook (it hangs from the carried end, so the pendulum is driven by the end's acceleration, `carryAx`), is hung and takes the catch's jolt. The key's own node does the flying (it is hidden and not hittable until its letter has done, `rebuildPropHits`), so the key is the real one all along. When a key leaves the rack the ones after it **glide** to the next hook (lifted a little, then rocking). **A finished key goes out in a letter** (`MAIL_OUT_S` 3.8 game seconds, `outLetterPose` / `outKeyPose`, the ghost of the key that left the port is the key in it): when the key in the port had its result (`done` or stage `done`) and leaves, an envelope flies in beside the port with its flap open (`mailOutStage`, the gap west of the port), the key swings out of the port, turns upright and sinks into the pocket, the flap shuts, and the envelope is carried up to the **letterbox** (`OUTBOX` in `layout.ts`, drawn by `room.ts`: a small red wall box with a slot in its top and an `OUT` plate, on the free wall between the router's shelf and the case, above the LAN cable's row, 1,900 west of the case's rear wall, following the monitor like the rack). It turns upright and shrinks to the slot's width (`POST_K`), is held over the slot and slides down into it; from `POSTING_FROM` the two layers of the envelope are masked by a rect ending at the slot's line (in world space, on each of the two layers), so the slot hides it, and the box's lamp lights for a moment (`outLedG`). A key pulled before it is done still just fades out of the port as before. The mug stands on the desk; the room's `over` container (the mug and its steam) is a separate layer above the cable layer (`roomOverLayer` in `board.ts`), so the video cable that runs through the gap passes behind the mug. The router shelf is a real plank on two slim metal braces (seen from the front, each with a lit edge and two screws) under the router spot, above the monitor. Nothing about the machine's own layout moved except the monitor, the tray and the cables to them.
- **The mains.** The PC has an IEC inlet in its rear wall at the PSU's end (`PC_POWER`, `acInlet` in `ports.ts`, drawn by `exterior.ts` and by `rearWall` in `case.ts`) and **the monitor's cord is built in** (no inlet: `MONITOR_POWER` is where it comes out from behind the bezel's bottom edge; its start is hidden because the room's back layer is under the monitor). `powerArt` in `room.ts` draws the PC's C13 plug in its inlet (`acPlug`) and the two cords: the PC's **leaves the plug's tail, turns down once at `PC_CORD_X` and falls straight to its outlet** (the plate is set so that its PC outlet is right under that drop: `POWER_PLATE` from `PC_CORD_X`), the monitor's drops behind the desk and runs along the jog row to its outlet (the cords are in the room's back layer, so the slab covers them, and only the stretch above the desk and the stretch under it show) to a **double wall socket on the wall under the desk** (`geo.socket`, both outlets plugged, face-on plugs). In the 3D room that plate is a two-gang Swiss one (`wallPlate` in `board/render/three/swisssocket3d.ts`, drawn at `ROOM_SOCKET`'s scale: a type 13 recess with its three holes at each outlet), and each cord ends in a type 12 plug (`swissPlug`, built by `Machine3D` at the same scale, upright, earth pin down). All scenery: nothing in the sim knows about it.
- **Clickable props.** `lamp` (switches the bulb and the lamp's light: the pool round the hood, the cone over the desk, the glass's reflection) and `blind` (rolls over the window glass, under the frame). Their ids are external (any zoom), hit last (behind every machine id), and a click does its thing at once with no card (`roomClick`, called first in `Interact.click`); tooltips come from `describeId` (`ROOM_TIPS` in `ui/logic.ts`). The state (`roomState`) is a per-viewer convenience kept in `localStorage` (`pipeline-city.room`) when it is there. Nothing in the room touches the sim.
- **Not in the room:** the sim, the tutorial rings and the hit order of every machine id are unchanged; the router and wall plate art is still `net.ts`.

## The 3D room's draw cost (`room3d.ts`, `src/core/render/three/merge.ts`)

Three steps cut the meshes and the material changes a frame draws. None changes the picture.

- **By material** (`mergeStatic`, at build): still meshes of one material become one mesh. The light bake finds its receivers by these meshes' hashes, so change this step only with a new bake.
- **Equal materials** (`shareMaterials`, PERF22): see "Merges" in [render/three.md](../../../docs/render/three.md).
- **By palette** (`mergePalette`, PERF3), once the bake is in (`mergeByPalette`): still meshes whose materials differ only in colour, roughness and metalness become one mesh per 2 m cell of space (`PALETTE_CELL`, so frustum culling still works). Each vertex holds its mesh's colour (`_pc`) and its roughness and metalness (`_prm`). The palette material (`|pal` on the source's program key) reads them through flat varyings (`palettePatch`) and keeps the source's patches. A set holds one material key, so one light map; each part keeps its `uv1` and `_ao`.
- **Left as they are:** what answers a click (an `id` over it: lamp, blind, tray, router, mail, clock, socket, monitor), what moves, fades or glows, a material a writer owns (`own`), a material outside the room's reflections (`RoomEnv`), the mouse (its stand-in hides when the model loads), transparent, instanced and see-through meshes, and lone meshes.
- **The bake's export** (`tools/bake/scene.ts`) reads each palette mesh's parts (`partsOf`), so a new bake sees the same meshes, colours and hashes as before.
- **Casters:** at Low all six room lights cast, so no mesh stops casting. A caster set that changes (the merge) redraws the shadow maps once (`ShadowCache`).
- **Low only, per parent:** the key rack, the router, the mailbox and the monitor housing merge with `mergeWithin` (the room's `lowDetail()` gate). See [three.md](../../../docs/render/three.md#cheap-materials-low) "Cheap materials (Low)".

## The wall cords

**The cords belong to their part.** The PC's cord exists only while a power supply is in the case (`pcmains` is the supply's), the monitor's only while there is a monitor (`monmains`). Take the part out and its cord goes with it; put it in and the cord is there again, unplugged. Build my PC shows both: the hand pulls the cord off the wall and takes it into the machine before the part leaves, and runs it out to the wall and plugs it when the part is in.

**Dragging**: grab a plug (`cable.pcmains` / `cable.monmains`, external ids: hit at any zoom, never reveal the interior), keep it where it is relative to the pointer, drop it within `SEAT_R` (300) of **its own** outlet to seat it (`connectCable`), anywhere else leaves it loose (a plug pulled out of the wall is `disconnectCable` at the grab, which works while the machine runs and cuts its power if it is the PC's). A drop on the other cord's hole gets a short toast. The socket is the id `socket` (highlight rect for the tutorial's `wall` step; `rectsFor('cable.pcmains')` gives the plug and its outlet). A monitor without its cord is **dark** (`monitorPowered` in the snapshot: no text, no NO SIGNAL, no standby card, the LED nearly out, no screen light in the room).

## The power lens

`powerLens` (`app/hover.ts`; the bolt button in the bottom bar, or the **P** key) shows where the machine's power goes, in 3D, in the machine's own space: the real cables and the board's copper glow, pulses run along them toward the parts, the parts glow by what they draw, and a cable that is out shows a red ring and words (`board/render/three/power/`, `docs/render/power.md`; `Lens3D` hosts it). The two lenses exclude each other. The room's old 2D lens (`PowerLens`, `lensModel`) is gone: its model lives on as `powerLedger` (`power/ledger.ts`): what the wall gives the PC is `power.totalW` less the monitor, the monitor's cord carries the monitor's own draw, and the cables split the rest, so **the flows add up** (tested). The energy the wall has given the machine (`Snapshot.energy.wh`) is metered for the power bill (`src/features/economy/GAME.md` "The power bill"). The lens itself shows no bill; a wall cut for an unpaid bill leaves the machine off, so the lens shows the idle lines.

## The airflow lens

`airflowLens` (`app/hover.ts`, A key or the fan button) shows the case's air as 3D particles in the machine's own space (`board/render/three/air/`, `docs/render/air.md`; `Lens3D` hosts it). It is a thermal view (`docs/render/air.md` "The drawing"): a cold veil darkens the room, the parts glow by their temperature, and the air, the water and the glow share one scale (`core/render/three/thermal.ts`, read off a bar under the `ROOM` tag). The air also goes on outside the case as jets, and room air is drawn in toward the intakes. The two lenses exclude each other. This file keeps the lens's MODEL, `airflowModel` (pure, `airflow.test.ts`): the words and the numbers, not the drawing. The 2D drawing (`AirflowLens`) and the 2D flow field (`airfield.ts`) are gone.

- The sim cools the case's air per AREA (`board/sim/airflow.ts`: north, cards, bay; `BOARD.md` "Airflow zones"), from which case fans turn. The lens still draws the lanes from the fan cables, the temperatures and the PSU load, and names a part whose area is poorly ventilated (`board.air[].flow` under 70%: `AIR 40%`). The main cage's drives stand in the front fans' air: the sim takes the air they block from the board (`BOARD.md` "Airflow zones"), and in the field they are obstacles (zone `storage.cage`); the basement's drives and the M.2 sticks are flat. Card fans are in the sim too (`DeviceSnap.fan`, `BOARD.md` "Card fans").- **The lens knows no part.** Each feature exports an `AirSource` (`src/core/render/airsource.ts`) from its own `render/air.ts`: its heat zones (`heat`: rects, temperature, 0..1 heat) and its own fans (`fans`: an id and a rect). `AIR_SOURCES` (`desk/airsources.ts`) lists them. A new part kind with thermals writes a `render/air.ts` and adds it there; `airflow.test.ts` fails while a kind with thermals has no source (or is not in `OUTSIDE_THE_CASE`).
- **Fan speeds.** Every fan has one motor (`core/render/fanmotor.ts`): it eases up from still and coasts down, never jumps, and a rebuilt scene keeps its speed. A fan may turn only while `machineLive` (on, not tripped, board not burnt) and its own conditions hold (cable in, part not burnt; a graphics card also needs to draw power: `cardSpins`). Speeds are in `fanBank` by id (`case:front0`, `cooler`, `radiator:top1`, `vrm`, `ramvrm`, `psu`, `gpu:<slot>`). The 3D air reads them each frame (`levelOf` in `air/view.ts`), so it ramps and dies away with the real fans. The 3D machine's fans publish them (`Machine3D.frame`: `fanBank.setLevel` by the fan's cable); the cards' fans come from `gpuview.ts`. The sim still has no fan speed: targets come from temperature and load in `live/fans.ts` and `gpuview.ts`.
- Local fans (cooler, cards, sinks, PSU) are the machine's own 3D fans: the 3D air blows through them at their `fanBank` speed (`localFans` in `air/plan.ts`). The PSU is in the 3D flow like the rest (`psuBay`, `casing` in `air/plan.ts`): its fan on the floor draws the room's air up through its unit and out of a rear vent, and the unit is a heat part (`psuTempC`). A passive sink has no fan. The liquid loop is tagged `RAD x W · COOLANT t` at the radiator (`board.loop`), with `WARMS THE CASE` for a front radiator.
- Lanes: front fans take air in, the top and rear fans blow it out. A dead fan (cable out, or the hub's `sysfan` out) keeps its lane in red. A radiator replaces the fans of its side with one lane, strength by the fans plugged. A Mini's rear grille is a weak lane.
- Heat: each part kind is a zone, blue to amber to red (`heatOf`: 30 C to 85 C); the zones give the tags their temperatures and trends. The board's two VRMs are zones too, hot against their own throttle point. The 3D particles take their heat from the parts' own coefficients (`airParts` in `air/plan.ts`), not from these zones.
- Warnings: `NO FAN TURNS`, `NO EXHAUST`, `NO INTAKE`, `FAN OFF`, and `MACHINE OFF` (no particles). `CASE +x C` uses the sim's 0.05 C per card watt.
- **The air is a solved 3D flow** (`board/render/three/air/`, `docs/render/air.md`), not lanes. The machine's part boxes and the case's openings make a voxel grid; each fan's flow is solved once for it; particles ride the sum of the fans' flows at their real speeds, slide round the parts, take heat from the hot ones and leave where air leaves. No exhaust: the air stalls (the `NO EXHAUST` warning). The sim cools by area (`board/sim/airflow.ts`) but has no particles: the 3D air is a picture of the same layout, fed by the same numbers (`airParts`), not read back by the sim.

## USB keys

A key is a **plain dark stick** (`keyStick` in `props.ts`, 960 x 230 world units on the rack, 750 long compact in the port: a neutral shell with a quiet rim, no urgency colour on it) that carries its **job id and type** (`#72 scan`, the font as large as the room by the dial allows) and the **template** (`cli.scan`), or the stage while an OS works on it, or `DONE`. **The workload is the lanyard's colour** (`WORKLOAD_COLOURS` / `lanyardColour`, by the key's `type`: log blue, calc amber, checksum green, scan violet, db orange, web teal, secure red, gpu pink, npu lime, hpc silver). **The time left is a dial** by the collar (`keyTimerSpot`, `drawKeyTimer`): a disc that a hand sweeping clockwise from the top eats, the part still to go filled green, amber once more than 65 percent of the span is gone, red and empty (the hand at the top) when late; a key with its result has none. The dial is redrawn on every snapshot (`PropObj.retime`, `refreshTimers` in `board.ts`: the sim's age moves every tick, the stick is rebuilt only when something else changes) and lives outside the stick's baked texture. Text is ellipsized only when it would overflow the body. The tray holds 2 rows of 5. The plugged key is the **compact** variant (a 190 plug seated in the USB port plus a 560 body sticking out west), so it stays clear of the monitor. Hover lift, click to plug or eject, and drag to the port work on both.

**Remove cue.** A finished key in the port (`stage` `done`) gets a glow around the stick and a tag under it: `TAKE ME` on a no-OS machine, `REMOVE` once an OS is installed. Both use the key's own green accent (`T.st`, not an urgency colour). They breathe on the game clock (one slow sine, about 1.8 s; frozen when paused) and vanish with the key. The tag and the glow scale up with the camera (`drawKeyCue`, up to 2.6x) so they stay readable at the exterior view. They live in the key's node, outside its baked art, so they still animate far out. The tray is a glass panel (gradient body, a top sheen, a sunken well), the same structure in light and dark.
