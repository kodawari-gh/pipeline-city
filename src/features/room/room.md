# Rendering: the room, props, keys, wall cords, power lens

Part of the renderer docs; the hub is [`RENDER.md`](../../../docs/RENDER.md). The room is split in three: this file (what is in it and what answers a click), [`render/room/shell.md`](shell.md) (wall, floor, ceiling, desk, perspective, baking), [`render/room/light.md`](light.md) (window, time of day, light). Code: `src/features/room/render/`.

## Files

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `room.ts` | the bedroom behind the machine: wall, window (the whole height of the wall, no plant) with a sky that follows the game clock, clock, the desk with a drawer pedestal, the key rack (two hook rails) on the wall, the wall socket (the cords are live, `cords.ts`), the things on the desk; the lamp and the blind answer a click; `roomBox()` is the camera cap; see "The room" |
| `props.ts` | world props: part icons (drag ghost), the USB key rack's sticks on their lanyards (`hangingKey`, `trayArt`), wide key stick, power button and its lights, the remove cue on a finished key (`drawKeyCue`), a glass panel for the held rack |
| `mail.ts` | a new key's arrival (the letter that flies in, opens and gives up its key, the key carried to its hook: pure poses and the letter's art) and the sway of the hanging keys (a damped pendulum, `swayStep`, plus a slow breath, `swayAngle`) |
| `cords.ts` | the two cords from the wall (the PC's and the monitor's): `cordPlan` / `cordRoute` / `plugRect` (pure, `cords.test.ts`) and `CordsView` (draws the cords and their plugs, fills `scene.cords`); see "The wall cords" |
| `lens.ts` | the power lens: `lensModel` (pure: what flows where, the watts, what is missing; `lens.test.ts`) and `PowerLens` (dims the room, draws the flows, dots, rings and tags); see "The power lens" |

## The room

`room.ts` draws the world's setting: a bedroom wall and a compact desk, seen from the front like the rest of the world. It is scenery in `roomLayer`, the first child of the world (behind the interior, the exterior and every prop), rebuilt with the scene (theme and case tier changes) and moved each frame by `RoomView.frame(gt, dt)`.

- **The desk follows the case.** The desk top is `DESK_Y = CASE.y + CASE.h + FOOT` (`layout.ts`; the case feet stand on it), so a taller case sits lower on the same wall; the monitor, the lamp, the keyboard and the mug go with it, the window, the key rack and the digital clock (wall things) do not. **From the west the desk holds: the lamp (the very first thing, 3,600 west of the monitor: **as tall as the monitor and a little more**, a weighted base, a lower arm leaning back to the west, an elbow, an upper arm reaching forward over the desk and a hood pointing down and east whose cone lights the whole desk: `LAMP` in `room.ts`), the 24 inch monitor over the **keyboard** (real size against the monitor: 6,200 wide, a box turned a few degrees on the desk top, its front, the end that faces the vanishing point and a top of five rows of keycaps (each a little proud, the space bar in the front row), a pair of lamps at the back (`peripherals.ts`); on a desk mat; drawn on the `over` layer, so it stands in front of the monitor's foot) with the **mouse** (just its basic shape for now (rings of its outline at rising heights, each band lighter; low and slim at the front, the crest three quarters of the way back, flat sides, a dark skirt; no buttons, seam, wheel or cord)) east of it, the **mug** (`mug.ts`, in the gap between the monitor and the case: seen from a little above, so every circle of it (the rim, the coffee, the red band with its skyline of little towers, the base, the coaster) is the same ellipse squashed by `MUG.e` (0.12) and the band and the towers follow the body's curve; a tapered ceramic body, a handle that leaves its right side at two heights, coffee in it; sized so its handle stays west of the cables' trunks). **The steam** (`steam.ts`): 18 soft puffs (an uneven cloud texture made of overlapping soft discs) that rise 2,100 units over a life of 6.4 game seconds, widen and stretch as they go, each on its own phase and sway with a slow drift of wind, at most 0.14 opaque and fading in and out (`puffAt`, a pure function of the game clock, so it freezes with the pause), and the PC, the last thing on the desk: the desk ends 500 east of the case** (`roomGeo().desk`). A side panel at the west end and a **drawer pedestal at the east end** under the PC (three drawers) stand on the floor **in front of** the baseboard (see "The room's perspective" in [render/room/shell.md](shell.md)); the wall under the desk is in its shadow. There is nothing on the wall east of the machine but the clock. The **key rack** (`TRAY` in `layout.ts`, `geo.keyRack`): **two wooden rails of hooks on the wall above the monitor, five grey pegs a rail, seen end-on as dots (a hook's bend did not read at that distance)** (10: the queue cap), left of the router's shelf and with its lowest stick end clear of the monitor's top; it follows the monitor (so the case tier), as a wall thing. Each stick **hangs from its hook on its own lanyard** (a flat two-strand cord in the colour of its workload, ending in a ring on the stick's tail; `trayArt` in `props.ts`, the lanyard is part of the stick's `node`, so it lifts with it): straight down by `LANYARD` (380), upright, its plug at the bottom and its lettering reading down it (the stick is `keyStick` rotated a quarter turn). The lower rail is `ROW_DROP` (700) under the upper and staggered half a hook pitch (`HOOK_PITCH`, 660), so its sticks hang between the upper rail's; keys fill the upper rail first (`trayHook(i)`, `trayKeyCentre(i)`). `room.ts` draws the rails and the hooks; `trayArt` the `KEYS` heading and the state note on the upper rail and the sticks. Dragging a stick off its hook works as before (a flat stick follows the pointer). **Every hanging key sways**: its `swing` container (inside the node, pivoting at the hook) turns by a damped pendulum (`SWAY` in `mail.ts`, a period of about 3 s, lightly damped) plus a slow always-there breath of about 1.7 degrees, each key on its own phase; kicks come from a key landing, a key leaving the rack (its neighbours rock away from it), a key gliding to another hook, and the carry. It runs on the game clock, so it freezes with the pause. **A new key arrives in a letter** (`board.ts` `keysFrame`, the poses in `mail.ts`, `MAIL_S` 3.6 game seconds, shown in slow motion with `window.__board.mailSlow(0.14)` in dev): a key id the rack has not seen (not the keys of a loaded game, not a key put back) starts a letter, at most 3 in flight (the rest just hang there, swinging), one on the stage at a time (`mailStage`: the open wall above the monitor, west of the rack). The envelope (`buildLetter`: its inside in `mailBack` under the keys, its pocket and flap in `mailFront` over them) flies in from the upper west, settles, the flap turns over (and goes behind the key), the key rises out of the pocket with its lanyard uncoiling (`lan.scale.y`), the envelope drifts down and fades, and the key is carried by the end of its lanyard, up and over to its hook (it hangs from the carried end, so the pendulum is driven by the end's acceleration, `carryAx`), is hung and takes the catch's jolt. The key's own node does the flying (it is hidden and not hittable until its letter has done, `rebuildPropHits`), so the key is the real one all along. When a key leaves the rack the ones after it **glide** to the next hook (lifted a little, then rocking). **A finished key goes out in a letter** (`MAIL_OUT_S` 3.8 game seconds, `outLetterPose` / `outKeyPose`, the ghost of the key that left the port is the key in it): when the key in the port had its result (`done` or stage `done`) and leaves, an envelope flies in beside the port with its flap open (`mailOutStage`, the gap west of the port), the key swings out of the port, turns upright and sinks into the pocket, the flap shuts, and the envelope is carried up to the **letterbox** (`OUTBOX` in `layout.ts`, drawn by `room.ts`: a small red wall box with a slot in its top and an `OUT` plate, on the free wall between the router's shelf and the case, above the LAN cable's row, 1,900 west of the case's rear wall, following the monitor like the rack). It turns upright and shrinks to the slot's width (`POST_K`), is held over the slot and slides down into it; from `POSTING_FROM` the two layers of the envelope are masked by a rect ending at the slot's line (in world space, on each of the two layers), so the slot hides it, and the box's lamp lights for a moment (`outLedG`). A key pulled before it is done still just fades out of the port as before. The mug stands on the desk; the room's `over` container (the mug and its steam) is a separate layer above the cable layer (`roomOverLayer` in `board.ts`), so the video cable that runs through the gap passes behind the mug. The router shelf is a real plank on two slim metal braces (seen from the front, each with a lit edge and two screws) under the router spot, above the monitor. Nothing about the machine's own layout moved except the monitor, the tray and the cables to them.
- **The mains.** The PC has an IEC inlet in its rear wall at the PSU's end (`PC_POWER`, `acInlet` in `ports.ts`, drawn by `exterior.ts` and by `rearWall` in `case.ts`) and **the monitor's cord is built in** (no inlet: `MONITOR_POWER` is where it comes out from behind the bezel's bottom edge; its start is hidden because the room's back layer is under the monitor). `powerArt` in `room.ts` draws the PC's C13 plug in its inlet (`acPlug`) and the two cords: the PC's **leaves the plug's tail, turns down once at `PC_CORD_X` and falls straight to its outlet** (the plate is set so that its PC outlet is right under that drop: `POWER_PLATE` from `PC_CORD_X`), the monitor's drops behind the desk and runs along the jog row to its outlet (the cords are in the room's back layer, so the slab covers them, and only the stretch above the desk and the stretch under it show) to a **double wall socket on the wall under the desk** (`geo.socket`, both outlets plugged, face-on plugs). All scenery: nothing in the sim knows about it.
- **Clickable props.** `lamp` (switches the bulb and the lamp's light: the pool round the hood, the cone over the desk, the glass's reflection) and `blind` (rolls over the window glass, under the frame). Their ids are external (any zoom), hit last (behind every machine id), and a click does its thing at once with no card (`roomClick`, called first in `Interact.click`); tooltips come from `describeId` (`ROOM_TIPS` in `ui/logic.ts`). The state (`roomState`) is a per-viewer convenience kept in `localStorage` (`pipeline-city.room`) when it is there. Nothing in the room touches the sim.
- **Not in the room:** the sim, the tutorial rings and the hit order of every machine id are unchanged; the router and wall plate art is still `net.ts`.

## The wall cords

The PC and the monitor each have a power cord that must be plugged into the wall socket under the desk (the sim side: `src/features/cpu/ASSEMBLY.md`, cables `pcmains` and `monmains`). `cords.ts` draws them live (they used to be baked into the room): the **cord's body** runs behind the desk like the ISP lead (`CordsView.behind`, put into `RoomView.behind` by `board.ts`), the **plug** is drawn above the room (`CordsView.root`, at every zoom). A plugged cord seats its plug in its outlet (`geo.socket.pc` / `.mon`); a loose one **lies below the socket** (`rest`, 1,050 under the plate) with its cord hanging to it. The baked room keeps the plate with empty outlets (pins and earth) and the PC's plug in the case's inlet. `board.ts` `syncCords` uses the same optimistic scheme as the other cables (`interact.cableStateOf`), and the cord in the hand is drawn by the view itself (`Interact.onCordHeld`).

**Dragging**: grab a plug (`cable.pcmains` / `cable.monmains`, external ids: hit at any zoom, never reveal the interior), keep it where it is relative to the pointer, drop it within `SEAT_R` (300) of **its own** outlet to seat it (`connectCable`), anywhere else leaves it loose (a plug pulled out of the wall is `disconnectCable` at the grab, which works while the machine runs and cuts its power if it is the PC's). A drop on the other cord's hole gets a short toast. The socket is the id `socket` (highlight rect for the tutorial's `wall` step; `rectsFor('cable.pcmains')` gives the plug and its outlet). A monitor without its cord is **dark** (`monitorPowered` in the snapshot: no text, no NO SIGNAL, no standby card, the LED nearly out, no screen light in the room).

## The power lens

A switch (the bolt button in the bottom bar, or the **P** key; `powerLens` in `src/app/hover.ts`) that dims the room (`PowerLens.dim`, 0.76 dark / 0.6 light) and lights the electrical map over it, above everything but the interaction layer. `lensModel` (pure) turns the snapshot into **flows** (source to sink: each cord, the PSU's `atx` and `cpu` cables, the SATA lead with a branch to every drive, the fan hub's lead, and one run per part kind from the board's main connector, the CPU's from its own cable), their **watts**, **tags** (`WALL 54 W`, `LOAD 54 W / 250 W`, `MONITOR 1.2 W`, one per part kind) and **marks** (rings at the plugs and at a cable connector that is open). What the wall gives the PC is `power.totalW` less the monitor, the monitor's cord carries the monitor's own draw (`devices[].powerW`), and the cables split the rest, so **the flows add up** (tested). A line is thicker and its dots faster with more watts (`flowWidth`, `flowSpeed`); the dots run on game time, so they stop with the pause. **Amber is power flowing**, a faint amber a linked run with nothing to carry (machine off), and **red, pulsing** a run that needs linking and is not (a cord out, the 24-pin or the 8-pin out) with a warning beside it (`PLUG THE PC IN`, `NO MAIN POWER`). Words are a constant 13 px on a dark plate whatever the zoom. The model is rebuilt only when the snapshot or a plug changes. The energy the wall has given the machine (`Snapshot.energy.wh`) is metered for the power bill (`src/features/economy/GAME.md` "The power bill"). The lens itself shows no bill; a wall cut for an unpaid bill leaves the machine off, so the lens shows the idle map. The lens is not measured on a real GPU.

## USB keys

A key is a **plain dark stick** (`keyStick` in `props.ts`, 960 x 230 world units on the rack, 750 long compact in the port: a neutral shell with a quiet rim, no urgency colour on it) that carries its **job id and type** (`#72 scan`, the font as large as the room by the dial allows) and the **template** (`cli.scan`), or the stage while an OS works on it, or `DONE`. **The workload is the lanyard's colour** (`WORKLOAD_COLOURS` / `lanyardColour`, by the key's `type`: log blue, calc amber, checksum green, scan violet, db orange, web teal, secure red, gpu pink, npu lime, hpc silver). **The time left is a dial** by the collar (`keyTimerSpot`, `drawKeyTimer`): a disc that a hand sweeping clockwise from the top eats, the part still to go filled green, amber once more than 65 percent of the span is gone, red and empty (the hand at the top) when late; a key with its result has none. The dial is redrawn on every snapshot (`PropObj.retime`, `refreshTimers` in `board.ts`: the sim's age moves every tick, the stick is rebuilt only when something else changes) and lives outside the stick's baked texture. Text is ellipsised only when it would overflow the body. The tray holds 2 rows of 5. The plugged key is the **compact** variant (a 190 plug seated in the USB port plus a 560 body sticking out west), so it stays clear of the monitor. Hover lift, click to plug or eject, and drag to the port work on both.

**Remove cue.** A finished key in the port (`stage` `done`) gets a glow around the stick and a tag under it: `TAKE ME` on a no-OS machine, `REMOVE` once an OS is installed. Both use the key's own green accent (`T.st`, not an urgency colour). They breathe on the game clock (one slow sine, about 1.8 s; frozen when paused) and vanish with the key. The tag and the glow scale up with the camera (`drawKeyCue`, up to 2.6x) so they stay readable at the exterior view. They live in the key's node, outside its baked art, so they still animate far out. The tray is a glass panel (`glassPanel`: gradient body, a top sheen, a sunken well), the same structure in light and dark.
