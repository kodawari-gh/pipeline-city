# Rendering: the room's shell, desk and perspective

Part of the renderer docs; the hub is [`RENDER.md`](../../RENDER.md); see also [`render/room/room.md`](room.md) and [`render/room/light.md`](light.md). Code: `src/render/room/` (`persp.ts`, `desk.ts`, `wall.ts`, `shell.ts`, `decor.ts`, `peripherals.ts`).

## Files

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `persp.ts` | the room's one perspective: the vanishing point, `spreadX/spreadY` (a wall-plane point brought forward to a scale), `floorScale`, `deskGeo` (the desk top's far and near edges, the slab, where the legs meet the floor); see "The room's perspective" |
| `peripherals.ts` | the mat, keyboard and mouse on the desk top, in plan, turned a few degrees, in the room perspective |
| `desk.ts` | the desk in that perspective: top, slab, side panel, drawer pedestal, the shadows under it |
| `wall.ts` | the **slat wall** over the whole wall (see "The room"): `slatWall`, `drawSlats` (in perspective), `drawWallShade`, `slatTone` |

## The room: wall, floor, ceiling, desk and perspective

- **Camera cap.** `roomBox()` is the box the zoom-out stops at: the desk and the window with 900 of wall beyond, from 500 above the key rack (or the router shelf or the window) to 700 under the wall socket (the dock covers the bottom edge): no ceiling and no floor (it follows the case, `camera.bounds` is set with the scene): `Camera.bounds` keeps the view inside it (a view bigger than the box is centred) and `capZoom` in `board.ts` sets the lowest zoom: the zoom where the box fills the free area, but never above `LOD.fadeTo` (0.8) of the overview, so the far exterior case view is always reachable (on a wide or tall screen the wall and the desk run on past the box). That is a short zoom-out (about 1.25x the overview): the exterior case crossfades in over `LOD.fadeFrom` (0.92) to `fadeTo` (0.8). The overview margin is `ROOM_MARGIN` (0.84), which leaves some wall and desk around the machine. `Camera.clampView(v)` gives the settled view of a target (the overview fit uses it, so `atOverview` stays true near the box).
- **The wall** is a **slat wall from the ceiling to the floor** (`wall.ts`, drawn into the baked wall art under everything that hangs on it): thin vertical slats (`SLAT`: 230 wide with 70 between) on a dark felt backing, from 60,000 west to 60,000 east of the origin, in three tones (walnut in the dark theme, oak in the light one, close to each other so the wall stays quiet behind the machine; a fixed pseudo-random pick, `slatTone`), each with a lit and a shaded edge. The lamp rakes across the slats and the light layer's shadows and rims sit on it.
- **Decor** (`decor.ts`): a **plant** on the window sill (`drawPlant`: a terracotta pot and seven blades) and a **rug** on the floor under the desk (`drawRug`: its sides run to the vanishing point, with a border, stripes and a fringe), both in the room's front art, so the light layer lights them. The wall just above the desk's far edge is a little in shadow (24 fine bands that ease out upward, a warm dark in the light theme: the wall's own pale blue over the tan slats had read as a haze, and a single dark band had read as a stripe). The light theme's felt between the slats is lighter, so the wall is quieter. **The overview** frames `WORLD` down to the foot of the wall plate under the desk (`overviewRect` in `board.ts`: the plate shows the internet plan, and the HUD's bottom pills would cover it at the screen's edge).
- **The room's shell** (`shell.ts`): a tall screen (a phone in portrait shows about four times the room's height) must show a room, not a void, and no edge of the picture. `RoomGeo.ceil` is where the wall meets the ceiling (2,700 units above the highest thing on the wall); the **ceiling** above it (`drawCeiling`) goes from its colour next to the wall to a far colour in 3,600 units (`FADE.ceil`) and then stays flat for ever; the **crown moulding** (`drawCrown`, 520 tall) and the **baseboard** (`drawFloor`, 440 tall) trim the wall; the **floor** (`drawFloor`) below `FLOOR_Y` is boards of equal depth in the room, so in the perspective they widen away from the wall (`BOARD`: each seam `step` of the way to the viewer, `boardTop`; two tones, a seam between them and butt joints that run to the vanishing point) darkening over 5,400 units (`FADE.floor`) to a flat far colour that runs on for ever; the **ceiling** has boards in the same perspective (faint, fading with it); the desk's side panel and pedestal stand on the floor with a soft shadow. The palettes are in `shellLook` (the dark theme's floor is a dark brown fading to near black, the light theme's a warm oak fading to a cool grey). The camera box (`roomBox`) ends 2,200 units under the floor line (the desk's legs stand in front of the baseboard) (a strip of floor and the baseboard are part of the picture), and the baked region (`roomBakeRect`) runs from the ceiling's fade to the floor's, so a tall phone's view still fits the bake. The flat far colours are why no edge shows on any screen.
- **The room's perspective** (`persp.ts`, `desk.ts`; found by a review of why the scene looked flat and wrong): the wall is a plane facing the viewer (scale 1, like the machine on it) and everything that comes out of it toward the viewer is drawn in one one-point perspective, so their edges meet at one vanishing point (`roomPersp`: x the middle of the desk, y `EYE` (0.58) of the way up the wall, so the eye is above the desk top and the top shows). A wall-plane point brought forward to a scale `s` lands at `vanishing point + s * (point - vanishing point)` (`spreadX`, `spreadY`); the floor plane's scale at a screen y is `floorScale`. **The desk** (`deskGeo`, `drawDesk`): the things on it stand at the scale `S_FEET` (1.08, the middle of the top); the top runs from its far edge against the wall to its near edge at `S_FRONT` (1.16), a trapezoid whose sides run to the vanishing point, with a grain, a crease against the wall and a lit front edge (868 units deep, the feet line `ys` in the middle of it); the slab's front face (`SLAB_FRONT`, 440) is under the near edge; the **side panel** at the west end and the **drawer pedestal** at the east end stand on the floor at the near plane, `floorFront` below the baseboard's line, and show their inner face, receding to the wall (the west panel's east face is shaded, the pedestal's west face is lit); the desk's shadow is on the floor (`drawDeskShadow`) and on the wall under it (`drawUnderDesk`); the lamp's light is clipped at the top's near edge (`LightGeo.deskTop`) and its smear lies over the top's depth. **The floor, the ceiling and the rug**: boards are equal steps of depth, so the seam `k` is at the floor scale `1 / (1 - k step)`, and their butt joints and the rug's sides are lines to the vanishing point (they were vertical and slanted at 45 degrees before). **The wall**: a slat shows the side that faces the vanishing point, `SLAT_DEPTH` (0.0045) of its distance from it (so the gaps close toward the ends of a wide screen; the near side is shaded and the far one lit), and `drawWallShade` darkens the wall toward its ends and the ceiling. **The window** is a recess: the glass is set back (scale 0.97), so the west side of the opening, the head from below and the sill's inner top show, and the sill is a board seen from above (a trapezoid to the vanishing point, then its front edge). Things on the desk stay front elevations with their own small tilt (the keyboard, the mug and the plant show their tops by a little): the machine, the monitor and the things hanging on the wall are not put in perspective.
- **On the desk** (`peripherals.ts`): the mat, the keyboard and the mouse are placed on the desk top in plan (`placer`: a centre, a yaw, plan sizes; a plan point is turned, put on the top in the room's perspective and lifted by its height; plan depth is squashed to the screen by the mug's `MUG.e`). The keyboard is turned 7 degrees (its right end toward the viewer) and the mouse -35 (turned enough that the camera sees a little of its side and the wheel); the mouse has no cord; the keyboard shows its end that faces the vanishing point; the mouse is built from cross-sections along its length from the owner's three drawings (the plan from a top-down photo: `widthAt`; the top line from a side drawing: `heightAt`, a crest of 575 on 900 wide, the wireframe's proportions; the dome section from a back photo: a superellipse of exponent 2.2 above a short inset dark skirt), lit tile by tile from each tile's normal, with a scroll wheel (a cylinder whose axis runs across the mouse, square to its centre line, radius 144 and 86 wide, sunk in a slot of the shell: its tread and ends are cut along the shell's curve). The lamp's base is a disc seen from above (rim and top are ellipses squashed by `MUG.e`).
- **Baked room.** The wall and the desk (the two big static Graphics, 180000 wide) each sit in a wrapper container with a fixed `boundsArea` (`roomBakeRect`: the camera box plus 2,500 each side and 4,000 above and below), because a Graphics ignores `boundsArea` and would size the texture from its geometry. While the camera is far out (`roomBakeFits`: the view inside that region, and `zoom x devicePixelRatio` at most 1.2 times `ROOM_BAKE_RES`, 0.09) `RoomView.setView` calls `cacheAsTexture` on both wrappers (two textures of about 3,500 x 1,900 px, 27 MB each as power-of-two GPU memory about 33 MB); close in, on a 2x display at overview zoom, or on a screen so wide or tall that the view leaves the region, they are drawn as shapes again (so there is never an edge). While the view lies inside the case (`roomHidden`, the case shrunk by 200) the whole room is hidden and its live parts are not redrawn. `board.ts` calls it from `syncRoom` in every camera change. The sky, blind, shafts, bulb, clock and the cables behind the desk stay live (and the light layer, a separate sprite set above everything). Headless Chrome with software GL, 1360x800, a powered machine, median frame: overview 167 to 133 ms (the room hidden altogether is 50), case view 133 to 83, CPU close-up 150 to 117 (room hidden), room wide 134 to 117. Real GPU not measured. What was left of the room's cost in software GL was the live layers (a full-screen wash, the window's shafts and the sky under its mask, the lamp's cone); the wash and the cone are now the light layer, see [render/room/light.md](light.md).
