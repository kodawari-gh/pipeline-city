## The case, the bay and the OS screen (storage and OS phase)

- **Case** (`CASE`, `case.ts`): a thin, low-contrast chassis around the motherboard and the PSU. The **rear wall** is a slab (`WALL_T`) whose outer face is the I/O shield plate's west edge (`IO_X`): the board's connectors have their mouths flush with it, the keys, the HDMI plug and a network card's jack pass through it, it carries an opening for the I/O plate, a **slot cover** per expansion slot (a card's bracket takes its place) and the **rear exhaust** above them: a fan on the Micro, Mid and Full tower (`REAR_FAN_FITTED`), and on the Mini, whose wall has only 290 units above the I/O opening (a fan that size is a stub; the 290 figure is not traced to code yet), a perforated grille in the same place (no fan, no stub, no hub header). Under the top panel hang the **top fans** (`TOP_FANS`: none on the Mini, one on the Micro, two on the Mid, three on the Full tower, clear of the 8-pin cable's run above the board); the front fans stand east of the cage. All of them are `EdgeFan`s (seen edge-on: frame and screws baked with the case, blades and light strips live: they spin with the machine and speed up with the CPU heat). Top panel with brand, vents, status lights, **the power button** (`power`) and a **disk activity LED** (lit only while a drive is busy), a quiet glass side over the board, a PSU compartment and feet. The monitor, the key tray sits outside it. The chassis is static. The camera fit (`WORLD`) covers the case, the monitor and the tray.
- **Rear I/O in side profile** (`ports.ts`): the case is a side view, so every connector is drawn the way it looks along the board's normal: depth along x with its mouth at the wall's outer face (`IO_X`), width along y, never as a face-on plate or an x-ray of its contacts. One light model everywhere (a lit top band, a shaded lower band, a contact shadow below and to the right). The wall's inner sheet (`shieldSheet`, in the wall's plane, `WALL_T` thick) has a rubber grommet round each fitted port; a port the board does not carry is a covered blank. The exterior shows the same sheet with only the part of each port that sits in the wall (`depth`), so inside and outside agree; the NIC cards' jacks use the same RJ45 (the 40G card has two QSFP+ cages, `qsfp3d.ts`). Plugs share the scale: the HDMI plug (D-shaped shell, overmould with grip ridges and a colour band, a boot onto the cable), the RJ45 plug (clear body with its blades and latch clip, a boot) and the QSFP+ plug (nickel body, 38 contacts, a pull tab) are drawn in the plug's own axes (nose at the plug's origin, body trailing along `dx, dy`), the monitor has a socket stub for the HDMI plug, and the power plugs stay seen from above. A USB stick's plug (`KEY_PLUG_H` = 88) is smaller than the port's opening (108) and slides in `KEY_INSERT` units: the stick is masked at the port's mouth, so the part inside the shell is hidden (the remove cue is not masked).
- **Fan hub** (`FAN_HUB`, `FAN_SLOT`, `fanHub` in `case.ts`; its cables are the player's plugs, `src/features/board/render/wiring/fancords.ts`): the basement, west of its drive cage, holds a fan and lighting controller: a board with a controller chip, one 4-pin header per case fan (`hubHeaders`, in the order of `fanMounts()`: the mounts' ids are the sim's `caseMounts`, `fan:<mount>` is the fan's cable), three ARGB outputs and a USB header. It is powered from a second socket on the PSU's east face (`PSU_FACE_X`, `HUB_POWER_Y`). Its control cable (`sysfan`) and one cable per fan are plugs the player drags in (`fancords.ts`: the cable leaves a slot in the tray, hangs lifted over its header while loose and seats on it); the hub itself draws only the headers and the stubs' slot. `radiatorSpan('front' | 'top')` boxes the mounts of one side for a radiator (`rect`, `side`, `ids`: the mounts it spans); the rear has none. Tested: under the shroud, west of the basement's cage, clear of the cages and the fans, a header per fan.
- **Drive cages** (`BAY`, `BAY_CELLS`): two columns of cells east of the cable gutter, by the front fans. Cell i is slot `sata<i>`; the case has `CASE_BAYS` (4, 6, 6, 8), the ones beyond the board's SATA ports are locked and say so. The basement's cage under the shroud holds the first `BASE_BAYS` (2); the main cage (`BAY`) holds the rest, in the glass area, its bottom cell level with the bottom of the board, so each of its cells is above its port. Each cage runs top down. Only the main cage's drives cost the board air (`BOARD.md` "Airflow zones"). The drives are drawn at their design size (700 x 500) and scaled to the cell (`driveScale`, 1.5). An installed drive slides in (650 ms) and its **SATA data cable** (`sataCablePath`) runs from its plug west to its own column (`SATA_LINK.col`, west to east in port order, the basement's the other way, so no two cables of one cage cross), down to the port's row and west into the port (`sataPort(i)`, under the three PSU cables in the gutter). One **power lead** (`sataPowerArt`) comes from a socket on the PSU's east face, east to the cage's west side and up it, with a plug on every installed drive's power connector (a daisy chain). M.2 sticks sit on the board (`M2_SLOTS`, slots `m20 ..`). Slots come from `snapshot.slots` / `BOARD_TIERS` (only existing slots are drawn live; absent ones are faint).
- **Drives**: `hdd` (3.5in, label, platter and head arm), `ssd-sata` (slim 2.5in with a window onto controller and NAND), `ssd-nvme` (M.2 stick on the board's M.2 slot with a heat spreader). They animate only while `storage[].busy` (platter spin, head swing, LED blink); a bus (SATA or NVMe trace plus the cable) lights only then or in an OS flow.
- **Memory sticks** (`ram3d.ts`, `ramface3d.ts`): one linear ladder by tier (`RUNGS`). Tier 0 is the bare green stick; every tier up keeps the same straight plate and adds a step: a low plate, the PCB's full height, then a light bar (4, 7, 10, 13 mm over the edge), one more groove line a tier, a better metal (steel, anodised, matte black, gunmetal, titanium, titanium with gold lines). The ECC sticks are the DDR4 and DDR5 sticks one rung up. The PCB face is painted copper: each finger rises, jogs at 45 degrees to its chip's columns and ends in a pad (`ramRoutes`, tested not to cross); the plates are one picture each (brushed metal, lines, model), so a stick stays a few draws.
- **Drop targets**: `sata0 .. sata7`, `m20 .. m22` (as many as the board has) are drop targets when empty (storage) and when occupied (an OS item is dropped on an installed drive; `inventory[].freeSlots` decides). A refused OS drop on a bare socket explains it.
- **Bus flows (OS)**: `booting-os` lights the boot drive's bus and cable, DMI and memory (drive to RAM/CPU); `ingesting` runs key to drive (USB, DMI, drive bus); `flashing` runs drive to key. Idle stays dark.
- **USB LED** follows `keyActivity`: enumerating slow amber blink, ingesting fast amber, flashing fast cyan, done steady green that breathes (collect the key). Without an OS the old loading/writing blink applies.
- **OS screen** (display only): when an OS is installed, powered and the monitor is live, in `booting-os`, `idle-os`, `running-os`, `ingesting`, `flashing`, the monitor shows a terminal-style status screen built from `snapshot.os`, `storage`, `display`: OS name/tier and state, uptime, program slots and queue use, the running job with a sliding progress cue, queue rows (`+N AHEAD` / `NEXT` / `DONE`), a `PLUG KEY ... TO COLLECT` line, storage use of the boot drive, and the live POST/boot lines while booting. Missing fields are skipped. Otherwise plain CLI text, `NO SIGNAL` and POST as before. The screen is **not** a hit target: the monitor is one object (`monitor`).
- **OS badge** (`os`): a small `OS` chip with a status dot on the drive that hosts the OS (`snapshot.os.slot`; HDD or SATA SSD in the bay, NVMe on M.2; `osBadgeRect` in `drives.ts`). The dot follows the phase on the game clock: booting pulses amber, idle steady green, ingesting / flashing blink, running breathes cyan. The badge is the hover / `selected` / `highlight` / `flyTo` target `os` and exists only while an OS is installed on a present drive; clicking the drive body still selects the drive's device id.

## The 3D cases: air, access, I/O and mounts

Review of 2026-11-07 (`case3d.ts`, `casedetail3d.ts`, `basement3d.ts`, `buildmotion.ts`). Each item below is a fix of that review.

- **Fans.** Each case has one fan size on every mount (`FAN_LEN`, the sim's `fanSize` at 23 units a mm: 92 mm 2120 on the Mini and Micro, 120 mm 2760 on the Mid, 140 mm 3220 on the Full, 20 mm thick). Front: 2, 3, 3, 3. Top: 0, 1, 2, 2. Rear: on the Micro, Mid and Full (`REAR_FAN_FITTED`), ends above the first expansion slot; the Mini has a punched round grille (the sim's `fans.rear` is false for it). The front stack sets the head room: the Mid is 410 units taller and the Full 1150 than their boards alone need, the board and the desk do not move. The rear fan stands beside the I/O plate in depth, so the case's depth follows too: `CASE_DEPTHS` 4200, 4200, 4500, 4900 (`roomplan.ts`), live bindings with `setLayoutTier`. The case is 180 units wider on every tier for the thicker fans.
- **Air paths.** Every intake and exhaust is a real opening: the front wall has an opening behind the front fans on all four kinds (the Mini's plastic front has a round slot grille per fan); the top has an opening under its filter over the top fans; the rear fan's hole is round; the floor has an opening under the supply's fan with a dust filter that slides out at the rear; the side panel's lower intake is open over the vent span; the shroud's east wall has slots, so the front fans' air reaches the supply (the main cage stands in their stream over the shelf; its drives cost the board air, the basement's cage is out of it). A perforated sheet shows from both sides.
- **Power supply access.** The supply sits in the back corner of the basement, its rear in an opening of the rear wall (four screws, the exhaust grille, the inlet and the switch). Its fan is on its underside over the floor's opening; a passive supply (`fan: false`) has a punched vent. To take it out: the shroud's cover comes off (two thumbscrews, `PSU_COVER`), the four rear screws come out, the supply slides 240 off the rear wall, then comes out through the open side. `PSU_ROOM` (360) leaves the slide its room before the drive cage. The cord is drawn only while a supply is fitted.
- **Fan parts.** A case comes with no fans. An empty front or top mount shows a plate with the throat and four screw holes (`fanMountPlates`); the rear wall has the four holes round its grille, and the screws show only with a fan (`rearFanScrews`). A fan device is the one model `fanPart` (`fans3d.ts`): flanges, venturi and posts, motor and struts on the air's exit side, the rotor, a hub badge. High-airflow has nine steeper blades and violet corner pads. A heatsink or a radiator builds the fans on its own spots (`coolerModel`, `radiatorModel`, the `vrm-sink` case of `buildPart` in `parts.ts`); each runs on its cable (`Spin.needs`: `fan:<mount>`, `cpufan`, `vrmfan`, `ramfan`). A free spot is a pick box and a drop target (`fanPicks`, `fanMounts`).
- **Fan service.** A front fan moves inward off the front wall first, a top fan down, a rear fan inward, then each comes out through the open side (`fan-front`, `fan-top`, `fan-rear`). A fan on a heatsink lifts straight off it (`fan`).
- **Board mounts.** Threaded holes at every position of the boards the case takes, brass studs under the fitted board only (see board.md). The board lifts off its studs, slides its ports out of the rear opening, then comes away.
- **Rear I/O by tier.** The ports stand at their centre line, where the plugs aim. Starter: stamped steel shield, the PS/2 keyboard and mouse, a serial and a parallel port (look only), audio, one black USB 2, HDMI. Basic: stamped shield, one PS/2 combo port, VGA and DVI (look only), audio, LAN, two black USB 2, HDMI. Mainstream: printed black shield, blue USB 3, a BIOS button, DP. Enthusiast: red USB 3.2, USB-C, Wi-Fi, optical, clear CMOS. The front panel: USB-A tongues by the board's tier; the front USB 3 header (`USB_3`) is on the Mainstream and the Enthusiast boards.
- **Expansion slots.** Every card's bracket fills its slot's opening (`bracketSpan`: a bay 355 units north of the slot's line and 325 south, whatever the card's size, so the bays tile the slot pitch with a 20-unit rib; a NIC's jacks stand in the north part, a GPU's ports in the south part); a GPU's bracket has vent slots and an HDMI and two DisplayPorts. A free slot has a vented blank (`slotBlank`) in the case's finish (`SLOT_BLANK`). Each bracket folds over at the glass side, so the retention bar and its thumbscrews are there. The monitor cable's plug goes into the GPU's HDMI (`gpuVideoAt`), inside the opening.
- **Openings.** Every extrusion with holes goes through `extrude` (`core/render/three/geometry.ts`), which winds each hole against its outline. ExtrudeGeometry corrects a hole only when it turns the outline, so a hole wound like a clockwise outline got walls that faced into the material. They were culled, and the background showed through the sides of every grille, vent and slot (`casekit3d.test.ts` checks it).
- **Panels.** The back panel has a flange folded onto the rear wall with two thumbscrews on every case; the Mini's side panel has the same flange (its two thumbscrews turn out before it opens). The Mini's front has two blank 5.25" bay covers (a rim, a gap and a finger notch each) and a "PC-1" badge.

Open choices, not changed: the supply stays in the basement on every case (an old OEM tower like the Mini had it at the top of the rear wall); the Basic and Mainstream boards both have 100 Mb LAN in the sim.

## The empty case

Each case must look good with nothing in it: the shop's viewer shows it so (`showcase.ts`: the body, the closed door, the shroud, the cage, the hub and the coiled front-panel leads). Real scale: 23 units a mm, so a case screw's head is r 45 to 70. The fixings are one kit (`casekit3d.ts`: `screws`, `rivets`, `thumbscrews`, `capScrews`, `pads`); `screwM` and `rivetM` (`casetray3d.ts`) give each kind's finish: zinc in the Mini, black in the Micro and Mid, polished in the Full.

- **Tray** (`casetray3d.ts`). A back panel and the tray's sheet over it with real holes: the CPU cut-out (not on the Mini), the Mini's punched vents and keyholes, lacing slots. A stamped panel under the board; a brass-toned stud under the fitted board (current: `#b99a58`, `casetray3d.ts`; target: the one gold `#d4af5f`, art-style §2, on hold), a threaded hole at every other position, each stamped with its form factors (I, M, A, E). Stiffening beads, tie-down bridges in two columns, Velcro straps on the Mid and Full. The kind's cable holes (`grommet`, `wires3d.ts`: a ring, the dark hole, slit rubber flaps; the Mini's are bare rolled holes) and each lead's own hole. The Full has a vented cable cover over its gutter.
- **Frame.** Riveted flanges round the tray, rivet rows on the walls, a rolled lip round the side opening with rubber bumpers and the receivers of the door's hooks. The door has its hem and hooks (`doorInner`, on `side`: it follows the case's cut corners).
- **Basement** (`basement3d.ts`). The shroud's top is a sheet with two cable holes by the tray and vent slots over the vent span, riveted to the tray. Its cover has the print, a slotted vent, two thumbscrews and two screws; the fixed panel east of it is perforated over the vent span. East of the panels the top runs on as the main cage's shelf (its glass-side edge folded down), open from the fan hub on, to the slotted east wall. Under it: the fan hub on the tray sheet and the basement's cage of two. Rubber strips under the supply.
- **Drive cages** (`drivecage3d.ts`: `cagePlan`, `baseCagePlan`). The main cage stands on the shelf from the tray sheet to the glass; the basement's cage of two (sata0 and sata1) stands on the floor under it, out of the front fans' air. Each is screwed to the tray by its back flanges, in the case's finish (`CAGE_TONE`, `TRAY_TONE`): windowed side walls with guides, slotted top and bottom plates, front folds. Each bay has a tray (a floor with air windows, windowed rails, four pins in rubber rings, a handle with a finger hole and a latch); the trays slide out toward the glass. **Holes (decision, 2026-11-16, ASSET-02):** a pattern of many small holes (slots, mesh, vents) is a perforated sheet with the alpha texture (`perforatedFlat`); one structural opening (a side wall's window per tray, a rail's window, the floor's air windows, a screw hole, a cable cut-out) stays cut as geometry. Its outline and the drive behind it read at every zoom, it costs a few dozen triangles and no material, and an alpha cut-out of that size blurs at the near zoom. The LOD1 cap of 4 materials is the overview stand-in's job (LOD-02); the case's LOD0 cap of 16 holds (`casemat.test.ts`).
- **Fan hub** (`fanhub3d.ts`). A green board on four brass standoffs with its print and traces, the controller, passives and two capacitors, a side-entry 4-pin header per mount (the fan cords' seats do not move), the input header, the SATA power socket, three ARGB outputs and a USB 2.0 header.
- **Rear wall** (`rearWall`). The rear fan's screw holes are slots (the fan can sit higher or lower), and its screws are the kit's. A vented column beside each expansion slot, a lip folded in round the I/O opening, a kit thumbscrew per slot on the retention bar.
- **Mini's bays** (`bayFrame`). A zinc frame behind the two bay covers: side plates with screw slots and a window, top and bottom plates, rails between the bays, a tool-less latch per bay on the glass side. The corner screws of its side panel are kit screws.
- **Front-panel leads** (`frontPanelLeads`). From their hole under the board's south edge to the `F_PANEL` (four thin wires), `USB_2`, `USB_3` (thick blue) and `ARGB` headers, static and hung as the system fan's cord. They are in `wires`, and `boardRoots` lists them, so a board swap lifts them. With no board (the viewer) they lie coiled and tied over their hole (`leadCoil`).

**Part redos accepted (2026-11-16, ASSET-01).** Judged by art-style v3 from real-GPU shots (RTX 5080, High and Low, 14:00): the case shell and kit (one hole method, charcoal `case0` in the shop's 3D thumbnail and viewer), the city trees past 300 m (Low: green masses along the avenue, no popping), the NVMe label drape, the fans' silhouettes (round at 24 to 40 sides), the solder joints, TPM header and NIC, the heat pipes and the AIO pump, the teacup and the bedside lantern, and the grey ladder (one family of greys across the room and the machine). No item was rejected. The dark heat-pipe variant stays on hold.

## Level of detail: interior and exterior (far zoom)

The 2D zoom bands (`LOD` in `board.ts`, the exterior crossfade and the room cap) were removed with the 2D board. The 3D case has its own detail rules: see "The 3D cases" above and [three.md](three.md).

**The far board texture.** The 2D far texture (`Z_BAKE`) was removed. The frame rule `boardBakeFrame` (`layout/layout.ts`) still takes the board and its rear I/O plate, so the plugs seat in their ports in any far picture that uses it.

The 2D interior and exterior layers (`interior`, `exteriorLayer`, `monitorLayer`, `cableExtLayer`) were removed with the 2D board. The 3D case is built in `three/case3d.ts`.

**Exterior case** (`exterior.ts`; its 2D layer was removed with the 2D board): an opaque chassis on the same `CASE` rectangle, so the power button, its on / off / error / disk lights, the USB port and the video cable need no moving. Brushed steel body with a lit top edge, the top panel with vents and brand, a **smoked tempered glass** side (a hole in the body with a thin smoked pane and faint reflections, so the real interior shows through it at every zoom), a perforated PSU intake and nameplate, feet, and the **drive bay front** (a faceplate per installed HDD / SATA SSD, a blanking plate for an empty cell, a flat plate for the cell this tier lacks; NVMe lives on the board and is not visible). The **rear I/O plate** shows the external connectors this board has: audio, an ethernet jack only when `features.ethernetMbps > 0`, HDMI, a second display port only when `displayPorts >= 2`, and `usbPorts` of the four USB positions (bare outlines for the rest). The static art is built once per (theme, tier, drives) and baked to one texture (0.35x). The only live part is the **accent strip** (a thin bar on the front rim, plus the drive-front activity LEDs), on the game clock: off dark; idle a dim steady cyan; halted a steady amber (on, finished or waiting for a boot key); working (posting, loading, running, writing, any OS phase) a bright pulse travelling down the strip; error (`phase error` or PSU trip) blinking red; dead board a steady dim red. The power button and the ON / OFF / ERR lights at far zoom follow the same rules as up close (see "Power-off rules" in [render/live.md](live.md)). Each case tier is its own drawing (`STYLES` in `exterior.ts`, see "Four real cases" below). All share the rear I/O plate, the top panel with its lights and the PSU intake. Dark and light themes use the same structure. **Four real cases** (`CaseStyle.kind` in `STYLES`, helpers in `casedecor.ts`): Mini is a beige OEM tower (square corners, a **solid side with no window**, a perforated vent patch, screws, a label plate, round feet; the interior shows when the camera zooms in and the exterior fades), Micro a matte black mid-tower (a tinted window over the upper part, a seam, a debossed logo), Mid a gaming case (slanted top vents, an angled mesh cap, a window with a cut corner, one violet light strip under the glass, a debossed logo plate, two rubber rails) and Full a dark monolith (a charcoal square box with a small radius, one smoked glass as large as the gaming window in a thin gasket, a debossed four-bar mark, a hex mesh intake and filter, a flat graphite plinth; no pillars, rails, name plates or lit fan windows). All static, baked with the rest. Only `LAYOUT_CASE` picks the look. The Micro side is one sheet (no separate top plate, I/O column, bezel or PSU panel outlines). The Micro, Mid and Full carry a PSU exhaust fan in the rear wall (`PSU_REAR_FAN`, spun with the PSU fan) and the mains inlet sits below it; the Mini has neither. The two cheapest PSUs (`psu0`, `psu1`, `fan: false`) are passive: no fan art, no airflow stream, the readout says NO FAN. One brand mark only (the badge); the PSU shroud follows the case kind (cream steel, black slots, angled RGB, silver hex). **Inside each case** (`caseInterior`, `kindNow` in `props/case.ts`; `CASE_KIND` and `caseWindow` in `casedecor.ts` are shared with the exterior, so the two cannot drift): cream-painted steel and no glass in the Mini (four screw holes where the side panel fixes), black powder coat with a rubber-rimmed cable cut-out and a tinted window over the upper part in the Micro, blue-black with a slanted cut-out, a cut-corner window, a violet floor glow and one colour per fan light in the Mid, a dark charcoal body with a smoked full-height window, a hex-perforated tray and a violet light from the front edge in the Full. The fan light strips are off in the Mini and Micro. Board, cables, drives and every live part are the same in all four.

**In 3D** the same four cases are real boxes (`features/board/render/three/case3d.ts`, the 3D overview is docs/render/three.md); they read `CASE_KIND`, `caseWindow`, `WINDOW_CUTS` and `CASE_CUTS` from `casedecor.ts`, so 2D and 3D keep one design. The 3D side panel opens on a hinge (`casedoor.ts`) where the 2D exterior fades.

**The Full tower is a dark monolith** (`'show'`): a square charcoal box (`CASE_CUTS` and `WINDOW_CUTS` are square, so no chamfers), one smoked glass (tint 0.25) the size of the gaming window in a thin dark gasket, a debossed four-bar mark, a hex mesh intake and top filter, a flat graphite plinth inset from the sides. Its only lights are the front state strip and the two inner strips (violet, `innerStrips`). The 2D exterior matches it (`exterior.ts`). No coolant loop is drawn inside: the board covers the cavity.

**Inside the case (look).** The board is a gradient solder mask lit from the top left with a lit and a shaded bevel, silkscreen corner brackets round the CPU socket, and two-layer drop shadows under parts (`shadow` in `boardParts.ts`: a soft penumbra and a tight core). The light theme's board is a sage, mixed in `makePalette`. The case cavity is a gradient with stamped ribs and an inner shadow, the tray stands off the back panel, and the drive cage is a metal rack (perforated uprights, a recessed bay per slot with rails and grommets, corner brackets on a usable bay). `caseShade` (case.ts) sits over the board under the glass: a corner vignette and a warm wash from the lamp's side. The glass's reflections (`caseGlass`) fade to 30% once the case is open. Gradient helpers `lgrad` / `rgrad` are in `ink.ts` (tests stub `FillGradient`, as `mail.test.ts` does).

**Interaction at far zoom.** The 2D hit rules for the exterior (external ids only, by exterior alpha) were removed with the 2D board. Hit rules are in [interact.md](interact.md).

## PSU face

The bottom strip (`PSU`, the drop target) is two things: the **PSU unit** (`PSU_UNIT`, at the east end under the cable sockets: the intake fan seen edge-on in its bottom face (`psuFanRect`, an `EdgeFan`; it draws air up from under the case), label plate with the rated watts and an 80+ badge, the output sockets on its top edge, the power switch) and, beside it, the **shroud** that hides the cables (a lit top edge, the vent field `VENTS`, a brand line and the readout panel `PSU_PANEL`). The readout panel: **load** (watts drawn, "of N W", percentage, 20-segment gauge with the 100% mark, red over budget, `TRIPPED` / `OFF` states), **temperature** (value, COOL/WARM/HOT, bar) and **fan** (spinning icon, rpm, `STOPPED`). Data: `power.totalW` and `power.budgetW`, and the PSU fan speed, which follows load. **The temperature is an estimate**: the sim does not model PSU heat (its `tempC` stays ambient), so the renderer low-pass filters `25 + 34 * load^1.5` C; the fan rpm is likewise derived from the load-driven fan speed. If the sim grows a PSU temperature, replace `psuTemp` in `live.ts` with it. No snapshot fields were added.

## Build my PC's frame (PERF2)

Goal: on the phone profile (412x915, dpr 2.6, CPU /4) a drawn frame of Build my PC holds 30 fps, with no change to the picture. Code: `buildmode3d.ts` (`frame`, in `src/features/board/render/three`), and `ondemand.ts`, `post.ts` (`meter`), `stillworld.ts`, `drawsort.ts`, `eye.ts` (in `src/core/render/three`).

What the stage draws (the biggest machine, counted in Node): the machine has 990 objects, 689 meshes (193 instanced) and 440 materials; the monitor adds 32 meshes and 11 materials; the baked art tiles add the rest of the profile's 957 meshes and 522 materials. The machine is off: no fan turns, no strip pulses, `Machine3D.moving` is false. So nothing moves at idle.

Rule: **a frame draws only for a change the player can see, and a drawn frame does no work for a part that did not change.**

1. **Tile frames come in batches.** One source of idle frames is the art's bake queue (a hypothesis for the bench's idle frames, not a proven cause: see Measure): each baked tile calls `sceneChanged`, and each call drew the whole scene (about 45 ms at CPU /4) to show one tile. `DrawGate.want(now, busy, more)`: while the queue has jobs (`more`) and nothing else asks, the changes wait and draw as one frame each `BATCH_MS` (200 ms). The last tile draws at once. A tick that drew nothing bakes for 12 ms, not 6. A camera move, a part's motion or an ask draws on its tick as before. Result: the tiles sharpen in steps of 200 ms, and the queue empties sooner, because the time goes to the bakes.
2. **The light meter does not hold the frame.** `Post.meter` read its 16x9 grid with `readPixels` on each fourth drawn frame; that call waits until the GPU has drawn the whole frame. It now starts `readRenderTargetPixelsAsync` (a pixel buffer and a fence) and returns the reading when it has come. One read runs at a time. The eye eases over about a second, so a reading one or two frames late shows no change. The stage takes a late reading on a tick with no draw too (`Post.metered`), so the last frame's reading is not lost.
3. **Still parts keep their matrices.** three composes and multiplies the matrix of each of the 1000 objects on each frame. The stage sets `scene.matrixWorldAutoUpdate = false` and calls `syncWorld(scene)` before a draw: it composes an object only when its position, turn, scale or parent differs from its last compose (or when three's own update composed another pose between), and multiplies only under a changed object. No list of "still" objects: a card swings flat with the zoom (`pose`), a holder lifts any part, so the check is on the values. A matrix written by hand (`matrixAutoUpdate` false) counts as changed.
4. **Opaque draws go in program order.** three sorts the opaque list by material id; the builders make materials of several programs in turn, so the program changes often, and each change uploads the lights and the camera again. `byProgram` (`renderer.setOpaqueSort`) sorts by program, then material, then depth. `rankMaterials` gives each material its program's number after a frame with a change, and each 32 drawn frames. A stale rank costs time only: the depth buffer gives the same picture in any order.
5. **The eye rests.** Each drawn frame meters again, and a reading a little off (a baked tile, the last bits of a float) could keep the eye moving, so the stage would draw each frame. `Eye` (`eye.ts`) moves its goal only for a reading more than `EYE_DEAD` (3 % in log) from it; less is not seen. The eye is at its goal within `EYE_DONE` (1e-3), and then it asks for no frame.

6. **Low: stand-ins at every zoom and one material for equal parts (PHONE-01, 2026-11-16).** The profile of a zoom frame (CPU /4, 7.3 s) said `setProgram` took 40 % (material changes), `syncWorld` 8 %, `projectObject` 15 %; the scene had 675 drawn meshes and 424 materials of 217 different looks. Two cuts, both Low only: `shareMaterials(…, { across: true })` (equal materials of different parts become one: 424 to 362 materials, 28.7 to 31.3–32.1 fps) and the stand-ins without the `LOD_CEIL` floor (585 draw calls to 223, 41.7–44.5 fps, dp95 26–35 ms). A step holds the whole machine (`holdDetail([root])` while `anim` or `busy`). Numbers, shots and the one known difference (the video cable) are in [lod.md](lod.md) ("Build my PC at Low"). Medium and High keep every part itself from the own zoom in. Not done: a cheaper `syncWorld` (1,117 objects, 26 compares each); a deeper merge across parents (a parent may carry a fade, a pick or a motion that nothing marks).

Weighed and not done:

- A lower cadence for the fans: the stage's machine is off, so no fan turns. In the room the fans do turn; that is the menu's frame, not this task.
- `matrixAutoUpdate` off on fixed lists of parts: not safe (see 3). `syncWorld` gets the same saving with no list.
- Materials shared across parts: 232 of the machine's materials have a share key, and 97 of the keys are distinct, so a share over the whole machine drops about 135 materials. `shareMaterials` keeps each part's materials apart because a fade (`fadeTree`) and a part's disposal write to them. It needs the audit of PERF22; it is the next cut for `refreshUniformsCommon` and `markUniformsLightsNeedsUpdate`.
- The monitor as one merged mesh (32 draws to about 10): 3 % of the draws; `Monitor.set` writes to its face and LED, so the merge needs a skip list.
- Culling: `intersectsObject` is one sphere test per mesh. A group test saves time only when the whole group is out of view, which is not the case in the framed view.
- Casters: the shadow maps are not drawn again in a frame with no moving part, so the 689 casters cost nothing.

Tests: `ondemand.test.ts` (the batch, the last change, `forced`), `post.test.ts` (`meterMean`, one read at a time, a failed read, a read after the close), `stillworld.test.ts` (the same matrices as three; no compose for a still tree; a move, a new parent, a pose changed and changed back), `drawsort.test.ts` (the program changes fall; three's order holds for the rest), `eye.test.ts` (the eye rests under small jitter; a 2x change moves it and it settles; the range clamps; no reading changes nothing), `buildmode3d.stage.test.ts` (`BuildStage.frame` with the GPU parts mocked: a still stage draws nothing; a late meter reading on a tick with no draw wakes it, and it goes still again; a reading inside the dead band does not wake it).

Measure (`bench:modes --phone`, Low, Medium, High): the zoom row's drawn fps and dp95. The idle row still draws nearly every frame at CPU /4: over 15 s idle on Low, main drew 159 frames and this change 167 (Reviewer, PC, 2026-11-12). The cause is not found. Suspects, none proven: the bake queue drains slowly at CPU /4 (its 6-12 ms budget does a quarter of the work); a negative `dt` on the first tick (the rAF time before the `performance.now()` of `open`) moves the side panel's `t` the wrong way, past 1 when the gap is long (seen on a slow software-GL run, where `t` reached 2.2), and it then eases back over many frames; part skins that still ease (`Machine3D.moving`); the eye. Without the throttle the idle stage draws no frame in 5 s. In the frame profile, `readPixels` and most of `updateMatrixWorld` must be gone, and `setProgram` and `markUniformsLightsNeedsUpdate` lower.
