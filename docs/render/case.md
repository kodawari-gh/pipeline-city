# Rendering: case, bay, OS screen, level of detail, installed devices

Part of the renderer docs; the hub is [`RENDER.md`](../RENDER.md). Code: `src/render/room/case.ts`, `room/exterior.ts`, `board/devices.ts`, `board/drives.ts`, `board/accelart.ts`, `board/ports.ts`.

## Files

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `ports.ts` | the rear connectors, the plugs that seat into them and the wall they pass through, all in **side profile** with one light model (`portProfile`, `shieldSheet`, `hdmiPlug`, `rj45Plug`, `usbPlug`, `monitorSocket`); see "Rear I/O in side profile" |
| `devices.ts` | static art of installed devices: CPU package + die floorplan, DIMM, cooler, PSU shroud, monitor, sleeved cables and plugs |
| `exterior.ts` | the far-zoom exterior case (static art baked to one texture) and its live accent strip, `LOD` thresholds are in `board.ts` |
| `case.ts` | the PC case: chassis and rear wall (the I/O opening, a slot cover per expansion slot, the rear fan), the tray with a brass standoff at every mounting hole of every form factor the case takes, the fan hub in the floor corner and the fans' cable stubs, top and front fans (`EdgeFan`: blades and light strips that spin with the machine), glass, the drive cage, SATA data cables and the power lead |
| `drives.ts` | drive art (`hdd`, `ssd-sata`, `ssd-nvme`) and their live parts (platter, head arm, activity LED) |
| `accelart.ts` | the accelerators: GPU and NPU cards in a PCIe slot (`cardArt`, drawn like the NIC, longer cards reaching further east; a GPU has 1 to 3 fans in bezels along its top edge over a shroud, `cardArt(..., live)` leaves the blades and the cover to `GpuView` on the board and draws them still for a shop tile; a grid of cells on an NPU), and the TPM module standing on the board's `TPM` header (`tpmArt`, `tpmRect`); the cards and the module are shop / picker pictures too (`thumbs.ts`), with an SVG fallback in `PartArt.tsx`. The `pcie` power cable is drawn only while the snapshot lists it (a card needs the lead, or it is plugged) |

## The case, the bay and the OS screen (storage and OS phase)

- **Case** (`CASE`, `case.ts`): a thin, low-contrast chassis around the motherboard and the PSU. The **rear wall** is a slab (`WALL_T`) whose outer face is the I/O shield plate's west edge (`IO_X`): the board's connectors have their mouths flush with it, the keys, the HDMI plug and a network card's jack pass through it, it carries an opening for the I/O plate, a **slot cover** per expansion slot (a card's bracket takes its place) and the **rear exhaust** above them: a fan on the Micro, Mid and Full tower (`REAR_FAN_FITTED`), and on the Mini, whose wall has only 290 units above the I/O opening (a fan that size is a stub), a perforated grille in the same place (no fan, no stub, no hub header). Under the top panel hang the **top fans** (`TOP_FANS`: none on the Mini, one on the Micro, two on the Mid, three on the Full tower, clear of the 8-pin cable's run above the board); the front fans stand east of the cage. All of them are `EdgeFan`s (seen edge-on: frame and screws baked with the case, blades and light strips live: they spin with the machine and speed up with the CPU heat). Top panel with brand, vents, status lights, **the power button** (`power`) and a **disk activity LED** (lit only while a drive is busy), a quiet glass side over the board, a PSU compartment and feet. The monitor, the key tray sits outside it. The chassis is static (a vector layer close up, one baked texture below zoom 0.14). The camera fit (`WORLD`) covers the case, the monitor and the tray.
- **Rear I/O in side profile** (`ports.ts`): the case is a side view, so every connector is drawn the way it looks along the board's normal: depth along x with its mouth at the wall's outer face (`IO_X`), width along y, never as a face-on plate or an x-ray of its contacts. One light model everywhere (a lit top band, a shaded lower band, a contact shadow below and to the right). The wall's inner sheet (`shieldSheet`, in the wall's plane, `WALL_T` thick) has a rubber grommet round each fitted port; a port the board does not carry is a covered blank. The exterior shows the same sheet with only the part of each port that sits in the wall (`depth`), so inside and outside agree; the NIC cards' jacks use the same RJ45. Plugs share the scale: the HDMI plug (D-shaped shell, overmould with grip ridges and a colour band, a boot onto the cable) and the RJ45 plug (clear body with its blades and latch clip, a boot) are drawn in the plug's own axes (nose at the plug's origin, body trailing along `dx, dy`), the monitor has a socket stub for the HDMI plug, and the power plugs stay seen from above. A USB stick's plug (`KEY_PLUG_H` = 88) is smaller than the port's opening (108) and slides in `KEY_INSERT` units: the stick is masked at the port's mouth, so the part inside the shell is hidden (the remove cue is not masked).
- **Fan hub** (`FAN_HUB`, `FAN_SLOT`, `fanHub` and `fanTrunkArt` in `case.ts`): the floor corner east of the PSU and under the drive cage holds a fan and lighting controller: a board with a controller chip, one 4-pin header per case fan (`hubHeaders`: front, top and rear fans), three ARGB outputs and a USB header. It is powered from a second socket on the PSU's east face (`HUB_POWER_Y`), its control cable runs from the board's `SYS_FAN` header down over the board's south edge, east under the PSU cables and up into its input header, and a cable from each fan header rises into a slot in the tray (the fans' own cables show only a plug and a stub, `fanStubs`, then go behind the tray). Tested: inside the case, clear of the PSU, the cage and the fans, a header and a stub per fan.
- **Drive cage** (`BAY`, `BAY_CELLS`): a column of cells in the glass area, east of the cable gutter, in front of the front fans and level with the board's SATA ports (cell i is slot `sata<i>`, top to bottom; the cage has the case's bays (`CASE_BAYS`: 4, 6, 6, 8, so it fills the case's height), the ones beyond the board's SATA ports are locked and say so; the bottom cell lines up with the bottom of the board, so every cell is above its port). The drives are drawn at their design size (700 x 500) and scaled to the cell (`driveScale`, 1.5). An installed drive slides in (650 ms) and its **SATA data cable** (`sataCablePath`) runs from its plug west to its own column (`SATA_LINK.col`, west to east in port order, so no two cables cross), down to the port's row and west into the port (`sataPort(i)`, under the three PSU cables in the gutter). One **power lead** (`sataPowerArt`) comes from a socket on the PSU's east face, east to the cage's west side and up it, with a plug on every installed drive's power connector (a daisy chain). M.2 sticks sit on the board (`M2_SLOTS`, slots `m20 ..`). Slots come from `snapshot.slots` / `BOARD_TIERS` (only existing slots are drawn live; absent ones are faint).
- **Drives**: `hdd` (3.5in, label, platter and head arm), `ssd-sata` (slim 2.5in with a window onto controller and NAND), `ssd-nvme` (M.2 stick on the board's M.2 slot with a heat spreader). They animate only while `storage[].busy` (platter spin, head swing, LED blink); a bus (SATA or NVMe trace plus the cable) lights only then or in an OS flow.
- **Drop targets**: `sata0 .. sata7`, `m20 .. m22` (as many as the board has) are drop targets when empty (storage) and when occupied (an OS item is dropped on an installed drive; `inventory[].freeSlots` decides). A refused OS drop on a bare socket explains it.
- **Bus flows (OS)**: `booting-os` lights the boot drive's bus and cable, DMI and memory (drive to RAM/CPU); `ingesting` runs key to drive (USB, DMI, drive bus); `flashing` runs drive to key. Idle stays dark.
- **USB LED** follows `keyActivity`: enumerating slow amber blink, ingesting fast amber, flashing fast cyan, done steady green that breathes (collect the key). Without an OS the old loading/writing blink applies.
- **OS screen** (display only): when an OS is installed, powered and the monitor is live, in `booting-os`, `idle-os`, `running-os`, `ingesting`, `flashing`, the monitor shows a terminal-style status screen built from `snapshot.os`, `storage`, `display`: OS name/tier and state, uptime, program slots and queue use, the running job with a sliding progress cue, queue rows (`+N AHEAD` / `NEXT` / `DONE`), a `PLUG KEY ... TO COLLECT` line, storage use of the boot drive, and the live POST/boot lines while booting. Missing fields are skipped. Otherwise plain CLI text, `NO SIGNAL` and POST as before. The screen is **not** a hit target: the monitor is one object (`monitor`).
- **OS badge** (`os`): a small `OS` chip with a status dot on the drive that hosts the OS (`snapshot.os.slot`; HDD or SATA SSD in the bay, NVMe on M.2; `osBadgeRect` in `drives.ts`). The dot follows the phase on the game clock: booting pulses amber, idle steady green, ingesting / flashing blink, running breathes cyan. The badge is the hover / `selected` / `highlight` / `flyTo` target `os` and exists only while an OS is installed on a present drive; clicking the drive body still selects the drive's device id.

## Level of detail: interior and exterior (far zoom)

The camera scale drives one clean LOD, in multiples of the **fit zoom** (`fits.z`, the zoom of the default overview). Constants are `LOD` in `board.ts` (exported, also `window.__board.LOD` in dev):

| Zoom (x fit) | What shows |
|---|---|
| `>= 1.9` (`LOD.fadeFrom`) | the case is open: the interior shows clear (about when the case overflows the screen; the default overview is 1.0) |
| 1.9 to 1.5 (`fadeFrom` to `fadeTo`) | the exterior case **crossfades in** over the interior (smoothstep; the exterior alpha is the fade, the interior stays opaque below it, so there are no see-through artefacts) |
| `<= 1.5` (`LOD.fadeTo`) | the case is closed, exterior over the interior. The glass side is see-through, so the (baked) interior still shows through it at every zoom; below `LOD.liveFrom` (0.8) `Live.setInterior(false)` skips the interior's per-frame work |
| the room cap | how far the camera can zoom out: where the room fills the screen, never short of `LOD.capK` (0.8) of the overview (see "The room" in [render/room/shell.md](room/shell.md)) |

**The far board texture.** Below a zoom of 0.2 the board is one baked texture (`Z_BAKE`); its frame is `boardBakeFrame(BD, BR.io)`, the board **and its rear I/O plate** (the plate stands in the case's west wall, outside the board's own rectangle), so the back panel with its audio jacks, HDMI, ethernet and USB ports stays in the picture at every zoom and the plugs seat in their ports (it used to be the board's rectangle alone, which left the case's black wall and the plugs stopping short of it between zooms 0.1 and 0.2). The exterior's own rear plate shows the same ports (a fitted USB port has its coloured tongue in the mouth).

**Interior group** (`interior` container): case chassis, board, devices (CPU, DIMMs, PSU, cooler, heatsink, bay, drives), SATA cables, ATX / CPU cables, glass, cooler, pipeline view, and `live.root` (bus flows, PSU readout, OS badge, VRM glow, PCH). **Always external** (outside the group, drawn at every zoom): the monitor (`monitorLayer`, one cached texture far out), the video cable (`cableExtLayer`), the tray, the power button and its lights, the plugged key, and `live.ext` (monitor screen text and OS screen, USB LED, monitor LED, boot bar, video cable glow).

**Exterior case** (`exterior.ts`, `exteriorLayer` above the interior, below the external things): an opaque chassis on the same `CASE` rectangle, so the power button, its on / off / error / disk lights, the USB port and the video cable need no moving. Brushed steel body with a lit top edge, the top panel with vents and brand, a **smoked tempered glass** side (a hole in the body with a thin smoked pane and faint reflections, so the real interior shows through it at every zoom), a perforated PSU intake and nameplate, feet, and the **drive bay front** (a faceplate per installed HDD / SATA SSD, a blanking plate for an empty cell, a flat plate for the cell this tier lacks; NVMe lives on the board and is not visible). The **rear I/O plate** shows the external connectors this board has: audio, an ethernet jack only when `features.ethernetMbps > 0`, HDMI, a second display port only when `displayPorts >= 2`, and `usbPorts` of the four USB positions (bare outlines for the rest). The static art is built once per (theme, tier, drives) and baked to one texture (0.35x). The only live part is the **accent strip** (a thin bar on the front rim, plus the drive-front activity LEDs), on the game clock: off dark; idle a dim steady cyan; halted a steady amber (on, finished or waiting for a boot key); working (posting, loading, running, writing, any OS phase) a bright pulse travelling down the strip; error (`phase error` or PSU trip) blinking red; dead board a steady dim red. The power button and the ON / OFF / ERR lights at far zoom follow the same rules as up close (see "Power-off rules" in [render/live.md](live.md)). Each case tier has its own look (`STYLES` in `exterior.ts`): the Mini is a pale aluminium shell with soft corners and an amber light, the Micro matte black with cyan, the Mid gunmetal with brass trim round the glass and violet, the Full graphite with an aluminium frame, square corners, an RGB light line under the glass and glowing feet. All have a bevelled rim, a recessed top panel with an accent hairline, a glass bezel and gasket, and a pressed-metal badge. Dark and light themes use the same structure.

**Inside the case (look).** The board is a gradient solder mask lit from the top left with a lit and a shaded bevel, silkscreen corner brackets round the CPU socket, and two-layer drop shadows under parts (`shadow` in `boardParts.ts`: a soft penumbra and a tight core). The light theme's board is a sage, mixed in `makePalette`. The case cavity is a gradient with stamped ribs and an inner shadow, the tray stands off the back panel, and the drive cage is a metal rack (perforated uprights, a recessed bay per slot with rails and grommets, corner brackets on a usable bay). `caseShade` (case.ts) sits over the board under the glass: a corner vignette and a warm wash from the lamp's side. The glass's reflections (`caseGlass`) fade to 30% once the case is open (`glassSheen` in `board.ts`). Gradient helpers `lgrad` / `rgrad` are in `ink.ts` (tests stub `FillGradient`, as `mail.test.ts` does).

**Interaction at far zoom** (exterior alpha above 0.5): only external ids can be hit: `power`, `usb`, `monitor` (hover writes `monitor`), `display0` (the empty monitor bay), `tray`, `key:<id>`, `cable.video`, and `case` (the **whole** chassis rectangle). Interior ids (slots, devices, cables, `bay`, `os`, `psu`, regions, die tiles) are not hit targets then. Below 0.5 exterior alpha the interior hits are back.

**Revealing the interior on demand.** While a part or an internal cable is being dragged (`dragging.kind` `part`, or `cable` other than `cable.video`), or while `highlight` contains an **internal** id that exists right now (anything that is not one of the external ids above), the exterior fades away (about 0.3 s, wall time) regardless of zoom, so drag and drop and the tutorial work from any distance. It fades back when the drag ends and the highlight is gone. `flyTo(id)` is unchanged (it ends at a zoom of at least the fit zoom, so the interior shows). Highlights on `monitor`, `power`, `usb`, `tray`, `key:*`, `case`, `display0`, `cable.video` do not reveal. In dev, `window.__board.lod()` returns `{extA, reveal, interior}`.

**Performance.** When fully exterior, the interior layers are neither updated nor drawn (static layers stay baked; the live interior work is skipped, only fans / drive LED levels / the disk LED keep their cheap state). Measured in headless Chrome with software GL (SwiftShader, 1360x800, a full machine with a SATA SSD, the same scene before and after this change): overview 32 ms/frame before and 33 ms after (no regression); mid-fade (x0.6) 24 ms; exterior (x0.45) 8-9 ms; far (x0.25) 8 ms (before, the camera could not go below 24 ms). Real GPU numbers were not measured.

## PSU face

The bottom strip (`PSU`, the drop target) is two things: the **PSU unit** (`PSU_UNIT`, at the east end under the cable sockets: fan grill, label plate with the rated watts and an 80+ badge, vent slots, the output sockets on its top edge, the power switch) and, beside it, the **shroud** that hides the cables (a lit top edge, the vent field `VENTS`, a brand line and the readout panel `PSU_PANEL`). The readout panel: **load** (watts drawn, "of N W", percentage, 20-segment gauge with the 100% mark, red over budget, `TRIPPED` / `OFF` states), **temperature** (value, COOL/WARM/HOT, bar) and **fan** (spinning icon, rpm, `STOPPED`). Data: `power.totalW` and `power.budgetW`, and the PSU fan speed, which follows load. **The temperature is an estimate**: the sim does not model PSU heat (its `tempC` stays ambient), so the renderer low-pass filters `25 + 34 * load^1.5` C; the fan rpm is likewise derived from the load-driven fan speed. If the sim grows a PSU temperature, replace `psuTemp` in `live.ts` with it. No snapshot fields were added.
