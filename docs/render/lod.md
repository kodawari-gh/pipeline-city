# Level of detail

Rewritten 2026-11-16 (LOD-03) for the 3D game. The scheme has five levels: LOD0, LOD1, LOD2, the stand-in and the card. Each row below says what draws it today. The reveal bands (lids, coolers and shrouds that fade to show a chip's art) are not geometry levels; they are at the end of this file.

## The scheme

| Level | What it is | Made by | What draws it today |
|---|---|---|---|
| LOD0 | The builder's full output. | Every builder. | Every view. Build my PC keeps every part at LOD0 from 0.8 of its own view's zoom in (`LOD_CEIL`, `buildmode3d.ts`). |
| LOD1 | The same builder at a lower level: fewer sides, no feature under 2 px, still parts in one mesh. Its outline stays within 1 px of LOD0 at the room overview. | `geometry.userData.low` (`withLow`, `fitLow`, `lowOf`; `geometry.ts`, `merge.ts`). Every shape of the maxed machine with 2,000 triangles or more has one. | The overview stand-in only. |
| LOD2 | A second lower level, for 60 px and less. | The fan only (`fanPart`, `fans3d.ts`). | Nothing: at 900 p and more, no camera rest shows a fan under 60 px. |
| Stand-in | Far parts merged into a few meshes by material set. It holds each part's LOD1 where there is one, else LOD0, with the same materials. | `partlod.ts`, `standIns` (`merge.ts`), `Machine3D.partLod`. | The size classes under 8 px, and the overview class under 0.05 px per unit. |
| Card | A flat PNG rendered from High, for the far city where parallax is under 1 px. | Not built (LOD-04). | Nothing. |

- **Why.** The frame is CPU bound: draws, materials and light uniforms cost more than triangles. So a level must cut draws first. A level that saves triangles but adds draws is not used.
- **Tiers.** The quality tiers do not choose a level: every tier uses the same levels and the same switches. Low also builds coarser geometry in some builders (`lowDetail()`, `core/render/three/detail.ts`; see [three.md](three.md#levels-of-detail-and-detail-tiers)).
- **Room.** The room has no distance level. Its draw cut comes from the merges ([three.md](three.md#merges)). `mergePalette` keeps 2 m cells of space apart (`PALETTE_CELL`), so Three can still cull a merged mesh by its bounds. Low's preset has `shadows: 0` and `SHADOW_LEVELS[0]` is `null`, so no room light casts a shadow at Low; the six casters exist and are off.
- **City.** The city has distance bands, not levels ("3D distance bands (city)" below). Low cuts it further ("The city at Low").

## LOD1 and LOD2: levels from the builders

The rule (LOD-01, a delegated decision of 2026-11-16, from the study below). It replaces the old 30 % and 10 % ratios.
- **Rule.** A lower level is the same builder called with a `level`. It has fewer sides (4 to 8 px per side at its switch), no feature under 2 px, and its still parts in one mesh. The switch is on the bbox diagonal on the screen: LOD1 from 120 px down, LOD2 from 60 px down (`FAN_LOD_PX`), the stand-in under 24 px. The outline moves at most 1 px at the switch. No mesh simplifier, no hand-made model.
- **Swap.** A hold band, as the stand-ins have (`LOD_BAND`). No dither fade.
- **The outline, by test.** `LOW_TOL` (12 units, half a millimetre, 0.6 px at `LOD_OVERVIEW`) is the most a level's outline may move. `outlineOff` (`geometry.ts`) reads it from the reach in the 26 directions of a cube. A soft box, a tube and a cable choose their level by it (`fitLow`: the cheapest that fits, else none; a fat mains cord keeps all its sides). `lodreach.test.ts` holds every level of the maxed machine within 1 px at the overview (worst 15.7 units, 0.79 px, in the cooler `dev11`). `lowOf` returns a level only while it has the same attributes as its geometry (baked occlusion or a second UV set has no copy on the level) and the same bounds within 2 % of the size; `lowof.test.ts` checks both.
- **The shared builders (LOD-05).** `softBox`, `post`, `drum`, `rod` and `tube` (`geometry.ts`) give each geometry a level at no cost to a caller: for a soft box the plain box while its corner stays within `LOW_TOL`, else a chamfer (108 triangles for 300); half the sides for a round shape (a multiple of 4, 8 at least; none when it has no fewer sides to give); half the steps and half the sides for a tube.
- **The part kits (LOD-05).** The case kit's extrudes (`slab`, `wallX`, `plateY`, a thumbscrew's head) take a coarser outline (`coarse`, `casekit3d.ts`). `lathe` and `turned` carry a level. The gull-wing leads are a box, a can capacitor has half the sides, the NVMe label drape a coarser grain, a solder fillet one ring (`SEG.jointRingsLow`), and a sleeved cable (`cableMesh`) fewer steps and sides. The braided sleeve (`braidedTube`), the PSU's guard ring (`guardRing`), the heat pipe (`pipe`) and the pin coil (`pinCoil`) have their own (`partlod.low.test.ts`). Only the instanced boxes (12 triangles a copy) have none.
- **The fan (`fanPart`, `fans3d.ts`).** The only part with LOD2. Sides 32, 24, 16 for 120 mm (`fanLodSides`: all, three quarters, a half; even; 8 at least). The lower levels have a venturi of 4 and 2 steps (10 in full), one bevel step on the flanges, 6-side screw holes and a flatter hub dome. Triangles, 120 mm: 6,394, 3,452 and 2,488. Still meshes: 7, then 2 (the housing with the motor, and the light's ring, which carries state). `fanlod.test.ts` holds the outline within 1 px of the full part at 120 px and at 60 px, and the round wall within 1 px of a true circle.
- **How a level reaches the screen.** The full geometry carries its level (`geometry.userData.low`). The sibling merge carries it on to the merged mesh, and `copiesOf` puts it in the stand-in. So the near view is the same mesh for mesh (`highident.test.ts`), and the overview has no added draw.
- **Measured (maxed machine, 2026-11-16).** At full detail: 418,070 triangles in 1,387 meshes. The levels hold 78,618 triangles in 366 meshes. At the room overview (High, hour 14), the drawn triangles fell from 368,246 to 287,498 (22 % less). The PC region of the real-GPU shots before and after differs in 0.17 % of its pixels by more than 24 of 255; by eye it is the same. The build is about 25 ms slower in Node (213 to about 240 ms), because the levels are made at the build.
- **Limits.** A fan is 138 px wide at the overview's limit (0.05 px per unit), a little over the 120 px switch; its wall is then 0.6 px short of a circle. The stand-in's shadow comes from the LOD1 triangles. The levels stay in memory beside the full geometry (not measured).
- **Left.** Making the levels lazily (when the overview is first reached, as the stand-ins are) would take the build cost back. The overview's cost is now its draws more than its triangles (LOD-02).
- **Not planned (a delegated decision, 2026-11-16).** A per-part swap to LOD1 or LOD2 nearer than the overview, its dither fade, and a measure of the pop in motion. A per-part swap adds draws to save triangles, and the frame is CPU bound. LOD2 stays built and tested for the fan only. Take it up again if a GPU-bound device shows the machine's triangles in a profile.

### The study behind the rule (LOD-01, 2026-11-15, one fan)
It used a scratch override, before the fan had levels.
- **Method.** The first case fan (120 mm, 32 sides from `fanSides`) in the maxed sandbox machine, hour 14, yaw -55°, presets High and Low, real GPU (RTX 5080, ANGLE D3D11). A scratch override set the sides of every fan round to 24, 16, 12 or 8. Each count was shot 12 times, at 10 sizes: bbox diagonal 120, 90, 72, 60, 45, 36, 30, 23, 18 and 15 px. A crop on the fan was compared between two counts: share of pixels that differ by more than 12 of 255, and the peak difference. The size sweep stands in for the motion sweep: the camera moved back in steps, no video.
- **Limits.** Only the side count changed. No small feature was left out, no merged mesh, no shared material. The small sizes come from a smaller viewport (480×320, 240×160, 120×80), because the camera leaves the room at the true distance. The override and the tools were scratch only.
- **Noise floor.** Two runs of the same count: 0 to 0.1 % of the pixels on High (peak 14), 0 % on Low (peak 2). Each number below is far over it.

| 32 → 24 | 60 px | 2.8–4.0 %, 61–93 | 1.2–2.5 %, 53–98 | no facets |
| 32 → 16 | 60 px | 5.7–8.0 %, 66–84 | 3.5–7.3 %, 63–91 | no facets |
| 24 → 16 | 60 px | 4.2–6.8 %, 58–93 | 3.0–5.1 %, 63–91 | no facets |
| 24 → 12 | 60 px | 6.5–9.8 %, 76–109 | 4.9–7.9 %, 62–91 | facets just visible |

(Two values at 60 px and 30 px: the same size from two viewports.)
- **Still frames.** 16 sides at 60 px and 8 sides at 24 px show no facets, on both presets: 8 px and about 6 px per side on the throat, inside the rule's 4–8 px. 8 sides is an octagon from 60 px up; 12 sides shows from about 60 px up.
- **The swap.** A hard swap is not free. Each step changes 2 to 12 % of the fan's pixels in one frame, at each size, with peaks of 40 to 150 of 255. The share does not fall with the size, because the edge pixels move with each new set of vertices.

## The stand-in

PERF6 and LPC5 (`partlod.ts`, `standIns` in `merge.ts`, `Machine3D.partLod`). Far away, the machine's parts draw as a few merged meshes. The picture, the shadows and the state looks do not change; only the draw count falls. Hiding the small parts was tried and rejected: it removed visible parts.
- **Size classes.** By world radius: 25, 50, 100, 200 units (`LOD_RADII`). A class is far while its greatest radius is under 8 px (`LOD_PX`), at the zoom of the case's nearest point. It goes back only over 10 px (`LOD_BAND` 0.25), so a zoom at the limit switches nothing.
- **The overview class.** A fifth class holds every larger mesh (a fan frame, a card, a panel, the case's shell). It is far under 0.05 px per world unit (`LOD_OVERVIEW`), which is the room overview: the case is then under about 500 px tall. Its stand-ins hold most of the model's triangles, so they are made when the overview is first reached, not at each build (`owed`): about 90 to 140 ms in Node for the maxed machine, once. They are a second copy of the still triangles in memory (not measured).
- **Sets (`standInKey`).** A still material joins a palette set of its program, across parts. A material with state (a skin, a ring, a strip, an LED) joins only the meshes of that same material object, so the stand-in shows the state with no code. An instanced mesh joins copy by copy. A mesh with a list of materials (a RAM chip, a stick's edge) joins one set for each of its materials, with the triangles of that material's groups; if none of its materials joins another mesh, it draws as it is. Its stand-ins hold together (`settle`): when one goes back to the parts, the others go too, so the mesh never draws twice or half.
- **Anchors (`Machine3D.lodScope`).** Each fade (`fades`, `apart`), the board's small parts and the cables. The stand-ins of an anchor's content are its children, so its fade, its visibility and its motion reach them. Left out (`skip`): `keep` (the side panel's screws, a part a glTF model replaces), the side panel, the key, a card's own tree (it swings; its cooler is an anchor), a rotor, a see-through mesh. A cable's draggable end (its rig) is in while it is still; the hand hides it, and the guard builds the anchor again.
- **Swap.** A swap changes only layers. The part goes to `UNDRAWN` (`pick.ts`): no camera draws it, but the rays, `findById` and the outline still find it. Both stay `visible`, so the shadow scan sees no change and redraws no map.
- **Guard.** A part hidden after the build (a reveal nobody declared) makes its anchor build again without it. A part that moves alone must be an anchor or under `skip`.
- **Rebuild.** With the machine (`machineKey`), and the cables' anchor in `setCables` and `setCable` (a plug in or out puts a new rig in). The old geometry is freed there and in `dispose`.
- **Holds** (`heldBy`, `standInDraws`, `farClasses` `ceil`). Build my PC holds every part in full detail (`LOD_CEIL`). Its wheel's limit stays inside that at every aspect, so it holds no part by hand.
- **Off.** The last argument of `Machine3D` (`buildMachine(s, true, false)` in tests).
- **Census (maxed sandbox machine, High, `overview.test.ts`).** 514 drawn meshes near, 135 at the overview (135 draw calls), after the merges of LPC5 (`NO_RAY`, plugs and wires at every tier, a fan's hub and blades one mesh, the unseen click boxes on the undrawn layer) and MACH-06 (split multi-material meshes, still rigs in). The 135 are about 35 stand-ins, 16 rotors, 15 see-through meshes (the case glass, the LAN cable's clear jacket), 5 instanced meshes, the skipped parts (`keep`, the door, the key) and about 50 sets of one mesh: a material with a map (a label, a panel print) or a material no other mesh shares. The test holds the ceiling.
- **Browser count (room view, High, the sandbox's top machine, 2026-11-16).** The Node census reads no canvas texture, so the browser count is the truth: 216 drawn machine meshes at the overview (259 before the RGB strip became one mesh). They are 53 stand-ins, 16 rotor meshes (8 fans), 21 see-through meshes, 42 still meshes with a map of their own (labels, prints), 50 meshes with a map and state (the art sheets that glow: the board, the chipset, the VRM fins; the perforated case sheets), 23 with state and no map (LEDs, rings) and 11 still sets of one.
- **Decision (LOD-02, 2026-11-16).** The cap of 6 draws and 4 materials at the overview (LOD1, 3D_STYLE §2) is dropped. The overview shows the live machine: the fans turn, and the RGB, the LEDs and the art's glow follow its state. One impostor or one material loses that, or needs a state table and an atlas of the live art. The draw count does not limit a frame: the phone's Low room draws 242 calls in all (budget: under 500), and the look comes first. Done: the board's RGB strip is one mesh and one lamp (44 draws to 1 at every zoom, the same pixels at High and Low). Measured and not worth the code: one stand-in across the size classes (53 to 49 at High, 51 to 45 at Low) and no `uv` in the sets with no map (no change). The next levers, when the draw count limits a frame: an atlas for the 42 still meshes with a map (single images, no wrap), one material for the 8 perforated case sheets (only their texture repeat differs), and the art sheets.

## The card (planned, LOD-04)

The far city and the skyline as flat PNG cards rendered from High, plus a separate live layer of night windows. Cards only where parallax is under 1 px. The far bands (1,800–3,600 m and ∞) split into 4 or 5 depth slices. The seam hides behind a building row or in haze, with matched fog and mipmaps. ## The city at Low (`outside/`)
Only the Low tier cuts the city in the window (`isLow`, `room3d.ts`). High builds and draws the full city, and its picture does not change: `highident.test.ts` fingerprints High's city groups against `highident.expected.json` (refresh on purpose with `UPDATE_HIGH_GOLDEN=1`).
- **Swap.** `setCityDetail` (`room3d.ts`) replaces the window's city when the tier changes. One city is alive at a time: the old one is disposed, the new one built.
- **Far rings.** `towers.ts` keeps the two near rings of buildings (`LOW_NEAR_RINGS`) and merges the rest into one mesh. In that mesh a building within 600 m of the window (`LOW_WHOLE`, a ring's edge) is whole, as High's: the window looks down on its parapet, its roof plant and its signs. Past it a building is a plain block (`block`). A block's crown lights, tip beacon and sign boards are High's own: `Mass.dry` runs the roof plant's random draws with no vertex, so the crown's draws stay in step. Decision (2026-11-16, CITY-04): 600 m, not 900 m. Low draws 295k of High's 823k triangles (36 %; 22 % before); whole buildings to 900 m gave 410k (50 %). A triangle is 312 B of geometry, so 600 m costs about 36 MB more than before, and 900 m about 72 MB; past 600 m a roof is a few Low pixels. The streets are cut by 96 m cells (`LOW_CELL`, `detail.ts`). Small street furniture (drains, tree pits) is built only within 150 m of the window (`LOW_FURNITURE_M`, `street.ts`). Trees keep at least the `Coarse` detail, one mesh pair per ring (`trees.ts`).
- **Facade.** `lowfacade.ts` replaces the per-pixel facade shader with a Lambert. Its day map is baked once (AO in it). At night every wall's rooms are High's room model, window by window on High's grid (`lowRibLight`, CITY-01): near, in the atlas's pane (its glass mask); from 150 to 300 m (`LOW_FAR`), in High's own window (`lowRibGlass`). The frame round a lit window catches its light, the street lamps wash the walls' foot, and a tower with a head light floods its walls from that height, all as High's. A far block (`block`, `towers.ts`) keeps its floodlights, and its top is a roof (a facade top read as a lit shop strip). A window's details go to their means by the pixel's share of a bay or a storey times `LOW_FW` (0.5): Low draws at 0.75 of the screen and High at twice it, so unscaled a far tower went flat at a third of High's range. There is no night map. A band's wall (`STYLES.band`) is High's ribbon on High's grid (the facade's own uv): a beige frame, each storey a touch different, dark glass with slim mullions, curtains or blinds by High's hash of each window, a reveal's shade, the shop windows on the ground floor. At night its rooms are High's room model (`lowRibLight`: the same habits, shares, gather noise, zone switches, lamp colours and shops), with a home's lamp shade in depth, the frame round a lit window catching its light, and the walls across mirrored in the glass. So a band lights the same rooms at both tiers. Its tones are matched to High's shots (day frame about 155,150,144, glass 60,72,80; a lit room about 75,57,39). By day a far pane (past `LOW_FAR`) is its building's tint times `LOW_FAR_GLASS` (the dark room behind), and over it the horizon it mirrors through High's mirror tint (grey on plain glass, the tint on a coated one, white at a grazing look) times `LOW_FAR_MIRROR` (CITY-02). Under the roof line across the street the pane mirrors the walls there, or the road, not the sky (High's own lines, `lowHit`, CITY-04): the left mid-rise at hour 14 reads grey-green (93,111,120; High 97,113,119; before 112,132,142). A sign's face is High's glyph board (`lowGlyph`): its paint by day (the small sign is dark orange, not a flat bright slab), its tubes or its lit box at night (`lowSign`), and its mean far off. Where a bay or a storey is under a few pixels, the far window mask is its mean (Low has no supersampling, so a storey line would alias). So the far skyline reads pale teal, blue-grey and grey tower by tower, as High's. Pinned shots at hour 14 (crops of the spire, the right tower, the mid cluster): High (106,124,134) / (110,128,135) / (117,136,145), Low before (84,99,106) / (85,102,108) / (109,125,132), Low after (102,123,133) / (105,127,137) / (111,134,145); the skyline's luma spread 10.0 High, 16.6 before, 9.3 after. A thin thing (mast, LED line) grows to one window pixel at Low, as High's supersampled one (`view.ts` sets `fPixel` to half a window pixel at Low); a lit beacon grows to two, or the upscaled picture (render scale 0.75) dulls its red. The eight lamps are an analytic term on the walls (`LOW_LAMPS`, reach 45 m); the PointLights stay for the street and the cars.
  - Night target (decision, 2026-11-16, CITY-01): Low matches High's night at the same framing, not base `03fe0bc` Low's warm cast. Base Low's warm mid crop (61.7 %) came from its near office glass, which had no mirror: High's teal mirror there gives 38.5 %. Uniformity comes first. Warm % `(R > B+5)` on the 485×997 shot at hour 23, settled framing (std x 0–300, y 130–330 / mid x 0–300, y 330–600): High 34.8 / 34.4, Low before 57.8 / 41.0, Low after 34.6 / 41.4 (lit share of the std crop 8.7 % both). Tower means match High to 3 levels.
- **Sky.** `skynoise.ts` bakes the cloud and moon-sea noise once into a periodic 256 px map. `skyMaterial(true)` reads it, so the sky runs no live fbm loop.
- **Still steam and sway.** The steam and the leaves hold at `STILL_T` (0 s, `view.ts`). The sky, the facade's beacons and the haze keep the clock.
- **Fleet.** Half the cars drive (`LOW_FLEET` 0.5). A car is its full model within 100 m (`NEAR_LOW`) and the far box beyond. None is drawn past 350 m (`CLIP_LOW`), except on the window's avenue, which is kept out to 1000 m (`CLIP_AVENUE_LOW`, `cars.ts`). Within 300 m (`FULL_LOW`) every moving car drives. So does every car in a lane of the window's avenue (`ownAvenue`), out to its clip: the near block hides that avenue to about 350 m, so the lanes the window shows are past `FULL_LOW` (CITY-04; the count crop showed 1 near-lane car before, and now the same cars as a full fleet). A street that crosses the avenue keeps its share. Elsewhere a car drives when its key (`share(lane, place)`, fixed for the car) is under the share, so a car does not blink as others come and go. The car paint has no clearcoat (`carMaterial(…, false)`): matt.
- **Cadence.** The city is drawn each `LOW_EVERY` (6) frames of a still eye at Low, `EVERY` (3) at High (`view.ts`, `detail.ts`).
- **Isolation.** `?citycuts=a,b` keeps only the named cuts (`cadence`, `far`, `streets`, `furniture`, `trees`, `steam`, `fleet`, `palette`, `facade`, `sky`, `clock`; `facade` the lambert facade, `sky` the baked sky noise, `clock` the frozen steam and leaves), to measure what each costs. Without the query all cuts apply at Low, on every browser. High has none. `npm run bench:modes -- --query "citycuts=far"` and `npm run shot -- --query …` take the query; both take `--firefox`.
- **Firefox (CITY-05, 2026-11-16).** Firefox Low had no cut by default: at `7a64e36` its idle ran slower with the cuts than without. On the current code it does not, so the gate is gone and Firefox Low draws the same city as Chrome Low (shots: no shader error, the same picture). Measured: `bench:modes --firefox --device razr-fold-folded --presets low --modes game-max --views idle,zoom`, 3 interleaved rounds, on the dev desktop (RTX 5080, Firefox not throttled), not on the official box. Idle fps: no cut 69.3 / 66.7 / 69.9, all cuts 68.6 / 69.4 / 68.1, main `03fe0bc` 59.9 / 63.6 / 60.9. Zoom fps: no cut 102.5 / 101.4 / 96.3, all cuts 105.0 / 99.9 / 104.4, main 97.6 / 103.5 / 94.9. No cut is slower than its reference by more than the noise (about 3 fps). Chrome's path did not change.

In that sitting Firefox Low (then with no cut) had idle 59.0 vs main 62.0 (ranges do not overlap) and zoom about flat (103.9 vs 104.2); the official box has not run Firefox Low with the cuts yet (BENCH-02). ## 3D distance bands (city)

The city has no LOD tiers. Distance changes detail or what is generated. Only the generation limits remove an object. Rings and tree levels only change detail. Code at `03fe0bc` (`main`):

| Thing | Band (m) | Constant (file) | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Building rings | 150 / 300 / 600 / 900 / 1800 / 3600 / ∞ | `RINGS` (`outside/towers.ts:527`) | one geometry and one draw per ring; the detail does not step with the ring |
| Roof clutter, balconies | 900 | `CLUTTER` (`towers.ts:32`) | built only inside the band |
| Fine clutter variant | 420 | `FINE` (`towers.ts:32`) | a flag on the clutter inside the 900 band |
| Trees | 160 / 250 / 500 | `trees.ts:57-58` | four builds: Sprayed, Round, Coarse, Sparse; never dropped by distance |
| Moving and parked cars | 260 | `NEAR` (`cars.ts:19`) | full model inside; a box outside |
| Street detail | 520 (elliptical, `z × 0.7`) | `DETAIL` (`plan.ts:197`) | nothing placed beyond it |
| Signs | 1,500 / 3,400 | `SIGN_FAR`, `SIGN_TOWER` (`towers.ts:29`) | low-rise signs within 1,500; tower signs within 3,400 |

- The draft's ring set is 150 / 300 / 900 / 1800 / 3600 m. The building rings match it, plus one extra 600 step. The tree, car, street, fine-clutter and sign bands are off-ring. Snapping them needs one measurement origin first.
- Distance origin differs by system. Buildings, trees and street use the window (`av0`). Moving cars use the eye (`cars.ts:422`). Parked cars use the window (`cars.ts:326`).
- At Low, rings past 300 m merge, and cars use 100 / 300 / 350 m and `CLIP_AVENUE_LOW` 1000 m. See [The city at Low](#the-city-at-low-outside) (code at `384106a`).
- Culling, merges and the occlusion gate are in [three.md](three.md#city-culling-and-the-firefox-occlusion-gate).

## Reveal bands (`core/render/lod/lod.ts`)

Not a geometry level. As the camera closes on a part, a lid, a cooler or a shroud fades away to show the chip's live art, and the art adds its layers (names, counters, wires). The 3D board reads the bands each frame: `boardlive3d.ts` sets the live art's zoom and view (`setZoom`, `setView`); `machine3d.ts` (`reveal`, `rail`), `chips3d.ts`, `cpu3d.ts` and `card3d.ts` fade their parts by them.
- **`DEVICE_LOD`.** The one table of bands, in the zoom the camera gives at the part (`viewOn`): die, cooler, gpu, npu, tpm, disk, ram, labels. `BASE_LOD` is the ladder they share (lid fade 0.4 to 0.6, names 0.8, counters 1.5, hover 0.45). A layer that turns on at `z0` fades in over `z0` to `z0 * 1.15` (`onBand`, `TEXT_BAND`) and is hidden below `SHOWN` (0.01).
- **`deviceLod`, `seenZoom`.** Where a part is: on screen (the view plus 10 %) and its focus (1 at the view centre, 0 by 0.8 of the half-diagonal). A part reads the bands at `seenZoom(zoom, focus)` = `zoom * (0.35 + 0.65 * focus)`: only the zoom and the distance from the view centre move it along the bands, never its size.
- **`TierBudget`.** At most 2 cards or disks show their counters at once, the ones nearest the centre; a holder keeps its slot unless a challenger leads by 0.15 (`live.ts`, `chips3d.ts`).
- **`zoomSlow`.** The visual clock slows from the game speed below zoom 0.3 to one cycle a second from zoom 1 (paused stays paused). The HUD shows "Slow motion" while it is under 0.97 of the chosen speed (`zoomSlowdown`, `board3d.ts`).
- **A new chip view.** Put its bands in `DEVICE_LOD`, fade its layers with `bandAlpha(zoom, onBand(z0))`, and key its redraws on the on/off flags, not on the raw zoom. A card view also gives `candidates(view)` and a `frame(..., lod?: CardLod)`, as `gpuview.ts` does (`cardCandidates`, `cardText` in `lodcard.ts`). Tests: `lod.test.ts`, `lodcard.test.ts`.
