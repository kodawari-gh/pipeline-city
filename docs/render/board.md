# Rendering: board art, routing, tiers and VRM

Part of the renderer docs; the hub is [`RENDER.md`](../RENDER.md). Code: `src/render/board/` (`routes.ts`, `pins.ts`, `match.ts`, `boardlayout.ts`, `boardArt.ts`, `boardParts.ts`).

## Files

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `boardlayout.ts` | the geometry of each board tier (pure data): a form factor each (mini-ITX, micro-ATX, ATX, E-ATX), only the sockets the tier has |
| `pins.ts` | the **pin map** (pure geometry): the legs of a chip, the pad bar of a DIMM, the pad rows of a PCIe slot, the contacts of an M.2 and a SATA port, the pins of the 8-pin and the 24-pin, a header's pins, the rear I/O connectors, the pins of the CPU package (`pkgPads`). The art of a part and the router read the same functions, so a trace always ends on a pin that is drawn |
| `match.ts` | **length matching** (pure geometry): `serpentine` makes every lane of a bundle as long as the longest (see Routing) |
| `boardParts.ts` | drawers for the board's components (VRM phases, DIMM socket, PCIe slot, connectors, chips, capacitors, chokes, the mounting hole with its screw, the rear I/O connectors) |
| `routes.ts` | orderly routing: bundles of parallel lanes (0/45/90 degrees) that **land on pins** (`land`, `bundleOn`: a bundle changes its lane pitch only on a 45 degree leg right before its pads), every board bus of the current tier (one memory bundle per DIMM in a staircase, the VRM rails, the 8-pin comb), the hops, the PSU and video cables with their plugs |
| `boardArt.ts` | static motherboard (plate, dim bus traces, VRMs, connectors, rear I/O, sockets, a little quiet decoration) |

## Routing

`routes.ts`: a route is a centre line of horizontal, vertical and 45 degree segments; `bundle(centre, n, spacing)` copies it n times at a fixed spacing (miter offset, 90 degree corners chamfered to 45). Nothing is random, nothing is routed through a component, and no two buses cross (a bus may pass under a slot body, as traces do on a real board). `buildBuses()` builds the buses of the current tier from `GEO`:

| Bus | From to | Lit by |
|---|---|---|
| `mem` (a 6-lane bundle per DIMM, staircase) | a pin of the CPU package's east edge to the pad bar at the data end of each socket (no latch there) | RAM traffic (installed DIMMs only) |
| `nvme` (3 lanes per M.2) | CPU south edge (or east edge for the M.2 level with the CPU) to the middle contacts of the M.2 socket | program load |
| `dmi` (6 lanes, a pair each) | CPU south pins straight down onto the chipset's north legs | request start/finish, display writes |
| `usb` | four lanes CPU west edge to the hub controller's east legs, then two lanes per port from its west legs, fanning (all the same way) to the pads behind each USB-A | never |
| `sata` (3 lanes per port) | a run of the chipset's east legs, then a 45 degree fan to the pads on each port's west edge, straight along the port's row | drive traffic |
| `pcie` (pairs) | 12 lanes from every second pin of the CPU's south row straight down onto the x16 slot's north pads (both 40 apart: no fan); four lanes per further slot from the chipset's west legs, west under the slots, then up onto the slot's south pads (the slots end one after the other, so no rise crosses a slot) | never (GPU is decor) |
| `video`, `dp` (pairs) | CPU west pins, one 45 degree climb to the connector's row, into the pads behind the HDMI (DP: a second ramp below it) | display text changing (`dp` and `svid` never) |
| `svid` | the regulator controller's south legs to the socket's west (or north) edge | never |
| `pwrcpu` | a comb: the 8-pin's four pins drop onto the VRM input rail, which runs along every phase | CPU current |
| `vcore` | each VRM phase's output stub down into the package edge | CPU current |
| `pwr12` | straight west from the 24-pin's top pin (level with the memory regulator's rail) | 55% of the total draw |
| `vdimm` | the memory regulator along the DIMM sockets (a drop into the pad beside each far-end latch) | RAM current |
| `audio`, `lan` | codec legs to the three jacks, PHY legs to the RJ45, straight | never (decoration) |
| `pwmc` | a **lane per phase** from each regulator controller (the CPU's stands west of the phases, the memory one west of its phases, east on the top board): the nearest phase's lane runs straight in, the others drop to a level of their own under the phases and rise in the gap before their phase (they dive under the phases' power stubs) | never |
| `spi`, `lpc`, `hdr`, `rtc`, `fanc` | BIOS flash to the chipset's west legs; the I/O controller (SIO, west of the chipset) straight east to the chipset's last west legs (`lpc`) and, along a trunk under the header row, to every pin header; the CR2032 (under the chipset) up to the chipset's south legs (the RTC) and sideways to the vertical clear-CMOS jumper beside it; the I/O hub's south legs to the CPU_FAN header, and each regulator controller's legs to its heatsink-fan header (`VRM_FAN`: a vertical row west of the CPU controller; `RAM_FAN`: above the memory controller) (`fanc`) | never |
| `clk` | the reference crystals: the CPU's (24 MHz, under its socket: two lanes up onto pins of its south row) and the chipset's (25 MHz, above its north legs: two lanes down onto the first two legs) | never |
| `pwrpcie` | the PCIe 6-pin's 12 V rail: west from the connector (under the sockets, diving under the lanes it meets) to the tab at the x16 slot's latch end | never (no GPU) |
| `hda`, `phyl` | the audio codec's and the Ethernet PHY's east legs down to the I/O hub's north legs (`I/O HUB`, the former USB hub: it takes the PHY's four lanes on its left four legs, the codec's on its right four), diving under the display lanes they cross | never |
| `dbg`, `rgb` | the top board: four lanes from the chipset's south legs down onto the debug display, and the ARGB header's data pin to the RGB strip along the bottom edge | never |

Every lane ends **exactly** on a pin the part's art draws (`boardlayout.test.ts` checks each bus against `pins.ts`), a bend is 45 or 90 degrees, no lane turns back on itself, and no two signal lanes of different buses cross, **except by diving under** (below). The pads are drawn last, over the parts, so each trace visibly meets its pin. The lanes that leave the CPU start on **pins of its package** (the outer row of the three the package draws, 20 apart: `pkgPads`; each pin serves one lane, a bundle whose pitch differs opens or closes on a short 45 degree fan under the package), the empty socket draws the same contact rows.

**Length matching** (`match.ts`, `Bus.groups`, tested: every group of lanes that must arrive together is within 1.5 world units). A bundle that turns is shorter on the inside of the turn by `(n - 1) * pitch * 2 * tan(turn / 2)` however the corner is drawn, and the pad fans add more, so `serpentine` makes up the difference the way a board does: on the longest straight run all the lanes share, the bundle **opens out** toward the side of its shortest lane (a 45 degree flare, which already gives that lane some length back), every lane runs its own **row of square teeth** (as many as it needs, the shortest lane the most, each tooth as high as the room the flare opened; the teeth all lean the same way, so no two lanes touch) and the bundle closes again. It is applied to every memory bundle (the staircase's runs are `MEM_STEP` apart so each bundle has a free band to open into), every NVMe and DMI bundle, the chipset's lanes to each slot, the USB uplink, display, and the codec and PHY links. The x16 lanes need none (their pins and pads are both 40 apart).

**Diving under** (`HOP_BUSES`, `Lane.hops`, `crossings`): the decorative buses `pwmc`, `hda`, `phyl`, `lpc`, `rgb` may cross other traces; where they do, the lane dives under (a via ring at each end, nothing drawn between; `visibleParts` is what is drawn). The test allows a crossing only when one of the two lanes dives under there. **Pairs**: the fast serial buses (`pcie`, `nvme`, `dmi`, `video`, `dp`) are drawn as differential pairs, two thin strands per lane (`Bus.pair`).

Cables have three states (`syncCables` in `board.ts`, a layer of its own so plugging never rebuilds the baked scene): **connected** (routed, as below), **loose** (`looseCable` in `routes.ts`: a short stub from the PSU socket / monitor port ending in a plug that lies at rest, the PSU plugs in the gutter east of the board, the HDMI plug just outside the rear I/O; no routed cable, no glow) and **held** (in the player's hand: `interact.ts` draws a rubber-band cable from the fixed end to the pointer). The source part must be installed for the cable to exist at all.

PSU cables (`buildCables`): sleeved, braided look, rounded corners, plug at both ends. They leave the PSU's top-edge sockets and rise through the cable gutter east of the case's board area. The 24-pin and the PCIe cable turn west into plugs on the east-edge connectors; the 8-pin keeps rising to the top of the case, runs west over the board and drops onto the connector on the top edge (its plug points up, the cable leaves upward). ATX 24-pin and CPU 8-pin exist whenever a PSU is installed; the PCIe 6-pin only when a device occupies a `pcie*` slot (none exists today). The HDMI cable (thick, plugs on both ends) goes from the rear I/O HDMI port west into the gap between the case and the monitor (`VIDEO_TRUNK_X`), down it, east-to-west under the monitor (`VIDEO_RUN_Y`, above the shelf unit's plank) and up behind the monitor: its video cable is **built in**, so there is no socket on the monitor and no plug on that end: the cable simply comes out from behind the bezel's bottom edge (`VIDEO_SOCKET` is that point on the edge; the route ends `CABLE_HIDE` below it so its round end tucks under the edge). Only the board end (the HDMI port on the rear I/O) is a plug the player moves. Unplugged, the cable still comes out of the monitor and its loose plug lies in the gap by the case wall (`looseCable`; `rubberBand` starts downward from the monitor).

## Board tiers, cases and the VRM

**Four boards, four form factors** (`boardlayout.ts`, `TIER_GEO`; the CPU socket is the origin on every one and the rear I/O is on the west edge):

| Tier | Form | Board (w x h) | Sockets drawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Starter | mini-ITX | 5800 x 3900 | 1 DIMM, 1 PCIe, 2 SATA, no M.2, no ethernet jack |
| 1 Basic | micro-ATX | 5800 x 4700 | 2 DIMM, 2 PCIe, 3 SATA, ethernet |
| 2 Mainstream | ATX | 6800 x 5400 | 4 DIMM, 3 PCIe, 2 M.2, 4 SATA |
| 3 Enthusiast | E-ATX | 6800 x 6700 | 8 DIMM (two rows), 4 PCIe, 3 M.2, 8 SATA |

The **tray** carries a brass standoff at every mounting hole of every form factor the case takes (`standoffs()`: the union of the holes of boards up to the case's tier), and every board's holes carry a screw (washer, pan head, cross slot), so a small board in a big case sits on its own studs and the unused ones show beyond its edge.

A socket a tier lacks is **not drawn** (no ghost sockets). `src/render/board/boardlayout.test.ts` pins the geometry to the sim's slot and phase counts, keeps parts on the board and off each other, checks the memory bundles never cross, that every bus ends on a footprint and every plug sits on its connector. **The layout is global state**: `setLayoutTier(tier, caseTier)` (called first in `build()` and, saved and restored, by `thumbs.ts`) reassigns the live bindings (`BD`, `BR`, `PSU`, `CASE`, `WORLD`, ...). Never copy one into a module-level constant.

**How the copper is laid** (every run ends on a footprint, with pads drawn at the lane ends):
- **Memory** is point-to-point: one 6-lane bundle per DIMM from the CPU's east edge to the pad row of its socket, stacked in a staircase (the nearest DIMM takes the band nearest its pads) so nothing crosses. Row 0 pads face down, row 1 (tier 3) pads face up. Lit per installed DIMM by RAM traffic.
- **CPU power**: the 8-pin sits on the top edge (mouth up, the cable comes over the top of the board from the gutter), feeds an input rail (`pwrcpu`) along the top of the phases; each phase runs power stage, choke and output capacitors into a copper pour under the CPU's north edge (`vcore` rays, lit by CPU current).
- **Cell and fans**: the battery sits under the chipset's east half (`cellTop`, `cellSide`: its RTC lanes and the jumper's), the cooler's cable (`cpuFanCableArt`, drawn while a cooler is installed) runs from the CPU_FAN header, south of the I/O hub, east to the cooler. The 6-pin's rail (`pwrpcie`) feeds the x16 slot's tab; every other slot, and the x16 slot on a board without the 6-pin, ties into the board's plane (`slotTie`: a tab, a stub and a patch of copper stitched with vias), every M.2 has a 3V3 patch in its housing's corner (`m2Tie`). A heatsink with a fan has a cable (`sinkFanCableArt`, while it is installed) from its regulator's fan header, over the regulator, to the fan's housing. A pair's skew-tuning bump (`tuneBump`): one strand of each differential pair (alternating) steps out and back on the last stretch before its pad.
- **Memory power**: the 24-pin feeds the memory regulator's rail (`pwr12`, level with the 24-pin's top pin: `dRailY`, over every phase's input stub), whose outputs run along the pins of the DIMM sockets (`vdimm`: the upper run starts at the first socket's drop and ends past the last phase's output (`vdimmStart`, `vdimmEnd`), a drop into every socket, round the east end to a second rail under row 1), lit by RAM current. The rails are tested to line up: every phase output and every socket drop lands on a rail, the 12 V pin is on the input rail.
- The package's south edge feeds the columns, west to east: PCIe x16 (into the first slot), DMI (six lanes straight down onto the chipset's north legs, which are centred under it: `TierGeo.dmiX`) and one NVMe column per M.2 that lies below the CPU (`nvmeX0`, `NVME_DX`; the lowest M.2 in the westmost column, so none crosses another; an M.2 level with the CPU is fed straight from its east edge). The chipset sits south-east of the CPU, east of where the slots end; its SATA lanes leave a run of its east legs (three per port) and fan out on 45 degree legs (all ending at the same x) to the ports' rows, then run straight east to the ports (stacked pairs share a housing). **No signal trace crosses a PCIe slot, a DIMM, the M.2 or a chip it does not end on** (`boardlayout.test.ts` checks it). USB runs from the CPU's west edge to the hub controller and, two lanes per port, on to the rear I/O; video goes west and up one 45 degree ramp to the HDMI pads. Audio and ethernet PHY traces are decoration (`audio`, `lan`: never lit).
- **Mounting holes**: drawn last, each with a clear ring of bare board, so no trim or silkscreen line runs through a screw; the test keeps every part and every lane out of their keepout. The trim lines are drawn first, under every part, and the bottom silkscreen sits above them.
- **VRM**: a PWM controller, then the tier's phases (`vrmPhase(i)`, centred over the CPU; 2, 3, 4 and 6), each a power stage, a choke, two capacitors and decoupling. **Memory VRM** (2, 2, 3, 4 phases) above the DIMM bank. Glow, power-good light and crash flash are as before (`Live.drawVrm`).
- **Heatsinks** (`vrmSinkArt`) cover the power-stage strip (`VRM_SINK`, `RAM_SINK`).
- **Tier skin** (`SKINS` in `boardArt.ts`): PCB colour (Starter grey-green, Basic blue, Mainstream teal, Enthusiast violet), trim lines, PCH heat sink style, PCIe armour from tier 2.

**Cases** are purchasable (docs/BOARD.md "Cases"): `snapshot.caseTier` picks the case, `snapshot.boardTier` the board in it. The case is as big as the biggest board it holds (`CASE_BD`): the motherboard tray (perforated, cable cut-outs), the cable gutter and the edge-on **front fans** follow the case; a smaller board sits in the top-left of a bigger case. The PSU shroud, the drive bay, the case walls, the power button and `WORLD` move with it, and the camera glides to the new overview when it changes. Looks per case (`CASE_LOOK`): name on the top panel, vent count, an accent line on the big ones. A case item is dropped on the case itself (`freeSlots ["case"]`, like `["board"]` for a board); the case is the world id `case`.
- SATA ports are on the board's east edge (mouth east); each drive cable runs east, under the PSU cables, then down the lane between the PSU and the bay (`SATA_LINK.col`) into its cell. The two cables never cross each other; both pass under the three PSU cables in the gutter (drawn beneath them).

**Scale.** The monitor is drawn at its design size (3300 x 1995) and scaled by `MONITOR_K` (3.0) around `MONITOR`; its screen text and OS screen live in a container in design units (`SCREEN_D`) scaled the same way (`Live.scr`). The router and the wall plate are drawn at design size around the shelf corner and scaled by `NET_K` (1.45); the helpers in `net.ts` (`routerRect`, `lanX`, `wanX`, `sockLed`) give the scaled world positions.

**Pictures of parts** (`thumbs.ts`): `thumbOf(id)` returns a cached data URL or null while it is being made (one part per idle slice); `thumbsVersion` is a signal that bumps when one arrives or the theme changes. The art is the real thing: `cpuArt`, `ramArt` (on its side), `coolerArt` (cooler.ts; `coolerKitArt` adds the AIO radiator and tubes), `psuBoxArt`, `monitorArt`, `driveArt` plus its live parts, `vrmSinkArt`, `nicArt`, `routerArt`, `wallArt` (the plan plate), the whole board (`buildBoard`) and the case (`exteriorArt`), on a soft backdrop. `PartArt` shows the picture and falls back to its drawn icon (an OS, tests without a renderer).
