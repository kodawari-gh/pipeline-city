# Rendering: board art, routing, tiers and VRM

Part of the renderer docs; the hub is [`RENDER.md`](../RENDER.md). Code: `src/features/board/render/` (`routes.ts`, `pins.ts`, `match.ts`, `boardlayout.ts`, `boardArt.ts`, `boardParts.ts`).

## Files

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `boardlayout.ts` | the geometry of each board tier (pure data): a form factor each (mini-ITX, micro-ATX, ATX, E-ATX), only the sockets the tier has |
| `pins.ts` | the **pin map** (pure geometry): the legs of a chip, the pad bar of a DIMM, the pad rows of a PCIe slot, the contacts of an M.2 and a SATA port, the pins of the 8-pin and the 24-pin, a header's pins, the rear I/O connectors, the pins of the CPU package (`pkgPads`). The art of a part and the router read the same functions, so a trace always ends on a pin that is drawn |
| `match.ts` | **length matching** (pure geometry): `serpentine` makes every lane of a bundle as long as the longest (see Routing) |
| `boardParts.ts` | drawers for the board's components (VRM phases, DIMM socket, PCIe slot, connectors, chips, capacitors, chokes, the mounting hole with its screw, the rear I/O connectors) |
| `routes.ts` | orderly routing: bundles of parallel lanes (0/45/90 degrees) that **land on pins** (`land`, `bundleOn`: a bundle changes its lane pitch only on a 45 degree leg right before its pads), every board bus of the current tier (one straight memory bundle per DIMM, the VRM rails, the 8-pin comb), the hops, the PSU and video cables with their plugs |
| `boardArt.ts` | static motherboard (plate, ground plane, thin traces under the mask, VRMs, connectors, rear I/O, sockets, a little quiet decoration); with `far`, the picture for the far textures |
| `stitch.ts` | the ground stitching (pure geometry): small vias on a grid through the free plane, off the parts, the holes and every lane on any layer |

## Routing

`routes.ts`: a route is a centre line of horizontal, vertical and 45 degree segments; `bundle(centre, n, spacing)` copies it n times at a fixed spacing (miter offset, 90 degree corners chamfered to 45). Nothing is random, nothing is routed through a component, and no two buses cross (a bus may pass under a slot body, as traces do on a real board). `buildBuses()` builds the buses of the current tier from `GEO`:

| Bus | From to | Lit by |
|---|---|---|
| `mem` (a lane per stick in each of 6 rows, 4 on the eight-slot board) | pins of the CPU package's east edge, fanned out along the sticks, zigzags before the bank, to the pin column on its socket's west side; a lane dives under the sockets before its own | RAM traffic (installed DIMMs only) |
| `nvme` (3 lanes per M.2) | CPU south edge down a column of its own, then east along the M.2's row to the middle contacts of the socket | that M.2's traffic (its own lane group, straight to the CPU) |
| `dmi` (6 lanes, a pair each) | CPU south pins down, one 45 degree step east under the lowest M.2, onto the chipset's north legs | what crosses the chipset: a SATA drive, a card in a chipset slot, the BIOS at POST, the power switch, POST codes |
| `usb` | four lanes CPU west edge to the hub controller's east legs, then two lanes per port from its west legs, fanning (all the same way) to the pads behind each USB-A | the key (uplink + port 0's pair only); board ethernet (uplink only) |
| `sata` (3 lanes per port) | a run of the chipset's east legs, then a 45 degree fan to the pads on each port's west edge, straight along the port's row | that port's drive (then DMI) |
| `pcie` (pairs, `pcieBus`) | a slot's real pairs (`pcieLinks`: two a lane, so 32 on a x16 connector), half landing on each side of the slot (`slotLanding`). The CPU feeds the x8 and wider slots from its south pins: the first slot's band runs on the surface, a slot further down dives at the CPU (an inner pin row) and comes up in staggered vias above it. The pairs for the far side dive just short of the slot and come up beyond it. A x4 slot's lanes leave the chipset's west legs and run west onto it. The send pairs carry a row of coupling capacitors | that slot's card (host to card / card to host; a chipset slot's messages also cross DMI; `Bus.heads` picks the slot), a NIC's transfers |
| `video`, `dp` (pairs) | CPU west pins, one 45 degree climb to the connector's row, into the pads behind the HDMI (DP: a second ramp below it) | `video`: display text changing while the cable is on the board's HDMI (`dp` never) |
| `svid` | the regulator controller's south legs to the socket's west (or north) edge | the CPU asking for a voltage (power-on, a clock or throttle change) |
| `pwrcpu` | a comb: the 8-pin's four pins drop onto the VRM input rail, which runs along every phase | CPU current |
| `vcore` | each VRM phase's output stub down into the package edge | CPU current |
| `pwr12` | straight west from the 24-pin's top pin (level with the memory regulator's rail) | 55% of the total draw |
| `vdimm` | the memory regulator's rail along the sockets' north ends (a drop into the pad beside each north latch) | RAM current |
| `audio`, `lan` | codec legs to the three jacks, PHY legs to the RJ45, straight | `lan`: board ethernet transfers; `audio` never (decoration) |
| `pwmc` | a **lane per phase** from each regulator controller (the CPU's stands west of the phases, the memory one west of its phases on the Starter board and east of them on the others): the nearest phase's lane runs straight in, the others drop to a level of their own under the phases and rise in the gap before their phase (they dive under the phases' power stubs) | the CPU regulator's controller answering a voltage request (the first `pwmc`; the memory one never) |
| `spi`, `lpc`, `hdr`, `rtc`, `fanc` | BIOS flash to the chipset's west legs; the I/O controller (SIO, west of the chipset) straight east to the chipset's last west legs (`lpc`) and, along a trunk under the header row, to every pin header; the CR2032 (under the chipset) up to the chipset's south legs (the RTC) and sideways to the vertical clear-CMOS jumper beside it; the I/O hub's south legs to the CPU_FAN header, and each regulator controller's legs to its heatsink-fan header (`VRM_FAN`: a vertical row west of the CPU controller; `RAM_FAN`: above the memory controller) (`fanc`) | `spi`: the BIOS read at POST; `lpc` and `hdr`: the power switch at power-on (the `hdr` trunk and the F_PANEL tap only: `Bus.heads`, `{ header }` pick in `busmap.ts`), and the TPM's accesses and its POST ping (the trunk and the TPM header's tap); `fanc` (the CPU_FAN lead only): POST asking the cooler; `rtc` never |
| `clk` | the reference crystals: the CPU's (24 MHz, under its socket: two lanes up onto pins of its south row) and the chipset's (25 MHz, above its north legs, east of the DMI lanes: two lanes down onto the last two legs) | never (steady) |
| `pwrpcie` | the slot supply's 12 V rail: from the 24-pin's lowest 12 V pin west to the strip beside the connector, down it and west (under the sockets, diving under the lanes it meets) to the tab at the x16 slot's latch end (lane 0). Further lanes branch off it at a trunk at x 1100, just west of the M.2 housings: one per further slot (west along the gap above its card, then down into the top of its power patch) and one per M.2 socket (into its patch), in that order. The cards' aux power does not come this way: the PSU's leads plug into the cards | the watts of the cards in the slots (with the PSU's PCIe cable) |
| `hda`, `phyl` | the audio codec's and the Ethernet PHY's east legs down to the I/O hub's north legs (`I/O HUB`, the former USB hub: it takes the PHY's four lanes on its left four legs, the codec's on its right four), diving under the display lanes they cross | `phyl`: board ethernet transfers; `hda` never |
| `dbg`, `rgb` | the top board: four lanes from the chipset's south legs down onto the debug display, and the ARGB header's data pin to the RGB strip along the bottom edge | `dbg`: a POST step; `rgb` never |

`board/busmap.ts` (`BUS_ROLE`, the chains) is the single place that says which of these carry messages, which glow with current and which stay dark; the rules are in `render/live.md` "Activity".

Every lane ends **exactly** on a pin the part's art draws (`boardlayout.test.ts` checks each bus against `pins.ts`), a bend is 45 or 90 degrees, no lane turns back on itself, and no two signal lanes of different buses cross, **except by diving under** (below). The pads are drawn last, over the parts, so each trace visibly meets its pin. The lanes that leave the CPU start on **pins of its package** (the outer row of the three the package draws, 20 apart: `pkgPads`; each pin serves one lane, a bundle whose pitch differs opens or closes on a short 45 degree fan under the package), the empty socket draws the same contact rows.

**Length matching** (`match.ts`, `Bus.groups`, tested: every group of lanes that must arrive together is within 1.5 world units). A bundle that turns is shorter on the inside of the turn by `(n - 1) * pitch * 2 * tan(turn / 2)` however the corner is drawn, and the pad fans add more, so `serpentine` makes up the difference the way a board does: on the longest straight run all the lanes share, the bundle **opens out** toward the side of its shortest lane (a 45 degree flare, which already gives that lane some length back), every lane runs its own **row of square teeth** (as many as it needs, the shortest lane the most, each tooth as high as the room the flare opened; the teeth all lean the same way, so no two lanes touch) and the bundle closes again. It is applied to every NVMe and DMI bundle, the chipset's lanes to each slot, the USB uplink, display, and the codec and PHY links. The x16 lanes need none (their pins and pads are both 40 apart). Memory is matched on its own (`memBus`): all its lanes form one group, so a lane to a nearer stick makes up the distance to the farthest in a row of teeth (`toothFor`, `toothPts`) that ends just short of the bank.

**Diving under** (`HOP_BUSES`, `Lane.hops`, `crossings`): the decorative buses `pwmc`, `hda`, `phyl`, `lpc`, `rgb` may cross other traces; where they do, the lane dives under (a via ring at each end, nothing drawn between; `visibleParts` is what is drawn). `mem` and `pcie` set their own hops: they run on an inner layer under the sockets and slots, a packet goes dark there (`surfaced`). The test allows a crossing only when one of the two lanes dives under there. **Pairs**: the fast serial buses (`pcie`, `nvme`, `dmi`, `video`, `dp`) are drawn as differential pairs, two thin strands per lane (`Bus.pair`).

Cables have three states (`syncCables` in `board.ts`, a layer of its own so plugging never rebuilds the baked scene): **connected** (routed, as below), **loose** (`looseCable` in `routes.ts`: a short stub from the PSU socket / monitor port ending in a plug that lies at rest, the PSU plugs in the gutter east of the board, the HDMI plug just outside the rear I/O; no routed cable, no glow) and **held** (in the player's hand: `interact.ts` draws a rubber-band cable from the fixed end to the pointer). The source part must be installed for the cable to exist at all.

**Cable look and physics** (`src/core/render/cable.ts`; every cable uses it, a test keeps the sleeve colours out of other files). One painter (`paintCable`: shadow, sleeve, accent tint, braid on the fat PSU leads, lit streak; `wall` adds the pale halo over the dark wall for the mains cords, the ISP, ethernet and video cables). A cable that is not routed along the board hangs from its fixed end: a stiff stub, then a catenary of fixed length (`hangCable`, used by `rubberBand`, the loose PSU leads, the loose fan cords, the loose wall cords and the loose ethernet). A loose PSU lead lies on the roof of the PSU: its plug stands on its pin face, the plugs side by side in `LOOSE_ORDER`, the cable clamped to the roof (`floor`). The ethernet plug hangs from the router. A fan cord or wall cord plug that the player lets go away from its header falls, swings on its cord and lies at rest (`stepFall`, ticked by `board.ts`; the PSU, video and ethernet plugs still snap to rest). The cables over the wall (ethernet, ISP lead, video) are draped (`drape`): every corner is a round bend, a clip (`paintClip`, at `runClipYs`) pins each straight run to the wall, a level run sags a little between clips, and the video cable lies along the desk's back edge. The ethernet trunk (`LAN_TRUNK_X`) is west of the video trunk, so the two cross only where the ethernet reaches a jack. Cable management is visual only: ties round each lead at the heights in `gutterTieYs` and at long runs (`paintTie`, `tiePoints`), clip bases at the same heights in the cable duct up the gutter (`cableDuct` in `case.ts`), ties on the SATA data and power cables.

PSU cables (`buildCables`): sleeved, braided look, rounded corners, plug at both ends. They leave the PSU's top-edge sockets and rise through the cable gutter east of the case's board area. The 24-pin cable turns west into its plug on the east-edge connector; the 8-pin keeps rising to the top of the case, runs west over the board and drops onto the connector on the top edge (its plug points up, the cable leaves upward). ATX 24-pin and CPU 8-pin exist whenever a PSU is installed; each PCIe lead (`pcie`..`pcie4`, one cable per `cardLeads` of a card, dealt out by `assignLeads` in slot order) runs up the gutter, west over the board at `top - 150 - 110 * plug` above its card and drops onto the card's power socket (`cardPlugs`, a plug block on the card's north edge; the card's second socket one step higher so the leads do not cross). The PSU's sockets (`P1`..`P4`, `PSU_PORTS.pcie`, as many as `pcieLeads`) are dealt out innermost to the lowest run first. A lead the cards do not take is not drawn; a card on slot power (or the Starter board, which has no such card) has no socket. The HDMI cable (thick, plugs on both ends) goes from the rear I/O HDMI port west into the gap between the case and the monitor (`VIDEO_TRUNK_X`), down it, east-to-west under the monitor (`VIDEO_RUN_Y`, above the shelf unit's plank) and up behind the monitor: its video cable is **built in**, so there is no socket on the monitor and no plug on that end: the cable simply comes out from behind the bezel's bottom edge (`VIDEO_SOCKET` is that point on the edge; the route ends `CABLE_HIDE` below it so its round end tucks under the edge). Only the board end (the HDMI port on the rear I/O) is a plug the player moves. Unplugged, the cable still comes out of the monitor and its loose plug lies in the gap by the case wall (`looseCable`; `rubberBand` starts downward from the monitor).

## Board tiers, cases and the VRM

**Four boards, four form factors** (`boardlayout.ts`, `TIER_GEO`; the CPU socket is the origin on every one and the rear I/O is on the west edge):

| Tier | Form | Board (w x h) | Sockets drawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Starter | mini-ITX | 4700 x 4000 | 1 DIMM, 1 PCIe, 2 SATA, no M.2, no ethernet jack |
| 1 Basic | micro-ATX | 5000 x 4800 | 2 DIMM, 2 PCIe, 3 SATA, ethernet |
| 2 Mainstream | ATX | 6000 x 6060 | 4 DIMM, 3 PCIe, 2 M.2, 4 SATA |
| 3 Enthusiast | E-ATX | 6800 x 6700 | 8 DIMM (one bank), 4 PCIe, 3 M.2, 8 SATA |

The proportions are a real board's, about 22 units a millimetre: the CPU package is 1260 x 1060 (the die is drawn at `CPU_S` of its floorplan), the socket stands about a third of the way in from the rear I/O (`IO_X`), and a DIMM is a real stick's length (2400).

The **tray** carries a brass standoff at every mounting hole of every form factor the case takes (`standoffs()`: the union of the holes of boards up to the case's tier), and every board's holes carry a screw (washer, pan head, cross slot), so a small board in a big case sits on its own studs and the unused ones show beyond its edge.

A socket a tier lacks is **not drawn** (no ghost sockets). `src/features/board/render/layout/boardlayout.test.ts` pins the geometry to the sim's slot and phase counts, keeps parts on the board and off each other, checks the memory bundles never cross, that every bus ends on a footprint and every plug sits on its connector. **The layout is global state**: `setLayoutTier(tier, caseTier)` (called first in `build()` and, saved and restored, by `thumbart.ts`) reassigns the live bindings (`BD`, `BR`, `PSU`, `CASE`, `WORLD`, ...). Never copy one into a module-level constant.

**How the copper looks.** Copper under the solder mask is the mask a shade off, never metal (`Palette.trace`, `pour`, `tent`; a black mask gets the strongest shade, a pale one goes darker, so it always shows). Bare metal is only what a real board leaves bare: pads and tabs (`copper`, `via`). The ground plane covers the board; each trace runs thin in a clear moat through it, and small vias stitch the free plane (`stitchVias`). A thin trace is lost at far zoom, so the far textures are a second build (`buildBoard(..., far)`: the 2D far bake, the coarsest 3D level, the shop's picture): each lane is a wider, softer band of the same tone, without the moats and the stitching. The slots' 12 V rail (`pwrpcie`) runs on an inner layer: nothing on the surface and no glow, only the power lens shows it.

**How the copper is laid** (every run ends on a footprint, with pads drawn at the lane ends):
- **Memory** is point-to-point, in rows like a real board's byte lanes: each stick has a lane in every row (`memLanes`: 6 rows, 4 on the eight-slot board). The rows stack on the CPU's east edge (`TierGeo.memFrom`) and fan out at 45 degrees along the sticks (`memLand`), so a stick takes its pins along its length and nothing crosses (`memBus`). All the lanes are one length: the nearer sticks' lanes carry the zigzags. A lane to a stick behind others dives at the bank and comes up at its own socket (`Lane.hops`: an inner layer, nothing drawn, and a packet goes dark there, `surfaced` in `busflow.ts`). Lit per installed DIMM by RAM traffic.
- **CPU power**: the 8-pin sits on the top edge (mouth up, the cable comes over the top of the board from the gutter), feeds an input rail (`pwrcpu`) along the top of the phases; each phase runs power stage, choke and output capacitors into a copper pour under the CPU's north edge (`vcore` rays, lit by CPU current).
- **Cell and fans**: the battery sits under the chipset's east half (`cellTop`, `cellSide`: its RTC lanes and the jumper's), the fans' cables are `FanCordsView` (`fancords.ts`, drag handling in "Fan cables" of `docs/render/interact.md`): the cooler's runs from the CPU_FAN header east to the cooler, a heatsink fan's from its regulator's header, over the regulator, to the fan's housing, and each ends in a plug that seats on its header (or lies loose beside it, tilted). The 6-pin's rail (`pwrpcie`) feeds the x16 slot's tab; every other slot, and the x16 slot on a board without the 6-pin, ties into the board's plane (`slotTie`: a tab, a stub and a patch of copper stitched with vias), every M.2 has a 3V3 patch in its housing's corner (`m2Tie`). A pair's skew-tuning bump (`tuneBump`): one strand of each differential pair (alternating) steps out and back on the last stretch before its pad.
- **Memory power**: the 24-pin feeds the memory regulator's rail (`pwr12`, level with the 24-pin's top pin: `dRailY`, over every phase's input stub), whose outputs run along the sockets' north ends (`vdimm`: the rail starts at the first socket's drop and ends past the last phase's output (`vdimmStart`, `vdimmEnd`), a drop into every socket), lit by RAM current. The bank stands far enough under the board's top edge (`Spec.bank.top`) that the 24-pin, level with that rail, clears the corner's mounting hole. The rails are tested to line up: every phase output and every socket drop lands on a rail, the 12 V pin is on the input rail.
- The package's south edge feeds the columns, west to east: PCIe (a band of 32 pairs into the first slot; the other CPU slots' lanes leave its inner pin rows and run on an inner layer), DMI (six lanes down, then one 45 degree step east under the lowest M.2 onto the chipset's north legs: `TierGeo.dmiX`) and one NVMe column per M.2 (`nvmeX`, `NVME_DX`; the lowest M.2 in the westmost column, so none crosses another; its serpentine runs on the M.2's row, where the bundle has room to open). The chipset sits south-east of the CPU, east of where the slots end; its SATA lanes leave a run of its east legs (three per port) and fan out on 45 degree legs (all ending at the same x) to the ports' rows, then run straight east to the ports (stacked pairs share a housing). **No signal trace crosses a PCIe slot, a DIMM, the M.2 or a chip it does not end on** (`boardlayout.test.ts` checks it). USB runs from the CPU's west edge to the hub controller and, two lanes per port, on to the rear I/O; video goes west and up one 45 degree ramp to the HDMI pads. Audio and ethernet PHY traces are decoration (`audio`, `lan`: never lit).
- **Mounting holes**: drawn last, each with a clear ring of bare board, so no trim or silkscreen line runs through a screw; the test keeps every part and every lane out of their keepout. The trim lines are drawn first, under every part, and the bottom silkscreen sits above them.
- **VRM**: a PWM controller, then the tier's phases (`vrmPhase(i)`, centred over the CPU; 2, 3, 4 and 6; the top board's six stand `vdx` east and `vdy` north of that, so their runs still funnel onto the smaller package at 45 degrees), each a power stage, a choke, two capacitors and decoupling. **Memory VRM** (2, 2, 3, 4 phases) above the DIMM bank. Glow, power-good light and crash flash are as before (`Live.drawVrm`).
- **Heatsinks** (`vrmSinkArt`) cover the power-stage strip (`VRM_SINK`, `RAM_SINK`).
- **Tier skin** (`SKINS` in `boardArt.ts`): the real-market ladder (Starter green, Basic blue, Mainstream matte black, Enthusiast gloss black): solder mask, silk, slots, capacitors, trim lines, PCH heat sink style, PCIe armour from tier 2.

**Cases** are purchasable (src/features/board/BOARD.md "Cases"): `snapshot.caseTier` picks the case, `snapshot.boardTier` the board in it. The case is as big as the biggest board it holds (`CASE_BD`): the motherboard tray (perforated, cable cut-outs), the cable gutter and the edge-on **front fans** follow the case; a smaller board sits in the top-left of a bigger case. The PSU shroud, the drive bay, the case walls, the power button and `WORLD` move with it, and the camera glides to the new overview when it changes. Looks per case (`CASE_LOOK`, `CASE_KIND`): name on the top panel, vent count, and a different outside and inside for each of the four (see render/case.md "Four real cases"). A case item is dropped on the case itself (`freeSlots ["case"]`, like `["board"]` for a board); the case is the world id `case`.
- SATA ports are on the board's east edge (mouth east); each drive cable runs east, under the PSU cables, then down the lane between the PSU and the bay (`SATA_LINK.col`) into its cell. The two cables never cross each other; both pass under the three PSU cables in the gutter (drawn beneath them).

**Scale.** The monitor is drawn at its design size (3300 x 1995) and scaled by `MONITOR_K` (3.0) around `MONITOR`; its screen text and OS screen live in a container in design units (`SCREEN_D`) scaled the same way (`Live.scr`). The router and the wall plate are drawn at design size around the shelf corner and scaled by `NET_K` (1.45); the helpers in `net.ts` (`routerRect`, `lanX`, `wanX`, `sockLed`) give the scaled world positions.

**Pictures of parts** (`thumbs.ts`): `thumbOf(id)` returns a cached image URL or null while it is being made; `thumbsVersion` is a signal that bumps when one arrives or the theme changes. A worker draws them (`thumbs.worker.ts`: its own Pixi renderer on an OffscreenCanvas, `thumbs.env.ts` sets Pixi's worker adapter first) and posts back a PNG, so a picture never holds a frame; the drawing itself is `thumbart.ts`. The theme is passed in with each job. Without OffscreenCanvas WebGL the main thread draws them, one per idle slice, with the renderer given to `initThumbs`. The art is the real thing: `cpuArt`, `ramArt` (on its side), `coolerArt` (cooler.ts; `coolerKitArt` adds the AIO's tubes and a radiator, `radiatorKitArt` is a radiator part alone), `psuBoxArt`, `monitorArt`, `driveArt` plus its live parts, `vrmSinkArt`, `nicArt`, `routerArt`, `wallArt` (the plan plate), the whole board (`buildBoard`) and the case (`exteriorArt`), on a soft backdrop. `PartArt` shows the picture and falls back to its drawn icon (an OS, tests without a renderer).
