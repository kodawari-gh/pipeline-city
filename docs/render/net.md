# Rendering: network props

Part of the renderer docs; the hub is [`RENDER.md`](../RENDER.md). Code: `src/features/network/render/net.ts`, `src/features/network/render/netvis.ts`, `src/features/network/render/lens/` (the network lens). The network itself: [`NETWORK.md`](../NETWORK.md).

## Files

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `net.ts` | the network props: router, wall plate, ISP line, ethernet cable, NIC card art, link LEDs and traffic pulses (see Network) |
| `jackleds.ts` | the Ethernet jacks' lights, pure: `LinkLeds` turns the link's state and the frames the port carries into the levels of the link and activity lights (see LED meanings) |
| `lens/shape.ts` | the network lens, pure: the anchors (plate, router, LAN lead, OS drive), the lines (`netShape`), the numbers and tags of one snapshot (`netDraw`), `speedLevel` (see The network lens) |
| `lens/flows.ts` | the network lens: the lines as one instanced mesh of strips that face the camera, and the comet shader |
| `lens/view.ts` | the network lens: `NetView3D` (veil, lines, tags), hosted by `Lens3D` in the board feature |

## Network (router, wall plate, ethernet cable, NIC cards)

Code: `src/features/network/render/net.ts` (the 2D `NetView` was removed; the router and NIC helpers remain), geometry in `layout.ts` (`ROUTER_SPOT`, `WALL_PLATE`, `LAN_TRUNK_X`, `nicJack`, `nicRect`), the plug drag in `src/features/board/render/three/drag3d.ts`. Data: `snapshot.net`, `cables[id=lan]`, `devices[kind=nic]`, inventory kinds `router` and `nic`. Nothing here changes the overview fit: the router shelf and the wall plate sit above the monitor, inside the existing `WORLD` box (the case did not shrink).

**Ids** (hover, `selected`, `highlight`, `flyTo`, `anchorOf`, `hasTarget`; all of them are external: hit and visible at far zoom, they never open the interior):

| id | what |
|---|---|
| `router` | the installed router, or the empty marked spot (dashed `ROUTER`) when none is; it is also the drop slot `router` (`scene.slots`) of an inventory router (`freeSlots ["router"]`; a better router is a `swapSlots` target and is amber) |
| `wall` | the wall plate: plan speed (`50 Mb` .. `20 Gb`), plan name, five tier pips, the jack and its light |
| `cable.lan` | the ethernet plug (rest spot near the router, or seated in a jack); the cable body also hits. As a highlight it rings the plug and, while loose, every valid jack |
| `eth0` | the board's own jack (only boards with `ethernetMbps > 0`; port `"board"` in `connectCable`) |
| `eth1`, `eth2`, ... | NIC jacks in PCIe slot order (the lowest slot is `eth1`); the port to pass to `connectCable("lan", port)` is the card's device id (equal to the snapshot's `net.port.deviceId` once plugged) |
| device id (`dev9`...), `pcie0`..`pcie3` | the NIC card (hit rect = card plus bracket and jack) and its slot; the slot is the drop target of a `nic` item (free and swap slots from the inventory) |

The group is drawn only when it matters: a router or a plan exists, a router is in the inventory, or the cable is plugged. Before that the world is unchanged (calm early game).

**The router** (3D, `src/features/board/render/three/router3d.ts`, `routerModel(tier)`): one model for the gallery's showcase and its stills, the Build my PC studio's wall and the room's shelf, so a router reads the same in all three. Four tiers (`net.router.visual`): `router-home` (a soft-touch black box, two aerials, a row of four lights), `router-gbe` (a satin front over graphite, stouter aerials, vents, six lights), `router-10g` (dark anodised aluminium, no aerials, a lit bar, fins, eight lights), `router-core` (a steel 1U rack unit: ears, an SFP cage over each jack, two rows of lights). Every tier has the same five real-size RJ45 jacks on its front (`routerJack(k)`: LAN 1 east to LAN 4, the WAN west, a legend printed under them), so a cord's plug seats in the same place on every tier. relay makes it (`MAKER.router`, docs/brands/relay.md): its lockup is debossed in the top. In the room the lead's cord goes into a jack on the router's east side (`routerLan()`), the patch lead into the WAN jack. NIC cards (`nic-gbe`, `nic-10g`, `nic-40g`): PCB with a controller chip, gold fingers on the slot, a bracket and an ethernet jack with two LED wells on the board's west edge, a finned heatsink on 10G and a bigger one with a heat pipe and a second sink on 40G. NICs get the generic heat glow. A NIC does not draw the PCIe 6-pin cable (the slot feeds it).

**Cable flow.** The plug of the `lan` cable rests near the router (a short stub from the router's LAN socket). Drag it (a press that moves more than 6 px): a rubber-band cable follows the pointer, every jack pulses (valid) or is dimmed (a burnt card), the hovered valid jack glows and the plug snaps into it. A drop on a jack calls `connectCable("lan", "board" | cardId)`; this works while the PC is on (no "power off first" message, unlike the other cables). Dragging the seated plug out calls `disconnectCable("lan")` at once and puts the plug in the hand; dropping it anywhere but on a jack leaves it at rest. A drop elsewhere on the board says `Drop the plug on an ethernet jack`; a refusal from the sim (`lastError`) shows as the usual inline message and clears the optimistic plug state (about 1.5 s, as for the other cables). The router drag uses the normal part drag with one change: a router installs while the PC is on (it has its own power), so the "Power the machine off first" refusal does not apply to it. Routed cable: down from the router's plug, east along the row above the monitor, down the trunk `LAN_TRUNK_X` (between the video plug and the case wall), west into the jack across the case wall, with rounded bends. It never meets the PSU cables or the ISP line. **Known crossing:** the trunk to a NIC jack (below the HDMI height) crosses the video cable's sleeve once at a right angle (drawn in front of it), and a key plugged into the USB port is drawn over it; the onboard jack's run is above the video cable and crosses nothing. The cable is external, so it stays visible in the far-zoom exterior view; the jack LEDs are on the exterior rear plate too (`exterior.ts` draws the NIC jack cut-outs).

**LED meanings** (the Ethernet jacks' lights: `src/features/network/render/jackleds.ts`, shared by every jack that has them: the board's rear jack, a card's jack, the router's side jack and WAN jack in the room, its first LAN jack and WAN jack on the studio's wall). Each jack has two lights, a green link light and an amber activity light. Nothing is random, fixed or set by hand: a light shows the state of the line or the frames the port carries.

| light | meaning |
|---|---|
| link (green): the PC's jack and the router's LAN jack | lit while the cable is in a port that works, a router stands and the PC is on (a PHY links on the cable alone: no OS and no plan needed); dark with no cable, the PC off, a tripped supply or a burnt port |
| link (green): the router's WAN jack | lit while a router stands and a plan is on the line, also with the PC off |
| activity (amber): the same three jacks | follows the frames the PC's port carries. A frame lights it for `STRETCH` (30 ms), as a PHY's pulse stretcher does so a lone frame is seen: frames further apart blink one by one, frames that follow closer keep it lit. No traffic, no light. It runs on the game's clock and holds while the game is paused |
| the router's front row of lights | the router's power, not an activity light: lit with a plan on the line, dim without |
| a jack with no cable (the router's LAN 2 to 4, a card's second jack) | dark |

The frames come from the sim: `NetSnap.frames` (`rx`, `tx`: cumulative, not saved) counts the frames of the bytes that move on the uplink port (`NET.frameBytes` = 1500 bytes a data frame, an acknowledgement frame the other way for every `NET.ackEvery` = 2nd one; a download is data in, acks out, an upload the other way). `LinkLeds.step(snapshot, dt)` reads the counter at each new base tick (a second of the link at 1x), turns the growth into a rate (frames a tick) and holds it until the next tick; a snapshot that skips ticks gives the mean rate over them. The board's game scene (`board3d.ts`) and the Build my PC stage (`buildmode3d.ts`) each own one `LinkLeds`, step it with the visual clock (`dt * worldPace(speed)`) and hand the levels to `Machine3D.frame`, the room (`RoomFrame.net`) and the studio wall (`StudioWall.lights`). The gallery's stills and the shop have no game: their jacks are dark (`routerModel(tier, { drive: [] })` builds no lamp for them).

**Traffic pulses.** Only while transfers exist and the link is up: dots travel the lan cable and the ISP line, downloads towards the PC (cyan), uploads towards the router (violet), at a speed and density that follow `net.bandwidth` of that direction (log scale, 10 Mbit to 40 Gbit); the amount eases in and out on the game clock (`gdt`), so a pause freezes them. No transfers or no power: nothing moves.

**Tray and USB fallback** (`trayArt(T, keys, mode)`, mode from `net.usbFallback.state`): `off` as before; `retired` dims the rack and says `LINK UP: KEYS RETIRED` (or `LINK UP: n LEFTOVER`; leftover keys stay fully visible and usable); `waiting` says `NET DOWN` in amber; `fallback` says `NET DOWN: STICKS BACK AT 1/3 RATE` and the panel is normal again. The countdown text is the UI's job (the tray is rebuilt only when the state changes, not every second).

**Where things are.** The router stands on a shelf above the monitor (`SHELF_Y` = 700 above the monitor's top, so it follows the desk; its east end is 300 west of the monitor's, `ROUTER_EAST`). The ISP's wall plate (`WALL_PLATE`, jack at its bottom edge `WALL_Y`) is **under the desk, level with the power socket plate and just west of it** (`POWER_PLATE`). The ISP lead runs from the jack down a little (`ISP_ROW_Y`), along, and **up behind the desk, the monitor** to the router's WAN socket, like the monitor's power cord: its body is drawn behind the desk and the monitor, so only the stretch under the desk, the stretch between the desk and the monitor's underside and the stretch above the monitor show. The 2D layer that did this (`NetView.behind`, `RoomView.behind`) was removed; the 3D draw is not described here. The ethernet cable is as before (down the trunk `LAN_TRUNK_X` in the gap, now a longer run).

**LOD.** The router, wall plate, cable and jack LEDs are external: the far-zoom view keeps them. The 2D layer that held them (`NetView.root`) and its far-zoom hit rule were removed with the 2D board.

**Verified before the 2D board was removed** (headless Chrome, software GL, sandbox saves, real mouse events through `beginPartDrag` and the canvas; 1360x800 dark and light, 1920x1080 light): a router dragged from the inventory onto its spot installs (and is refused on a PCIe slot, a NIC on the router spot); all four router looks, the wall plate with plans 1 to 4; NIC cards `nic1`, `nic2`, `nic3` dropped on PCIe slots; the plug dragged onto `eth0` and onto a NIC jack, pulled out again (disconnects, plug back at rest) and re-plugged, all while the PC was on or off; power on, OS up, link up, downloads with pulses, the retired tray look; power off (router power and WAN lights stay, jack LEDs go dark, the tray says `NET DOWN`); two screenshots of a paused frame are byte-identical and differ again after the resume; far zoom with the router, cable and the NIC jack on the rear plate, and hover ids `router`, `wall`, `cable.lan`, `eth0` at far zoom; `anchorOf`, `hasTarget`, `flyTo` and a highlight for the new ids. Upload pulses and a `link-down` amber LED were checked with a spoofed snapshot (both directions drawn), not from a real upload; `saturated` came from a real run (spool full). Not verified: touch, the burnt-card dimming, the plugged-key-over-cable overlap. Not re-checked on the 3D board.

## The network lens

The lens shows the path data takes, in the room's own space: plan, router, LAN lead, port, OS drive. It opens with the `N` key or the dock's **Network** button (`NetLensButton` in `economy/ui/shop/Inventory.tsx`; the key is in the Controls list). It shares the screen with the power and airflow lenses: `networkLens` in `src/app/hover.ts` is a signal, and turning one lens on turns the other two off (`hover.test.ts`). It is ready when `netLensReady(s)` is true: unlock level 4, a router or plan, a router in the inventory, or the LAN lead plugged in. It is in the room game only (not in Build my PC).

**Layers.** `Lens3D` (board feature) hosts `NetView3D` next to the power and airflow views, draws it when `networkLens` is on and hides it otherwise. `NetView3D` owns the veil (`makeVeil(NET_VEIL)`: a cool blue that dims the room but lets the machine read), the lines (`NetFlows`) and the pinned words (`AirTags3D`, the same pills as the airflow lens).

**Shape and draw.** As in the power lens, what is built once is split from what changes. `anchorsOf(s, machine)` finds the places: the wall plate (`ispPlate()` in `layout.ts`, the one `room3d.ts` uses), the router (`ROUTER_SPOT`), the lead (`Machine3D.cableRuns()`, run `'lan'`: strands, seated, the jack, its radius) and the OS drive (`Machine3D.partBox`). `netShape(anchors)` makes two lines: the **plan's line** (`ispLine`: from the plate's west side along the wall to the router's back; it is a line of the lens only, there is no real cable there) and one line for each strand of the **LAN lead** (router jack to plug, lifted by the cable's radius so it sits on the cable). The shape is rebuilt when the machine or `machine.cablesRev` changes. A new snapshot only runs `netDraw(shape, s, anchors)`: for each line the down and up pace (`speedLevel` of the ladder's `downMbps` and `upMbps`, log scale 10 Mbit to 20 Gbit; 0 for a way that has no transfer), its state, and the tags.

**Look** (`flows.ts`, one draw call). Each straight stretch of a line is a strip that turns to face the camera, never finer than two pixels each side, additive, depth-tested, drawn after the room (`renderOrder` 1002). The shader finds the pulses, nothing moves on the CPU. Data coming in is a **cyan** comet that runs toward the machine; results going out are **violet** and run back; with both ways busy each has its own lane across the line, so the two colours do not add up to white. The faster the link, the faster and closer the comets. Link up with nothing moving: a faint slow trickle coming in. A line that needs setup (`no-os`): a dim thread. A lead that is out (not plugged in, with a router or a plan present) or a link that is `down`: **red**, pulsing, still. The plan's line is lit only with both a plan and a router. With neither, nothing is lit. The comets run on game time (`dt`: a pause holds them); the red pulse runs on the real clock.

**Words.** Four pills, from the same ladder as the network panel (`limiterLadder`): `Plan` over the plate, `Router` over the router (with the clients being served), `Port` at the jack, `Drive` over the OS drive. The slowest piece is amber and ends with `limit`. A missing piece says so in the warn colour (`No plan · click the plate`, `No router · drop one here`, `Port · plug the cable in`). When the state chip says warn or bad, one more pill (`link`) carries it under the router. The legend (`legend: 'net'`: a cyan dot `coming in`, a violet dot `going out`) sits under the `link` pill when there is one, else under the router. Tones: `net` (cyan), `limit` (amber), `warn`.

**Camera.** Turning the lens on flies to `netWindow()` fitted with `inFree(.., CASE_Z, ROOM_ANGLE, 0.86)`, but only when the camera is clearly higher than that fit (`goal.height > fit.height * 1.1`): a player who is already close is not moved. The view before the flight is remembered. Turning the lens off flies back (to the room overview, or to that view) only when nothing moved the camera in between (`goal` still the lens's own).

**Check tool.** In dev, `window.__net` is the `NetView3D` (`shapeNow`, `drawNow`, `anchorsNow`, `flowsNow`). Tests: `lens/shape.test.ts` (levels, shape, states, words, `netLensReady`), `src/app/hover.test.ts`.

**Limits of the first version.** The plan's line is short and a lens-only line. The lead is thin from far. There is no ring on an open jack (only the `Port` word). Nothing inside the case (PCIe to chipset to RAM and drive) yet.
