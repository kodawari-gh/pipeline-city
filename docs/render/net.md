## Network (router, wall plate, ethernet cable, NIC cards)

Code: `src/features/network/render/net.ts` (the 2D `NetView` was removed; the router and NIC helpers remain), geometry in `layout.ts` (`ROUTER_SPOT`, `WALL_PLATE`, `LAN_TRUNK_X`, `nicJack`, `nicRect`), the plug drag in `src/features/board/render/three/drag3d.ts`. Data: `snapshot.net`, `cables[id=lan]`, `devices[kind=nic]`, inventory kinds `router` and `nic`. Nothing here changes the overview fit: the router shelf and the wall plate sit above the monitor, inside the existing `WORLD` box (the case did not shrink).

**Ids** (hover, `selected`, `highlight`, `flyTo`, `anchorOf`, `hasTarget`; all of them are external: hit and visible at far zoom, they never open the interior):

| id | what |
|---|---|
| `router` | the installed router, or the empty marked spot (dashed `ROUTER`) when none is; it is also the drop slot `router` (`scene.slots`) of an inventory router (`freeSlots ["router"]`; a better router is a `swapSlots` target and is amber) |
| `wall` | the wall plate: plan speed (`50 Mb` .. `20 Gb`), plan name, five tier pips, the jack and its light |
| `cable.lan` | the ethernet plug (rest spot near the router, or seated in a jack); the cable body also hits. As a highlight it rings the plug and, while loose, every valid jack |
| `eth0` | the board's own jack (only boards with `ethernetMbps > 0`; port `"board"` in `connectCable`) |
| `eth1`, `eth2`, ... | NIC jacks in PCIe slot order (the lowest slot is `eth1`); the port to pass to `connectCable("lan", port)` is the card's device id (equal to the snapshot's `net.port.deviceId` once plugged) |
| device id (`dev9`...), `pcie0`..`pcie3` | the NIC card (hit rect = card plus bracket and jack) and its slot; the slot is the drop target of a `nic` item (free and swap slots from the inventory) |

The group is drawn only when it matters: a router or a plan exists, a router is in the inventory, or the cable is plugged. Before that the world is unchanged (calm early game).

**The router** (3D, `src/features/board/render/three/router3d.ts`, `routerModel(tier)`): one model for the gallery's showcase and its stills, the Build my PC studio's wall and the room's shelf, so a router reads the same in all three. Four tiers (`net.router.visual`): `router-home` (a soft-touch black box, two aerials, a row of four lights), `router-gbe` (a satin front over graphite, stouter aerials, vents, six lights), `router-10g` (dark anodised aluminium, no aerials, a lit bar, fins, eight lights), `router-core` (a steel 1U rack unit: ears, an SFP cage over each jack, two rows of lights). Every tier has the same five real-size RJ45 jacks on its front (`routerJack(k)`: LAN 1 east to LAN 4, the WAN west, a legend printed under them), so a cord's plug seats in the same place on every tier. relay makes it (`MAKER.router`, docs/brands/relay.md): its lockup is debossed in the top. In the room the lead's cord goes into a jack on the router's east side (`routerLan()`), the patch lead into the WAN jack. NIC cards (`nic-gbe`, `nic-10g`, `nic-40g`): PCB with a controller chip, gold fingers on the slot, a bracket and an ethernet jack with two LED wells on the board's west edge, a finned heatsink on 10G and a bigger one with a heat pipe and a second sink on 40G. NICs get the generic heat glow. A NIC does not draw the PCIe 6-pin cable (the slot feeds it).

**Cable flow.** The plug of the `lan` cable rests near the router (a short stub from the router's LAN socket). Drag it (a press that moves more than 6 px): a rubber-band cable follows the pointer, every jack pulses (valid) or is dimmed (a burnt card), the hovered valid jack glows and the plug snaps into it. A drop on a jack calls `connectCable("lan", "board" | cardId)`; this works while the PC is on (no "power off first" message, unlike the other cables). Dragging the seated plug out calls `disconnectCable("lan")` at once and puts the plug in the hand; dropping it anywhere but on a jack leaves it at rest. A drop elsewhere on the board says `Drop the plug on an ethernet jack`; a refusal from the sim (`lastError`) shows as the usual inline message and clears the optimistic plug state (about 1.5 s, as for the other cables). The router drag uses the normal part drag with one change: a router installs while the PC is on (it has its own power), so the "Power the machine off first" refusal does not apply to it. Routed cable: down from the router's plug, east along the row above the monitor, down the trunk `LAN_TRUNK_X` (between the video plug and the case wall), west into the jack across the case wall, with rounded bends. It never meets the PSU cables or the ISP line. **Known crossing:** the trunk to a NIC jack (below the HDMI height) crosses the video cable's sleeve once at a right angle (drawn in front of it), and a key plugged into the USB port is drawn over it; the onboard jack's run is above the video cable and crosses nothing. The cable is external, so it stays visible in the far-zoom exterior view; the jack LEDs are on the exterior rear plate too (`exterior.ts` draws the NIC jack cut-outs).

**LED meanings** (the Ethernet jacks' lights: `src/features/network/render/jackleds.ts`, shared by every jack that has them: the board's rear jack, a card's jack, the router's side jack and WAN jack in the room, its first LAN jack and WAN jack on the studio's wall). Each jack has two lights, a green link light and an amber activity light. Nothing is random, fixed or set by hand: a light shows the state of the line or the frames the port carries.

| light | meaning |
|---|---|
| link (green): the PC's jack and the router's LAN jack | lit while the cable is in a port that works, a router stands and the PC is on (a PHY links on the cable alone: no OS and no plan needed); dark with no cable, the PC off, a tripped supply or a burnt port |
| link (green): the router's WAN jack | lit while a router stands and a plan is on the line, also with the PC off |
| activity (amber): the same three jacks | follows the frames the PC's port carries. A frame lights it for `STRETCH` (30 ms), as a PHY's pulse stretcher does so a lone frame is seen: frames further apart blink one by one, frames that follow closer keep it lit. No traffic, no light. It runs on the game's clock and holds while the game is paused |
| the router's front row of lights | the router's power, not an activity light: lit with a plan on the line, dim without |
| a jack with no cable (the router's LAN 2 to 4, a card's second jack) | dark |

The frames come from the sim: `NetSnap.frames` (`rx`, `tx`: cumulative, not saved) counts the frames of the bytes that move on the uplink port (`NET.frameBytes` = 1500 bytes a data frame, an acknowledgement frame the other way for every `NET.ackEvery` = 2nd one; a download is data in, acks out, an upload the other way). `LinkLeds.step(snapshot, dt)` reads the counter at each new base tick (a second of the link at 1x), turns the growth into a rate (frames a tick) and holds it until the next tick; a snapshot that skips ticks gives the mean rate over them. The board's game scene (`board3d.ts`) and the Build my PC stage (`buildmode3d.ts`) each own one `LinkLeds`, step it with the visual clock (`dt * worldPace(speed)`) and hand the levels to `Machine3D.frame`, the room (`RoomFrame.net`) and the studio wall (`StudioWall.lights`). The gallery's stills and the shop have no game: their jacks are dark (`routerModel(tier, { drive: [] })` builds no lamp for them).

**Traffic pulses.** Only while transfers exist and the link is up: dots travel the lan cable and the ISP line, downloads towards the PC (cyan), uploads towards the router (violet), at a speed and density that follow `net.bandwidth` of that direction (log scale, 10 Mbit to 40 Gbit); the amount eases in and out on the game clock (`gdt`), so a pause freezes them. No transfers or no power: nothing moves.

**Tray and USB fallback** (`trayArt(T, keys, mode)`, mode from `net.usbFallback.state`): `off` as before; `retired` dims the rack and says `LINK UP: KEYS RETIRED` (or `LINK UP: n LEFTOVER`; leftover keys stay fully visible and usable); `waiting` says `NET DOWN` in amber; `fallback` says `NET DOWN: STICKS BACK AT 1/3 RATE` and the panel is normal again. The countdown text is the UI's job (the tray is rebuilt only when the state changes, not every second).

**Where things are.** The router stands on a shelf above the monitor (`SHELF_Y` = 700 above the monitor's top, so it follows the desk; its east end is 300 west of the monitor's, `ROUTER_EAST`). The ISP's wall plate (`WALL_PLATE`, jack at its bottom edge `WALL_Y`) is **under the desk, level with the power socket plate and just west of it** (`POWER_PLATE`). The ISP lead runs from the jack down a little (`ISP_ROW_Y`), along, and **up behind the desk, the monitor** to the router's WAN socket, like the monitor's power cord: its body is drawn behind the desk and the monitor, so only the stretch under the desk, the stretch between the desk and the monitor's underside and the stretch above the monitor show. The 2D layer that did this (`NetView.behind`, `RoomView.behind`) was removed; the 3D draw is not described here. The ethernet cable is as before (down the trunk `LAN_TRUNK_X` in the gap, now a longer run).

**LOD.** The router, wall plate, cable and jack LEDs are external: the far-zoom view keeps them. The 2D layer that held them (`NetView.root`) and its far-zoom hit rule were removed with the 2D board.

**Verified before the 2D board was removed** (headless Chrome, software GL, sandbox saves, real mouse events through `beginPartDrag` and the canvas; 1360x800 dark and light, 1920x1080 light): a router dragged from the inventory onto its spot installs (and is refused on a PCIe slot, a NIC on the router spot); all four router looks, the wall plate with plans 1 to 4; NIC cards `nic1`, `nic2`, `nic3` dropped on PCIe slots; the plug dragged onto `eth0` and onto a NIC jack, pulled out again (disconnects, plug back at rest) and re-plugged, all while the PC was on or off; power on, OS up, link up, downloads with pulses, the retired tray look; power off (router power and WAN lights stay, jack LEDs go dark, the tray says `NET DOWN`); two screenshots of a paused frame are byte-identical and differ again after the resume; far zoom with the router, cable and the NIC jack on the rear plate, and hover ids `router`, `wall`, `cable.lan`, `eth0` at far zoom; `anchorOf`, `hasTarget`, `flyTo` and a highlight for the new ids. Upload pulses and a `link-down` amber LED were checked with a spoofed snapshot (both directions drawn), not from a real upload; `saturated` came from a real run (spool full). Not verified: touch, the burnt-card dimming, the plugged-key-over-cable overlap. Not re-checked on the 3D board.

## Inside the NIC and the router

Code: `src/features/network/render/plan/` (shared), `nic/` (the card's die), `router/` (the router), the real parts in `src/features/board/render/three/` (`nic3d.ts`, `nicchip3d.ts`, `phychip3d.ts`, `card3d.ts`), the words in `src/features/network/ui/nicdesc.ts` and `routerdesc.ts`. The detailed views are built as the motherboard is: real 3D parts on a PCB, and a floorplan only on a chip's die, under its lid, as the CPU's (`docs/DIE.md`). Zoom in on a network card: its bracket and solder side fade, it swings down flat to the south as a GPU does, and its parts stand up on the PCB: the jacks, the controller, the crystal, the flash, the regulator, the MAC label. Zoom in on the controller: its lid fades and the floorplan of the die shows. Double-click the card or a part to fly to it. The router has its own view: see **The router** below the card's.

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `plan/netplan.ts` | `layPlan`: a tree of blocks placed edge to edge and wires routed through the gutters (`cpufloor`), cached by key. The card's plan uses it |
| `plan/netmeter.ts` | `FrameMeter` (frames a tick, from the sim's counters), `lineFrames`, `busy`, `loadOf` and the number formatters |
| `nic/nicplan.ts` | the die's plan: `NIC_LINK` (chip, standard, lanes, jacks, queue ring), `NIC_DIE` (680 by 680 plan units) and `nicPlan(visual)` |
| `nic/niclayout.ts` | the card's flat strip on the board (`NIC_FACE`, `nicFaceRect`: 150 units north of its slot's line and 450 south), the die's frame (`nicGeo`: a band of `NIC_BAND` for each slot), the hover parts (`nicParts`, `nicIds`, `NIC_BODIES`) |
| `nic/nicglyph.ts` | the symbol of each die block (`nicGlyph`), what lights now (`nicLive`), the geometry both share |
| `nic/nicroute.ts` | the way a frame goes: `nicRoute(plan, "rx" or "tx")` |
| `nic/nicart.ts` | the die's still art: `nicDie(T, visual, slot)` |
| `nic/nicshow.ts` | `NicShow`: what the view shows each frame, read off the snapshot |
| `nic/nicview.ts` | `NicView`: the live Pixi layer on the die (glow, lit blocks, numbers, packets), one die for each slot |
| `nic/nicmac.ts` | the card's MAC address (the 3D label prints the same) |
| `board/render/three/nic3d.ts` | the card's parts as real ones, on the PCB's top face (the chip face, which looks north while the card stands): jacks, controller, crystal, flash, regulator, MAC label. The jacks stand on that face at the bracket, with the PCB below them (`nicBracket` cuts their openings in the plate). `nicParts` returns the jack lamps, the controller's chip and the footprints of the tagged parts |
| `board/render/three/nicchip3d.ts` | the controller's package (a QFN on the gigabit card, a lidded BGA on the others), its die with the floorplan on it and the lid that fades off it (`NicChip`). The die fills the chip: `DIE_MM` (7.6 mm on the QFN's 8 mm pad, 21 mm on the BGA's 25 mm substrate) |
| `board/render/three/nicflow.ts` | the card's lit copper, pure: `nicLanes(copper, slot)` gives the lanes frames run on (the jack's pairs, the slot's receive pairs) in the drawing's own coordinates, in the card's band (`TRACE_BAND`) |
| `board/render/three/nictrace.ts` | `NicTraceView`: those lanes run by the board's own `BusFlow` (`wiring/busflow.ts`), launched by the die's view (`NicView.onLaunch`) |
| `board/render/three/card3d.ts` | the card's pose: standing with its parts and jacks on the PCB's north face, then swinging down flat to the south as a GPU does (no `roll`, no flip), so it lies component side up with its fingers at its own slot and its bracket west. `SLOT_BAY` and `bracketSpan` give the plate's span of each slot: 355 units north of the slot's line, 325 south |

**The die.** The floorplan is the controller's die, square (680 plan units), in the CPU die's style. Top to bottom, the way a frame goes: the PHY (a front end, a signal processor and a coding stage on each pair, and a speed gauge) beside the MAC (the Ethernet frame as its fields, a lane for the frame received and one for the frame sent) over the statistics (the port's counters); the receive queue beside the transmit queue (a ring of slots and a row for each transfer in flight); the DMA engine (the descriptor ring and the two ways to RAM), the PCIe core (a lane is two pairs) and the heat sensor. The wires run through the gutters: `phy-mac mac-rxq mac-txq rxq-dma txq-dma dma-pcie`, then `stats-mac temp-dma`. The jack, its magnetics, the flash, the crystal and the regulator are not on the die: they are 3D parts of the card. The three cards differ in what they carry: chip, standard (`1000BASE-T`, `10GBASE-T`, `40GBASE-T`), lanes (x1, x4, x8), jacks and ring size (`NIC_LINK`; the 3D card is built from the same numbers: `NIC_LANES`, `jacksOf`).

**The copper.** The card's traces light as the motherboard's do. The card's copper is routed for real (`niccopper.ts`), so the runs frames travel on are known: on the component side, the first jack's four pairs run east to the controller, and the slot's receive pairs (`PET`) run from the gold fingers to it. `nicLanes` turns those runs into `Lane`s (the jack's contacts and the fingers' middles are their starts) and `NicTraceView` runs them with the board's own `BusFlow` (a short bright head and a trail that fades; nothing is lit between frames). The runs are in a drawing of their own: the card's layout as it is built (x east, y down, the card seen from its component side, which is how it lies flat), in a band of `TRACE_BAND` for each slot. `Chips3D.traces` puts the drawing on a plane just over the mask's face (kind `nictrace`, 2 units up, under every part, so a part hides what runs under it), its light added to the mask (additive blending, as `BoardLive3D`). It is drawn at 30 a second at most, only while a packet is on the copper, and fades in over `DEVICE_LOD.nic.parts` once the card lies flat. A frame is one trip: `NicView` decides when a frame leaves (the same real counters and pace as the die's packets, see **What is real**), calls `onLaunch`, and the copper answers with the time it takes before the die. A frame from the cable runs the jack's pairs (cyan, as the die's receive packets), then goes through the die; one the host sends runs the slot's pairs (violet), goes through the die, then runs the jack's pairs out. The way back to the host (side A's `PER` pairs, through their AC-coupling capacitors) is on the solder side, and a frame dives to it at the controller: no light shows on this face for it. Only the first jack has a cable, so a second jack's pairs stay dark. The launches run whether the die is seen or not, so the copper lights from the zoom where the card lies flat, before the lid has gone.

**What is real.** Nothing is random, fixed or set by hand. The jack's two lamps are real lamps on the model: the link lamp is lit while the cable is in this card and `linkOf` says the PHY has a partner (the jacks' law, `jackleds.ts`); the activity lamp follows the frames (`actLevel`, the same `STRETCH`). On the die the frames a tick come from `NetSnap.frames` through a `FrameMeter` (the view owns one; the jacks have their own). The lanes light with how busy the port is (`busy`, a log scale: a faint light for a few frames, full at line rate) and no frames means no light and no packets; the load bars show the real share of the line rate (`lineFrames(mbps)`). The queues show `net.transfers` (the job and how far it is, up to three rows, `uploadsWaiting` next to the transmit queue); the PHY shows the speed the port has (a hot card runs at half: `DeviceSnap.throttle`) and the gauge shows it against the card's rating; the heat sensor shows `tempC` on a scale to the damage point with the throttle and unstable marks. The regulator, the flash and the crystal show no number on the model: their hover words carry the card's live numbers (`DeviceSnap.powerW` between the spec's idle and load watts; the MAC address, which the flash holds and the label prints: `nicmac.ts`). A card without the cable shows a dark line and its counters at 0 (the card's own port counters are `net.frames`, which count the one uplink port). The second jack of the 40G card is a model only, never lit (the game has one cable).

**Pose and zoom.** The card stands in its slot with its parts on the PCB's top face, the chip face, as the product photos show a card: the controller, the passives and the jacks stand on the board, and the PCB is below the jacks. That face looks north while the card stands, and the jacks' mouths pass through the back wall in openings of the bracket's plate (`SLOT_BAY` in `card3d.ts`, `bay` in `casedetail3d.ts`). Over `DEVICE_LOD.nic.fade` (0.4 to 0.6, seen zoom) its bracket and its solder side fade and it swings down flat, to the south, as a GPU does (`hinge.rotation.x`, the same for every card): the swing ends with its component side to the glass, its fingers at the top edge on its own slot's line and its bracket west. No flip and no `roll`: the card never shows its back. The flat PCB reaches 1320 units south of its line (over the slots after it, as a GPU's does) and the jacks stand 13.5 mm up its face; a GPU's or an NPU's cooler in the slot north of it starts east of the jacks (`COOLER_FROM` in `niccopper.ts`), so the two never meet (`fit.test.ts`). `Card3D.flat()` says how far it has got. Over `DEVICE_LOD.nic.parts` (0.6 to 0.78) the parts' live layers fade in. The die's frame ends upright (x east, y north) and facing the glass, so the floorplan reads as drawn. The controller's lid (`NicChip.lid`: the QFN's mould cap, or the BGA's nickel lid with its sink) fades over the die band (`DEVICE_LOD.die.fade` times `NicChip.scale`, which turns a plan unit into layout units); the live layer (`NicView` in `Chips3D`, kind `nic`, one die for each slot) runs on the die at 30 draws a second at most and only while the lid is gone. Far away the parts draw as stand-ins: they are an anchor of their own (`Machine3D.still`), as the card's tree is left out of the distance level of detail.

**Ids.** `pcieN.nic-<part>`: the 3D parts `jack ctl xtal nvm vreg label` (`edge`, the gold fingers, has words and no group of its own yet) and the die's blocks `phy mac stats rxq txq dma pcie temp`; the controller's chip and the die's frame share the id `ctl`. The prefix `nic-` keeps them apart from the GPU's, the NPU's and the TPM's ids in the same slot. Until the card lies flat, a part answers as the card (`Machine3D.pickId`); after it each part is a hit and a flight (`Machine3D.rail`, `focusPart`); a die block answers only through the live layer, once the lid is gone. `describeNet` asks `describeNicId` first, so `describeId` finds them; each part has a title, a plain line and a nerd line, and the nerd line carries the card's live numbers while the game has the card. The tests list the ids from `nicIds` (`nicdesc.test.ts`), so a part cannot be drawn without words.

**Verified** (headless Chrome, the real GPU, sandbox game): the 40G and the gigabit card standing, swinging, flat and at the die from zoom 2 to 5: the parts on the PCB, the lid fading off the die, the floorplan upright and covering the whole chip. The pose (jacks on the chip face, the PCB below them): standing, seen from the side, from above the west edge and from the rear, with the plug seating in the jack through the opening of the back wall; flat to the south at zoom 0.7 and 1, the gold fingers on the card's own slot's line and the jacks west. The lit copper with launches forced through `onLaunch` (the sandbox has no link), on the 40G card in that pose: the first jack's pairs lit toward the sink along the 45-degree jogs with the head and the trail, the slot's pairs lit from the fingers' middles. Not verified: the copper under a real link with real frames (the unit tests drive `NicView` with a linked snapshot and the frame counters). Tests: `plan/netmeter.test.ts`, `nic/{nicplan,niclayout,nicglyph,nicshow,nicview}.test.ts`, `ui/nicdesc.test.ts`, `board/render/three/{card3d,nic3d,nicflow,nictrace,chips3d,machine3d}.test.ts`, `board/render/wiring/busflow.test.ts`, the overview's draw ceiling (`overview.test.ts`) and the High golden (`highident.expected.json`).

### The Ethernet chip on the motherboard

The PC's own network chip (the jack on the rear panel, `eth0`) is the same kind of part as the card's controller: a real chip with a die, under mould that fades as the camera comes in. It stands where the 2D board draws it (`GEO.phy`, on every board from the first upgrade on) and wears the controller's floorplan, live. Zoom in on it: the mould fades and the die shows each block. Double-click it to fly there. The board does not move and the chip has no card: there is nothing to fold.

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `board/render/three/phychip3d.ts` | `phyChip(rect, look, id, plan, floor)`: a quad flat package over `GEO.phy` (as tall as the board's other small chips, `CHIP_H`; its gull-wing legs are the board's solids), the laser marking of its look (`PHY_MARK`), the leadframe pad, the die and the frame of the floorplan. It builds with `nicChip` (the card's QFN, `o.height` and `o.die` set) in the board's own frame. `nicChip` works in a frame with layout y as the negative of its y and depth up off the PCB (the card's component side is built in the same one), so `phyChip` gives it the chip's rectangle mirrored in y (`mirror`, its own inverse) and mirrors the die's rectangle back. The die is as wide as the pad (`min(w, h)` less 1 mm): the floorplan covers the whole chip |
| `nic/nicplan.ts` | `BOARD_LINK`: the two looks, `board-fe` (RL-E100, 100BASE-TX, two pairs: one to send, one to receive) and `board-ge` (RL-E111, 1000BASE-T, four pairs); `boardLook(mbps)` picks the look by the board's port speed; `BOARD_NIC` (`board`, the prefix of its ids); `nicInput(visual)` reads either family |
| `nic/niclayout.ts` | `BOARD_BAND` (its band of the die frame, past the four slots'), `boardNicIds`; `NicGeo.face` is null for it (no flat strip) |
| `board/render/three/boardmodel3d.ts` | builds the chip in the board's `small` group, with the floorplan baked on its die (`Board3D.phy`) |
| `board/render/three/machine3d.ts` | `phyBits`: the mould goes in `apart` (it fades alone, so its materials are its own), the die's face goes to `Chips3D`, and a rail `board.nic-ctl` leads a flight to the die |

**The die** is the card's, with two changes, because the board's chip is not a card's. It has no heat sensor: the sim keeps no temperature for it (`BOARD_LINK.heat` is false, so the plan, the wires and the legend leave it out), and the block that takes its row, the host link, reads `to the I/O hub` (`host`) in place of `the host link`. The 100 Mbit chip draws two pairs in its PHY (`pairs`, in `phyGeom`), the gigabit chip four.

**What is real.** The same as the card's, from the board's port. The link is `net.port.kind === 'board'` with a router, a plan and the machine on (`linkOf`); the rating is `board.features.ethernetMbps` (a burnt board has none: no link, no light); the frames are `NetSnap.frames` through a `FrameMeter`, the queues are `net.transfers`. The cable in a card is no cable in the board's jack: the chip shows a dark line and zero counters. It has no power or heat number to show (`nicShow` gives it none). Each part of the die has words (`describeNicId`, the prefix `board`): the controller, the PHY, the MAC, the statistics, the two queues, the DMA engine and the hub link.

**Ids.** `board.nic-<part>`: `ctl phy mac stats rxq txq dma pcie`; the chip and the die's frame share `ctl`. A part answers through the live layer once the mould is gone (`Chips3D.hitAt`); a click on one selects the board (`board3d.ts`), as a click on the board does. The tests list the ids from `boardNicIds` (`nicdesc.test.ts`), so a part cannot be drawn without words.

**Verified** (headless Chrome, the real GPU, sandbox game): the chip from the board view to the die, the mould fading and the floorplan covering the whole pad; the live layer under real traffic (`LINK 1 Gb`, the frame rate, the lanes, queues, DMA engine and hub link lit). Tests: `nic/{nicplan,niclayout,nicglyph,nicshow}.test.ts`, `ui/nicdesc.test.ts`, `board/render/three/{phychip3d,boardmodel3d,chips3d,machine3d}.test.ts`, the overview's draw ceiling and the High golden.

### The router

The router is one machine in four sizes (Home 100 Mbit, Gigabit, 10G switch-router, Core 40G). Its detailed view is a 3D board, built as the motherboard is: real parts on a PCB, and a floorplan only on the SoC's die. Zoom in on the router. The shell fades to nothing and the board shows through, where it lies: flat, on four standoffs over the plinth, parts up, behind the five jacks. **The board never moves.** The player turns the camera down onto it, or double-clicks a part to fly there. A flight to a part that lies looks down on it from the front, tilted as far as the camera goes (`Rail.lie`, `focusPart` in `board3d.ts`). Zoom in on the SoC and its lid fades: the floorplan of the die shows.

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `board/render/three/routerboard3d.ts` | the board: `routerBoardFit(tier, depth)` (pure: every part's rectangle on the plate), `BED` (where it lies in the router's frame), `routerBoard(tier, stand, floor)` (the PCB, four standoffs, the SoC with its die and lid, the DRAM, the flash, the two PHYs, the radio, the power input, the label). Each part is a group tagged `router.<part>` |
| `board/render/three/router3d.ts` | the shell: `routerModel(tier)` (its `shell` group fades; the jacks, the plinth and the feet stay), `routerStand(tier)` (scale, front, depth, place on the shelf), `ROUTER_FLOOR` (the plinth's top, where the standoffs stand) |
| `router/routerplan.ts` | the die's plan: `ROUTER_PARTS` (the numbers of each tier), `routerInput(tier)`, `routerPlan(tier)` over `layPlan`, `ROUTER_DIE` (680 by 680 plan units) |
| `router/routerlayout.ts` | `routerGeo(tier)`, `routerParts` (the hover rectangles of the die's blocks), `ROUTER_BODIES` (the parts that stand on the board), `routerIds` |
| `router/routerglyph.ts` | the symbol of each die block, what lights now (`routerLive`), the geometry both share |
| `router/routerroute.ts` | the way a frame goes: `routerRoute(plan, "rx" or "tx")`, the blocks of each way (`BLOCKS`) |
| `router/routerart.ts` | the die's still art: `routerDie(T, tier)` |
| `router/routershow.ts` | `RouterShow`: what the view shows each frame, read off the snapshot; `limitWords` |
| `router/routerview.ts` | `RouterView`: the live Pixi layer on the die (lit blocks, numbers, packets along the wires, the addresses) |
| `../ui/routerdesc.ts` | `describeRouterId`: the hover words, asked by `describeNet` after `describeNicId` |

**The board.** Sizes are real (23 units a mm). The PCB is as wide as the shell less 100 units a side, and as deep as the room behind the jacks lets it be. The jacks' backs end 15.5 mm behind their faces and the board starts just behind them. The SoC is a flip-chip BGA: a green substrate, a die that covers 85 % of the package (so the floorplan covers the chip), and a nickel lid over both (`pkg` with `lid`). The DRAM is one to four FBGA chips (by tier), the flash a SOIC-8 on gull wings (NOR, tiers 1 and 2) or a TSOP (NAND, tiers 3 and 4). The WAN PHY and the LAN switch chip stand behind the jacks they serve (the WAN PHY behind the west jack, the LAN chip behind the four others). The home and gigabit routers have a Wi-Fi module with two u.FL sockets. The power input is a DC barrel jack, or on the core router a mains inlet over a supply module. The label carries the two MAC addresses of the router (`routerMac`). The traces on the PCB are decoration, seeded by the tier: the router has no routed copper to light.

**The die.** The SoC's floorplan, in the card's style: the WAN MAC (the frame as its fields, the WAN address and MAC), the CPU (the cores, the DHCP lease), the memory controller (its channels), the NAT engine (the stages of a frame and the NAT table, one line for each connection), the LAN switch (its ports and what each has learned) and the traffic block (the counters, the plan's speed, the LAN link, and which limit binds). The wires run through the gutters: `wan-pkt pkt-sw cpu-pkt mem-pkt stat-pkt` and the control wires. The jacks, the magnetics, the PHYs, the DRAM, the flash, the radio and the power input are not on the die: they are parts of the board.

**What is real.** The router has no heat, power or CPU-load number in the game (the wall powers it, and it keeps no state but its place in the chain), so the die shows what passes through it and says so where a number would be. The LAN jack and the WAN jack have a partner by `linkOf` (the jacks' law, `jackleds.ts`). The frames a tick come from `NetSnap.frames` (the PC's uplink port is the router's whole traffic) through a `FrameMeter`; the lanes light with `busy` (a log scale) and the load bars show the share of the router's own rate (`lineFrames(r.mbps)`). The clients in service are `net.transfers` against the slots the router and the plan give (`min(router.clients, isp.clients)`): the engine's table lights a cell for each one. The addresses come from `netAddrs` (see **Addresses**): the WAN IP and MAC, the DHCP lease the PC holds, the NAT table, the MAC table of the switch. The radio says `idle` and `no wireless clients`: the game has none. The spec text (SoC marking, DRAM and flash size, standard) comes from `ROUTER_PARTS`: it describes the part, the game has no such numbers.

**Pose and zoom.** `Machine3D.buildRouter` builds the board when a router stands and the machine is the room's. The room makes the shell see-through over `DEVICE_LOD.router.fade` (0.3 to 0.42, seen zoom at the router's front): `Room3D.setRouterShell(alpha)` fades the shell group (`fadeTree`) and dims its lights with it, and at 0 the shell is hidden. The board is hidden until the shell starts to go (no draw from afar). A part answers a pick as itself only once the shell is mostly gone (`RouterBoard.shown`, `Machine3D.pickId`); before that it is the router. The SoC's lid fades over the die's own band (`DEVICE_LOD.die.fade` times the die's scale in the room); the live layer (`RouterView` in `Chips3D`, kind `router`) runs on the die at 30 draws a second at most, and only while the lid is gone. A router of another tier makes a new board (`syncCables` goes through `buildRouter`). The Build my PC stage has no board (its wall has a router of its own), and a machine without live chips has none either.

**Ids.** `router` is the whole board (a click on the bare PCB, the shell or a jack). `router.<part>` is a part: the bodies `dram flash wanphy lanphy radio power label soc` (`radio` on tiers 1 and 2 only) and the die's blocks `cpu mem wanmac pkt sw stat`, which answer only through the live layer, once the lid is gone. Each body is a rail (`Machine3D.lie`): a double click flies to it. `describeNet` asks `describeRouterId`, so `describeId` finds them; each part has a title, a plain line and a nerd line (`routerdesc.test.ts` lists the ids from `routerIds`, so a part cannot be drawn without words).

**Verified** (headless Chrome, the real GPU, sandbox game): all four tiers lying behind their jacks with the shell gone; the shell at four distances (the lights dim with it); the flights to the board, the SoC (the die's floorplan with the addresses and the NAT table), the DRAM, the PHY, the power input and the radio, each looking down at 0.75 rad. Tests: `router/{routerplan,routerlayout,routerglyph,routerroute,routershow}.test.ts`, `ui/routerdesc.test.ts`, `board/render/three/machine3d.router.test.ts` (flights, the shell, the lying board and the pick), the overview's draw ceiling and the High golden (`highident.expected.json`).

### Addresses

The sim moves bytes. It does not name anyone. So the views and the words make the names, in `plan/netaddr.ts` (`netAddrs(snapshot)`, pure, cached for a snapshot). Nothing is random: each address is a function of the parts that stand (the jack that has the cable, the router, the plan) and of the jobs in flight, so every view shows the same address in every frame and after a load.

| What | How it is made |
|---|---|
| a MAC | in the locally administered range (the second hex digit is 2: no maker owns it). The router: `02:52:4C:0T:00:0X` (RL, the tier, 01 the WAN jack, 02 the LAN side); the board's jack `02:48:41:4B:0T:01`; the ISP gateway `02:49:53:50:00:0T`; a card's MAC is burnt in (`nicmac.ts`, the label prints it) |
| the LAN | by tier: 1 `192.168.0.0/24`, 2 `192.168.1.0/24`, 3 `10.10.0.0/22`, 4 `10.0.0.0/16`. The router is the first host and the gateway, DHCP hands out from `.100` for 24 h |
| the PC's address | the first address of the pool, while the link is up (`linkOf`): a router, a cable in a jack that works, power on |
| the WAN | `203.0.113.x` (RFC 5737): the gateway at `.1`, the name servers at `.53` and `.54`, the router at `.101` to `.200` by the plan |
| a connection | the PC's end gets a port from 49152, the router's end (what NAT made of it) from 1024, the server `198.51.100.x` (RFC 5737) on 443 TCP (DNS jobs: 53 UDP); `natLine` prints it, `frameOn` gives the headers of its frame on each wire (the MACs change at each wire, the IPs at the router only) |
| ARP | the PC's table holds the router, the router's LAN side the PC, its WAN side the gateway |

The router uses it now: the hover words of every router part (`routerdesc.ts`) and the router's die (`routershow.ts`, `routerglyph.ts`) show the WAN address, the lease, the MAC table and the NAT table. With no router, no plan or no link, a part says what is missing and shows no address.

The words of the network ids carry them too (`ui/netdesc.ts`, `ui/nicdesc.ts`): `router` (LAN and WAN address with MAC, DHCP and NAT in the plain words), `wall` (the gateway, the name servers, the public address), `cable.lan` (the PC's end: jack, MAC, IP), `eth0` and `ethN` (MAC always, IP only while the cable is in the jack and the link is up) and the MAC block of the NIC die (`pcieN.nic-mac`, `board.nic-mac`: the port's own address). Test: `ui/netdesc.test.ts`.

The network panel (`NetPanel.tsx`) has two folds from the same model, `addressModel` in `ui/netui.ts`: **Addresses** (the PC's jack, the router's LAN and WAN side and the ISP's gateway, each with its IP address and its MAC; the PC's IP address says why it is missing while the link is down) and **NAT table** (one line for each job in flight: the server, the PC's port and the router's port; the whole `natLine` is the tooltip). Test: `ui/netui.test.ts` "addresses".

## The network lens

The lens shows the path data takes, in the room's own space: plan, router, LAN lead, port, OS drive. It opens with the `N` key or the dock's **Network** button (`NetLensButton` in `economy/ui/shop/Inventory.tsx`; the key is in the Controls list). It shares the screen with the power and airflow lenses: `networkLens` in `src/app/hover.ts` is a signal, and turning one lens on turns the other two off (`hover.test.ts`). It is ready when `netLensReady(s)` is true: unlock level 4, a router or plan, a router in the inventory, or the LAN lead plugged in. It is in the room game only (not in Build my PC).

**Layers.** `Lens3D` (board feature) hosts `NetView3D` next to the power and airflow views, draws it when `networkLens` is on and hides it otherwise. `NetView3D` owns the veil (`makeVeil(NET_VEIL)`: a cool blue that dims the room but lets the machine read), the lines (`NetFlows`) and the pinned words (`AirTags3D`, the same pills as the airflow lens).

**Shape and draw.** As in the power lens, what is built once is split from what changes. `anchorsOf(s, machine)` finds the places: the wall plate (`ispPlate()` in `layout.ts`, the one `room3d.ts` uses), the router (`ROUTER_SPOT`), the lead (`Machine3D.cableRuns()`, run `'lan'`: strands, seated, the jack, its radius) and the OS drive (`Machine3D.partBox`). `netShape(anchors)` makes two lines: the **plan's line** (`ispLine`: from the plate's west side along the wall to the router's back; it is a line of the lens only, there is no real cable there) and one line for each strand of the **LAN lead** (router jack to plug, lifted by the cable's radius so it sits on the cable). The shape is rebuilt when the machine or `machine.cablesRev` changes. A new snapshot only runs `netDraw(shape, s, anchors)`: for each line the down and up pace (`speedLevel` of the ladder's `downMbps` and `upMbps`, log scale 10 Mbit to 20 Gbit; 0 for a way that has no transfer), its state, and the tags.

**Look** (`flows.ts`, one draw call). Each straight stretch of a line is a strip that turns to face the camera, never finer than two pixels each side, additive, depth-tested, drawn after the room (`renderOrder` 1002). The shader finds the pulses, nothing moves on the CPU. Data coming in is a **cyan** comet that runs toward the machine; results going out are **violet** and run back; with both ways busy each has its own lane across the line, so the two colours do not add up to white. The faster the link, the faster and closer the comets. Link up with nothing moving: a faint slow trickle coming in. A line that needs setup (`no-os`): a dim thread. A lead that is out (not plugged in, with a router or a plan present) or a link that is `down`: **red**, pulsing, still. The plan's line is lit only with both a plan and a router. With neither, nothing is lit. The comets run on game time (`dt`: a pause holds them); the red pulse runs on the real clock.

**Words.** Four pills, from the same ladder as the network panel (`limiterLadder`): `Plan` over the plate, `Router` over the router (with the clients being served), `Port` at the jack, `Drive` over the OS drive. The slowest piece is amber and ends with `limit`. A missing piece says so in the warn colour (`No plan · click the plate`, `No router · drop one here`, `Port · plug the cable in`). When the state chip says warn or bad, one more pill (`link`) carries it under the router's last pill. The legend (`legend: 'net'`: a cyan dot `coming in`, a violet dot `going out`) sits under the `link` pill when there is one, else under the router's last pill. Tones: `net` (cyan), `limit` (amber), `warn`, `dim`.

**Addresses.** Dim pills under the pill of each place that has them (`netAddrs`, "Addresses" above; each hangs `under` its pill and is listed after it): `plan-addr` (`gateway 203.0.113.1 · <MAC>`, with a router and a plan), `router-lan` and `router-wan` (`LAN 192.168.1.1 · <MAC>`, `WAN 203.0.113.101 · <MAC>`; the WAN one with a plan), `port-addr` (`eth0 · 192.168.1.100 · <MAC>`, or `no IP address` while the link is down). A place that does not stand has no pill.

**Camera.** Turning the lens on flies to `netWindow()` fitted with `inFree(.., CASE_Z, ROOM_ANGLE, 0.86)`, but only when the camera is clearly higher than that fit (`goal.height > fit.height * 1.1`): a player who is already close is not moved. The view before the flight is remembered. Turning the lens off flies back (to the room overview, or to that view) only when nothing moved the camera in between (`goal` still the lens's own).

**Check tool.** In dev, `window.__net` is the `NetView3D` (`shapeNow`, `drawNow`, `anchorsNow`, `flowsNow`). Tests: `lens/shape.test.ts` (levels, shape, states, words, `netLensReady`), `src/app/hover.test.ts`.

**Limits of the first version.** The plan's line is short and a lens-only line. The lead is thin from far. There is no ring on an open jack (only the `Port` word). Nothing inside the case (PCIe to chipset to RAM and drive) yet.
