# Rendering: the airflow lens (3D air)

Part of the renderer docs; the hub is [`RENDER.md`](../RENDER.md). Code: `src/features/board/render/three/air/`, and the thermal scale in `src/core/render/three/thermal.ts`. The lens's words and numbers (the model) are in `src/features/room/render/desk/airflow.ts`; see "The airflow lens" in [`room.md`](../../src/features/room/room.md).

The lens is a thermal view of the machine, as a thermal camera sees it. The room goes cold and dark. The parts glow by their temperature. The air is particles in the machine's own 3D space, coloured by their temperature on the same scale. The air goes round the real parts, takes heat from the hot ones by the sim's coefficients, and leaves where a fan or a grille lets it. It goes on outside the case as jets, and room air is drawn in toward the intakes: the halo, fading out with the distance. The coolant of a liquid loop is drawn as a liquid in its hoses, the radiator's lanes and the pump head, coloured by its temperature along the loop. Every part that makes, moves or exchanges heat is in the view (see "Heat in the lens"). The power supply is a part of the air like the others: its fan, its unit and its rear vent are in the flow. Nothing here changes the sim: the lens is a picture of the same machine, fed by the same numbers.

## Cost: what is made once, what runs each frame

The case, the parts and the fans do not move. So the flow is solved once for a machine, and a frame only moves particles.

| When                                            | What                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Cost                                                                                |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| a part is fitted or removed (`planKey` changes) | the plan, the grid, one solved flow per fan (the supply's too)                                                                                                                                                   | about 0.2 s of work for a full tower (14,000 cells), done in slices of 4 ms a frame |
| a fan changes speed                             | the fans' flows are added again with the new weights (flow is linear in the fans' flows)                                                                                                                         | one pass over the cells, no solve                                                   |
| each snapshot                                   | the hot parts' temperatures and coefficients (`airParts`), the words, the coolant's temperatures, the thermal scale and the parts' glow                                                                          | tiny                                                                                |
| a liquid loop appears                           | the water's net (`waterNet`: the runs the coolant follows, from the real hoses and the radiator's tubes) and its one mesh                                                                                        | about 40 runs, once                                                                 |
| each frame                                      | about 260 particles of air (a tenth of them halo) move (trilinear sample of the field, eddies, collisions, heat) and the ribbons' buffer is copied; the water is 8 numbers of temperature and one instanced draw | 0.2 to 0.3 ms on the main thread (`AirView3D.cost`)                                 |

There is no per-frame solve and no per-frame grid work. The particles are a structure of arrays; no step allocates. There are few particles on purpose: each one is drawn as a fine streak with its own colour, so fewer particles with more detail read better than a cloud, and the picture stays quiet.

## The frame

The machine's root is the scene space: x east, y up (the layout's y runs down: world y is minus layout y), z toward the glass; 23,000 units to a metre (`UNITS_PER_M`). The air's domain is the case's inside, from the tray (`CASE_Z + 70`) to the glass (`CASE_FRONT`). The six walls are numbered 0 to 5: -x, +x, -y, +y, -z, +z. The front fans are on the east wall (1), the rear fan on the west wall (0), the top fans on the top wall (3). The layout constants are live bindings (`setLayoutTier`): the plan is made again on a tier change.

## The plan

- **Boxes** (`AirBox`): `Machine3D.airBodies()` gives each part's box in the machine's space (device id and kind; the board, the basement, the drive cage and the RAM bank as bodies of their own). `BODY_K` says how well air passes each kind along each axis (0 a wall, 1 free): the board and the basement are walls, the drives' cage lets 0.45 pass along x, a RAM stick lets air run along it and hardly across. The cooler's boxes are its own (`COOLER_AIR` in `cooler3d.ts`: the tower's stack, the stock heatsink, the pump head). The grid makes a thin plate block its normal and let air slide along its face (a harmonic mean of the two cells).
- **Fans**: a wall fan (`kind: 'wall'`: a disc on a wall, in or out) for each case mount with a fan fitted; an inner fan (`kind: 'inner'`: a plane across an axis, air from the cells behind it to the cells before it) for the cooler's fans, the two regulator sinks' and a card's (blowing up into its fins). A radiator's side keeps its fans, which then blow through the radiator's core (a box that passes air). The flow of a fan follows its size, its spec's `airflow` and `FAN_FACE_MS`.
- **Vents**: the rear grille where there is no rear fan (a fitted fan that stops shuts its wall by its own stiffness), the expansion slots, the front's mesh and the top's.
- **The power supply** (`psuBay`, `casing`): the basement is a wall, cut round the supply's bay (a slab left thinner than `SLIVER`, 100 units, is dropped: a wall of no air shuts every cell it touches along its normal, however thin it is, so a thin slab would shut a whole cell). The unit is a porous box (`PSU_K`: the air goes through it, round its parts). Its casing is four walls of WHOLE grid cells (`gridShape` gives the cells: lid, east, south and north layers; a wall inside a cell would shut the cell's whole width and leave a dead pocket). The supply's fan is a wall fan on the floor under the unit (bank `psu`, blowing in), and the air leaves by a grille in the rear wall (`PSU_GRILLE`). So the room's air goes in at the foot, through the unit and out at the rear, and does not go up into the case. A supply whose spec has no `fan` has no floor fan and no rear vent: its unit is a still box. It is a heat part of the air (`airParts`, kind `psu`): its temperature is `psuTempC` (30 C at idle, 33 K more at full load) and it loses 10% of the machine's power as heat.
- Each fan has a `bank`: the id its speed is published under in `fanBank` (`case:front0`, `radiator:top1`, `cooler`, `vrm`, `ramvrm`, `gpu:<slot>`; the supply's, `psu`, is turned by the lens itself: see "Where the fan speeds come from").

## The flow

Each fan alone makes a flux through its plane; the grid's conductances carry it; a wall fan's flow falls as pressure builds against it (its stiffness `kf`: a fan into a closed box stalls), an inner fan's by the conduction of the faces it sits on. That is a linear problem, so each fan is solved ALONE once into a field of velocities (a basis), and the air at any fan speeds is the weighted sum of the bases. `AirSim.levels` takes the weights from `fanBank` (through `levelOf` in `view.ts`: a fan that turns blows at 0.3 to 1.0 of its flow by its speed; one that does not turn blows none). Velocities are capped at `V_MAX`. A face with under a thousandth of its area open (`FACE_MIN`) is a wall when the cells' speeds are taken from the potential: across a wall the potential holds the whole pressure of the room, and a speed worked out from it is an artefact (streaks of 90,000 units a second beside a wall). A machine with no exhaust has its flow stall: the intake brings little and the lens says `NO EXHAUST`.

## The particles

- **Births**: air is born at the intake cells (a front fan, a vent that air comes in through), by the share of the inflow each has, with the room's temperature. A steady count is kept (`target`: a rate of births replaces what has left or aged). A paused game (`dt` 0) holds everything.
- **Motion**: each parcel rides the flow, plus eddies (a velocity that wanders and fades: the streams are not combed) and buoyancy (it rises as it is warmer than the room). What the air does in game time is shown at half pace (`TIME_SCALE`): the real air crosses a case in a second, too fast to follow.
- **Collisions**: the boxes are exact (a per-cell list; the grid only sets the flow). A parcel that runs into a box slides along its face; one born or pushed inside a wall is put out by the nearest face. The case's walls keep it in; where an exit cell is, it dies.
- **Heat** (`exchange`): a parcel inside a hot part's reach takes heat by that part's coefficient `ua` (W/K) from the sim: `power / max(1.5, surface - air)`. A heatsink's fins are at `air + 0.7 x (die - air)`, a card's surface at 0.75 of its rise, the radiator's at the water's mean with the power the loop rejects, the supply's casing at `psuTempC`. The exchange grows with the air's speed over the part (forced convection: `(speed / U_REF)^0.6`, between 0.35 and 2.2), and a part in still air still gives its natural share. A parcel is a small sample of air in the part's boundary layer, so it takes heat `TAKE_GAIN` (5) times faster than the air as a whole would; it never takes more than the part has. Its temperature is the particle's colour, so the heat a part gives is seen on the streaks that pass it. A warm parcel mixes toward the case's mean air in `MIX_TAU` (2.5 s: a plume fades over the width of a case) but never cools below it.
- **Warm parcels**: only the parcels that happen to cross a part would take its heat, and the plume above a part is what a thermal view is for. So a part that is `WARM_MIN_K` (6) K over the room sheds ordinary air parcels: one a second for each `WARM_W` (12) W it gives the air, at most 3 a second. They start at 0.7 of the way to the part's warmth, are carried by the flow, rise by buoyancy and mix away like the rest. They are AIR particles, not a kind of their own, and may swell the pool by `WARM_EXTRA` (60).
- **Trail**: eight points per particle (`TRAIL`). The trail moves on by DISTANCE (a streak is as long as the air is fast, between 80 and 340 units); still air moves it on after `TRAIL_WAIT`.

### The halo: air outside the case

The grid holds the case only, but a fan reaches the room. Two more kinds of particle (`EXHAUST`, `INTAKE`) fill the room round the case. Neither is solved: the room is not in the grid.

- **Jets**: half of the parcels that reach an exit (`HALO_TAKE`, while the pool has room) go on outside by their own place, speed and temperature, along the wall's normal with a spread (`SPREAD`), so the streak leaves the case whole. A jet slows (`JET_TAU`), mixes into the room's air (`OUT_MIX_TAU`) and rises a little by its warmth (`OUT_BUOY`).
- **Draw-ins**: room air is born in a cone in front of an intake (`CONE_COS`), a way off it, and runs to a point on the intake's wall, faster as it nears it (`SINK_R`). It is gone when it arrives (`SINK_END`); the case's own air is born at the intake cell.
- **Fade**: both fade with the distance from the case, smoothly to nothing over `HALO_K` (half) of the case's longest side, between 3,200 and 7,500 units (`AirParticles.halo`). The pool is `HALO_SHARE` (an eighth) of the air's count, 45% of it draw-ins. They are drawn about half as bright as the air inside (`HALO_ALPHA`, 0.55).

### Water cooling

The liquid loop is drawn as a liquid, not as particles (`waternet.ts`, `water.ts`).

- **The net** (`waterNet`): the coolant's way as straight runs, made from the real parts. From the pump's cold plate it goes up the hot hose to the radiator, in along the near tank, through the lanes of the core to the far tank, back along other lanes to the near tank's other port, and down the cold hose to the pump. The hoses are `Machine3D.loopPaths()` (the real cords, hot one first, smoothed); the radiator is `radiatorFlow` of `cooler3d.ts` (its tubes, its two ports, the way a fitting points, the core's half thickness), so the net uses the tubes the 3D model has (`radiatorTubeLayout`). The pump head's own ports and cold plate are `COOLER_AIR.pump`.
- **The lanes**: from the glass side the tubes of a radiator lie edge on, one over another, so a lane drawn in the middle plane shows no gradient. The water in runs along the half of the core nearer the board and the water out along the other half (`half / 2` each side of the middle plane), three lanes each at the depths of the tubes. Seen from the front, the hot water cools along its lane (amber to pink), turns in the far tank, and comes back cold (pink to violet) beside it.
- **The temperature**: each run carries the loop coordinate `s` at both ends, so `waterAt(net, s, hotC, coldC)` says how hot the water is at any place. Eight knots (`KNOTS`) give the shape: cold to the plate, heated across it, hot to the radiator's core, cooled along its lanes (to the middle at the far tank, to cold at the near one), cold back. The sim holds one mass of coolant (`loopC`) and a rise of a few kelvins (`loop.hotC`, `loop.coldC`), so the lens shows the water's spread `SPREAD` (3.5) times as far from the mean: this is a display choice, so that the gradient reads on the scale the parts glow by.
- **The look**: every run is one strip of one instanced mesh, turned to face the camera, never under a pixel and a half each side. It is shaded as a round tube of liquid (a lit body, one soft streak along a side, a soft rim) and laid over the picture with normal blending and both faces drawn (a strip's winding turns with the run's way, so back faces must not be culled; and a part that glows behind the water must not wash its colour out). Bands of light run along it at the speed of the flow (`board.loop.flow`); still water has none and is dimmer. The depth test is off: the water shows through the hose's sleeve and the radiator's plates, which glow by their own temperature round it (`HOSE_HOT`, `HOSE_COLD`, the radiator's skin).
- **The radiator**: the sim has no temperature of its own for it. The loop is one mass of coolant; the radiator is a conductance to the air (`rejectW = pumpPerC x (loopC - room) + radK x (loopC - air in)`, `board/sim/thermal.ts`), and a top radiator breathes the case's air while a front one warms it. In the lens it is a porous part whose coefficient comes from `rejectW`: the air that crosses its core leaves warm (`WARMS THE CASE` for a front radiator), its body glows at the water's mean temperature, and the water in its lanes is the picture of the exchange.

### The power supply

The supply is in the grid like any other part (see "The plan"). Its fan on the floor draws the room's air up through the unit and out of the rear vent, and the unit is a heat part, so the air that crosses it takes its heat by the same rule as the rest. The air inside the casing is drawn over it (the x-ray box, see "The drawing"). The supply's fan speed is `1 + 8 x load` by the 2D board's rule, eased by a `FanMotor` in `view.ts` (the 3D machine has no PSU fan to publish it), and is the weight of the flow of the bank `psu`.

## The drawing (`ribbons.ts`)

The picture is a thermal view: a cold dark room, parts that glow by their temperature, and streaks of air and water in the same colours.

- **The scale** (`core/render/three/thermal.ts`): one palette for every temperature the lens shows. Blue at the foot (the room's own air), then violet, crimson, red, amber, yellow, and white at the hot end. `heatOf` places a temperature on it; the scale bends by `THERMAL_BEND` (0.8), so the first kelvins over the room already show. The scale runs from the room's temperature to the hottest part's, eased (`SCALE_TAU`, 1.5 s) and kept between 30 and 70 K over the room (`SPAN_MIN`, `SPAN_MAX`), so a cool machine does not look hot. The particles' shader is built from the stops (`thermalGlsl`); the parts' glow and the scale pill read the same list. A colour means one temperature everywhere.
- **The parts** (`Machine3D.thermal`, `PartSkin.thermal`): each part with a skin darkens its own colours (`THERMAL_BODY`) and glows in the scale's colour for its temperature. It eases there, and back when the lens closes. The temperature is `surfaceTemps`: the sim's for the part, or the surface where the air meets another (a heatsink's fins, a card's shroud, a radiator's core, the supply's casing), so the glow and the air agree. The parts that have a skin are listed in "Heat in the lens".
- **The veil** (`makeVeil`): a full-screen sheet, under the ribbons, that multiplies the picture by a deep blue (`uTint`). The room goes dark and cold and keeps its light and shade; the air is added over it.
- **The ribbons**: one instanced mesh with a fixed pool; each particle is a strip along its trail of 8 points, turned to face the camera (the side vector is taken end to end of the trail, not step by step: the head's step is short and turns with every eddy). The width is between 0.8 and 2 pixels a side whatever the size the air has in the machine (a thread from far, a fine streak from close), and tapers to the tail; the alpha falls along the trail and the head has a brighter core. Additive blending, linear colours, no tone mapping.
- Two passes. The air is depth-tested: parts hide the streaks that pass behind them. The air inside the supply's unit (the x-ray box, `Ribbons.xray`) is drawn with the depth test off, as it runs inside a casing that would hide it. The water is a mesh of its own (`water.ts`, render order 1002, after the ribbons).
- **Quiet cold, loud heat**: the room's own air keeps 0.2 of the brightness (`COLD_SHARE`) and the air that took heat stands out, so the eye finds where the heat goes.
- **Near fade**: streaks nearer to the eye than 2,500 units (11 cm) are invisible, whole at 7,000 (30 cm) (`NEAR_FROM`, `NEAR_TO`). The camera of a close-up stands inside the case; without the fade a long line would cross the die the player came to see.
- **A pitfall**: `pow(x, y)` of a negative `x` is not a number, and a GPU's division can leave the tail's `1 - t / 7` a hair under zero. One NaN in the HDR buffer comes out of the bloom as a pixel of no colour and no alpha, and the page behind the canvas shows through: black dashes along the streaks. The shader clamps `along` to 0..1 and takes `pow(max(along, 1e-4), 1.2)`. `tools/aircheck.mjs` also checks that no particle has a value that is not a number.
- The density follows the graphics setting (`effective.particles`: 0.4, 0.7, 1.0 of 260).

## Heat in the lens

Every part that makes, moves or exchanges heat is in the view. `airParts` makes the heat parts (they heat the air); `surfaceTemps` gives each skin its temperature; the fans are in the plan (`PlanFan`) and publish their speed in `fanBank`.

| Role      | Parts                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | In the lens                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Source    | processor (under a heatsink: the fins carry its heat; bare: its package), GPU, NPU, NIC (a card with a heatsink), memory sticks, drives (all in the cage's box), regulator and memory regulator (their sinks), the supply (10% of the load, lens only) | heat part with its own coefficient `ua`, and a glowing skin. The TPM makes too little heat to show                                                                                                                          |
| Exchanger | air heatsink (stock and tower), regulator and memory sinks, the pump head and its cold plate, the hoses, the radiator                                                                                                                                  | porous parts (the air crosses the fins or the core); the head, the hoses and the radiator are the water's side: the head glows at `loop.hotC`, the hot hose at `hotC`, the cold hose at `coldC`, the radiator at their mean |
| Mover     | case fans, radiator fans, the cooler's fan, card fans, the regulator and memory sinks' fans, the supply's fan, the pump                                                                                                                                | fans are in the flow and turn at the speed they publish; the pump sets the speed of the bands of light in the water (`loop.flow`), and still water is dim. Fans themselves are not hot: they have no glow                   |

The skins: `PartSkin` in `core/render/three/materials.ts` owns the materials of a part (they are not merged into the palette), and a part's meshes of one skin material are merged by `mergeStatic(root, { own: true })`, so a skin costs a few draws and not one for each fin. The radiator (`radiatorModel` of `cooler3d.ts`), the sinks (`sinkBody` of `sink3d.ts`) and the hoses (`LoopHoses.skins`) have them.

## The words

`airflowModel` (room) makes the tags and the warnings from the snapshot: `RAD 61 W - COOLANT 46 C - IN 49 C OUT 43 C`, `CASE AIR 25 C`, `PSU CASE 38 C`, a part's temperature and trend, and the warnings `NO FAN TURNS`, `NO EXHAUST: HOT AIR STAYS IN`, `NO INTAKE: THE CASE STARVES`, `FAN OFF`, `MACHINE OFF`. `AirView3D.words` pins each at a 3D point on the glass side (`CASE_FRONT - 150`); `AirTags3D` draws them as DOM pills (sharp at any zoom, no draw calls). Under the `ROOM` tag hangs the scale pill (`AirTag.scale`, `under`): a bar of the thermal scale from the room's temperature to the hottest part's (whole degrees), so a colour can be read as a temperature. It is made again when the whole degrees change.

## Where the fan speeds come from

`fanBank` (`core/render/fanmotor.ts`) holds a speed per fan id. The 3D machine's fans publish it: `Machine3D.frame` eases each fan (`needs`: its cable) and writes the speed of its bank (`fanBank.setLevel`; the radiator's fans on a radiator side are `radiator:<mount>`, the others `case:<mount>`; the cooler, the VRM and the memory sink by their header). The cards' fans come from `gpuview.ts`. The supply's fan (bank `psu`) is turned by the lens itself (`AirView3D`: the 3D machine has none). So the air blows as hard as the fans on the screen turn, ramps up with them and dies away as they coast down. The Build my PC stage never steps its game, so its machine is off unless a dev harness forces the speeds.

## What it does not do

- It does not feed back to the sim: the sim cools by area (`board/sim/airflow.ts`) and has no particles.
- The room is not solved. The halo (jets, draw-ins) is a picture of where the fans reach, drawn on rays; the room's air does not flow round the case or the desk.
- The flow is a potential flow with stiffness and conduction, not Navier-Stokes: no turbulence model beyond the eddies, no wake behind a part, no pressure on the glass. The shape of the streams (round a part, through fins, out of a grille) and the way a stopped or missing fan changes them is what it shows.
- Parts that move while the plan stands (the side panel, a cable) are not boxes. The plan changes only when a part is fitted or removed.
