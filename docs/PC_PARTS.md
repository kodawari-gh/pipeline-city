# PC parts: review list

The list of every PC component in the game, with the state of its review. Goal (2026-11-07): each part is the best it can be, fits the parts it meets, and has an animation for going in and coming out. Update a row when you finish a check.

One model per part: the case (`machine3d.ts`), Build my PC (`buildmode3d.ts`, which draws a `Machine3D`) and the gallery (`showcase.ts`) call the same builder with the same palette (`builtinTheme()`); only the level of detail differs. A part that wears 2D art bakes it through an `ArtSet` (`artset3d.ts`).

Legend: `[x]` done and checked, `[ ]` open, `[~]` partly. Columns: **Model** the 3D model, **Fit** it sits in its socket and clears its neighbours (`render/three/fit.test.ts`), **Graph** its removal blocks are in `sim/removal.ts`, **Anim** its fit/remove motion in the build mode.

## Review pass 3 (2026-11-08): real size, mounts, detail parity

Goal: every part other than the case, the board and the CPU is real size, has its real fixings and its fan provisions, and has the detail of those three. The scale is the case's: 23 units a mm (its fans, screws and standoffs). The board's sockets are the reference for what plugs into them (the DIMM slot is 2400 long by the owner's choice). Measured with the gallery's models (`showcase.ts`), so the shop, the gallery, the game and Build my PC agree. Tick a row when its model is changed, shot in the gallery and in the game, and committed.

## Review pass 4 (2026-11-08): real 3D detail, no 2D art on boxes

The owner's call: the models from the NVMe on were low quality next to the coolers, the RAM and the VRM sinks. The 2D drawing pasted on a raised box (`Raise`) was a limit of the old 2D board; the 3D parts are now built as real hardware: real geometry and materials, at real size. The shared kit is `pcbkit3d.ts`: chip passives with tinned ends (0201 to 1206), moulded or lidded packages with their laser marking and pin-1 dimple, inductors, crystals, the PCB slab (its laminate edge), the solder-mask top (traces, vias, silkscreen, gold pads, cut-outs; also the underside), gold edge fingers, labels draped over the parts under them.

- [x] NVMe `nvme2`, `raid2` (`nvme3d.ts`): an 80 x 22 x 0.8 mm board with chamfered finger corners, the M key slot and the notch with its gold ring; 38 + 37 gold fingers at 0.5 mm; the PMIC with four inductors, a lidded 13 mm controller with its land-side caps and crystal, an 8 x 13 mm DRAM, two 12 x 18 mm NAND, about 150 passives; a maker's label draped over the DRAM and the NAND (name, capacity, interface, barcode, matrix code, serial); the underside's mask, test pads and serial sticker; an M2 pan-head screw into a brass hex standoff. The RAID 1 stick (11 W) wears a black anodised heat sink instead of the label: a thermal pad, chamfered base, edge skirts, two banks of fins and a plateau with a brushed silver insert, engraved; 9 mm over the board.
- [x] TPM `tpm1..2` (`tpm3d.ts`): an 18 x 13 x 1 mm board on a 7 mm female 2 x 7 connector (13 cells, the key plugged, entries and V notches moulded); the 13 tails come up through the south end in concave solder fillets. North of them a TSSOP-28 LPC TPM (TCG pinout, turned half round: pin 1 and its dimple at the NE, the marking upside down), C1 to C3 on VDD, C4 on VSB, R1 on PP, R2 and the 0603 status LED D1 east. The copper is routed, not random (`tpmcopper.ts`, rules in `docs/render/board.md` "Routing"): each LPC net from its leg to its header contact, ground pours on both faces, +3VSB on the bottom. ## Fit rules (checked in `fit.test.ts`)

- A GPU's or an NPU's cooler stands less than a finger's width (15 mm) from the next slot's card: it covers that slot (`COVERS`). A NIC leaves room.
- A card's cooler covers the M.2 sockets under it, by card and slot (`OVER_M2`): its stick cannot come out first.
- The cooler covers the CPU. The loop's hoses come off before its pump and its radiator.
- The board comes out bare. The case comes out last.
- Every part has a board tier with its slot (and a wide enough PCIe slot).

## Known visual defects

- [x] The 16 CPU shop tiles looked the same: at tile size the lid hid the die. Since 2026-11-08 a still opens the part (`PartHooks.open`: the lid's reveal done), so each CPU's tile shows its own floorplan (`npm run stills`).
- [x] Holes flickered: the perforated sheets (case mesh, hex and slot grilles, the Mid's top cap) were hard alpha cuts on mipped textures, with the dark backing a few units behind. Now alpha-to-coverage plus a depth bias (`casekit3d.ts` `perfMat`, `case3d.ts`), and the same for the art tiles (`artlod.ts`). A second step: `perfMat` fades the sheet to a solid, darker average once a hole is under about 6 to 18 texels per pixel, because no filter draws a sub-pixel hole steadily. Measured (2026-11-07, dev server, front view with the grille, 11 frames each a 0.0015 rad turn apart, share of pixels changed by more than 90/765 between neighbours): Full case 12.93 % before, 10.72 % after the coverage mask, 6.53 % after the fade (3.52 % with the maps stripped, the floor of real motion); views with no grille are equal (0.637 and 1.965 %). Mid case (Mid board and Mid tower fitted in the stage): front 0.79 %, top 0.40 %, side 0.48 %. A 2x texture (10.70 %) and a blended alpha (11.82 %) did not help; both were dropped. The clean test is a sub-pixel step (0.00005 rad per frame, so real motion is nil and any change is shimmer): Full front 0.27 % old, 0.15 % new, 0.10 % with no holes at all (the floor); Full side 0.26 -> 0.02 %; Mid front 0.33 -> 0.008 %, Mid top 0.0095 -> 0.0035 %, Mid side 0.071 -> 0.0023 %. So the shimmer is down to the no-holes floor. The residual shares at larger steps are camera motion, not flicker: the same view gives 0 % with the camera still, 1.12 % at 0.0004 rad per frame, 6.5 % at 0.0015 and 15.3 % at 0.006, so they scale with the step. At the slow step (legit motion is tiny) the old material (alpha cut, no coverage, no bias, no fade, rebuilt in the page) against the new one: Full front 3.15 % -> 1.12 %, Mid front 2.44 % -> 0.13 %, other views equal within 0.1 point. The `__build` dev hook (`BuildStage.jump`) lets a script repeat it.
- [x] First review pass (2026-11-07, close-up shots in Build my PC): CPU socket and cooler, RAM bank, M.2 row, PCIe row, chipset, no clipping or gaps found. Cards read flat at that zoom by design.

## Build my PC mode

- Entry: main menu, "Build my PC". Code: `render/three/buildmode3d.ts` (the stage), `render/three/buildpick.ts` (pure: which socket takes a part, which device owns a hit, a box on the screen), `board/ui/BuildMode.tsx` and the files it composes (`BuildShelf` over the shared `economy/ui/shop/PartShelf.tsx`, `BuildMachine` with `BuildStart`, `BuildBars`, `BuildBench` with `benchhud.ts`, `BuildCard`, `builddrag.ts`, `buildhud.ts`; see `docs/UI.md`).
- Stage: the machine alone on a neutral floor, no room. Orbit camera.
- A free sandbox: no inventory, no money. Every part is unlimited.
- Shelf (left): the catalogue. A rail of categories (All first; an icon and a short name each, `railLabel`; the full name and the count in the tooltip), then the tiles: the still, the name, five tier pips (the tier against the best of its kind) and the price. Drag a part onto the PC: it fits the socket under the pointer when that socket takes it, else the first free socket, else the socket of the part it replaces. Click a shelf part to fit it in the first free socket.
- Take out: drag a part off the PC onto the shelf (or press its Remove in the list). The graph gives the steps; each step plays its animation. The part is gone; take another from the shelf.
- Parts sit inside the case: the side panel must be open to drag them (the Side panel button, or it opens when a drag starts).
- Fit: blocks come out, the part goes in, the blocks go back.
- Animation per kind: `render/three/buildmotion.ts` (keyframes, out and in: fitting plays the removal backwards with its own wording). Each channel follows a monotone cubic through the keys (`poseAt`): no stop at a key the path runs through, no overshoot. A twist turns the part about its own centre (`placed`). A part fades over the last stretch of its way out and comes in from see-through (`solidAt`, `partfade.ts`: per-part copies of the shared materials).
- Water loop: the hoses (`LoopHoses`, cooler3d.ts) exist only with a liquid pump and a radiator (`hasLoop` in `sim/removal.ts`; a GPU water block would join there, none exists yet). They are their own graph node (`hoses`): they come off before the pump or the radiator, and go on last, after the parts and before the cables. The pump unscrews, twists free of the paste and lifts (motion `pump`); the hoses slide off their fittings and lift.
- Only the shape decides (`GameOptions.looseFit`, set by `BuildStage.open`; the sandbox and the campaign keep their strict rules): a part goes in whenever it fits a socket (a cooler still cannot go back without its CPU, a card too wide for its socket fits nowhere). A supply too small, a card the supply has no lead for, and a drive that holds the OS are not refusals: the machine then behaves as a real one would (a supply a little short slows every clock, one far short trips, a part that runs hot throttles, makes errors or burns, a start-up check that fails stops the machine), and Build my PC says so in the open, never in silence: the summary's pill goes amber or red with the problem in words, the right panel lists each problem (`BuildAlerts.tsx`; the pointer on a row outlines the part, a click picks it), the part's row carries a mark, and a change that causes a new problem shows it on the hint line, amber, as a refusal does. The list is `sim/buildalerts.ts` (`buildAlerts`: the game's own checks, `checkCompat` and `bootCheck`, and a dry run of the thermal model for the heat); the machine itself is not run on the stage. The OS follows its drive (`Game.keepOs`): when its drive comes out it goes on another drive, or waits and goes on the next one that goes in. The benchmark says the cause of a machine that does not start (the power button does nothing, the power-on check, a tripped supply, a burnt part) and of a score that is low because the machine ran slower than its parts (a supply too small, a part that ran hot: the warning mark on the time). The hint line says what a change made worse, not what it only moved (`newAlerts`: a problem is one family in two degrees, so a supply that sagged and now trips is news, one that tripped and now only sags is not). What only the shape can still refuse is the clearance in the case: a socket of another type or width, a fan the mounts do not take, a second radiator, a case too small for the board (`buildloose.test.ts`); a bigger board brings a bigger case with it. Undo of a removal or a swap brings back what the step took out for good (a fan whose heatsink went, a cooler whose CPU went: `PartStep.along`), after the part itself.
- A refusal (a part that fits no socket, a removal that cannot be done, a case that is already fitted) shows amber on the hint line for six seconds, from the end of the job (`BuildStage.refusal`; its × lets it go). It also goes in the log of what was done, which the right panel shows only with `?debug`.
- Board and case swaps: the parts come out first. The door closes. The board lifts out (`Machine3D.boardRoots`), then the case pieces (`caseRoots`). The new case lowers over the board, then the new board settles in.
- Empty start (`BuildStage.shown`): "Empty" is nothing at all, not a machine without parts: the stage shows no case. `shown` has three states (`Shown`, `sim/buildspecs.ts`): `nothing`, `case` (a bare case with no board in it) and `machine`; `bare` is true for the first two. The player brings a case in from the shelf (`fitShell`: the case comes down over the desk, then a steel panel opens), then a board (it lowers into the case as in a swap, `held`; a bigger board brings a bigger case, the sandbox is not gated by the case), then the parts. A bare case is built with `buildEmpty` (the tray shows its standoffs and the front-panel leads lie coiled on it, as a new case ships them). With `nothing`, `Machine3D` builds no case at all (`shown: 'nothing'`: no side panel, no door; every door call is a no-op). The sim `Game` always holds a case and a board: it keeps its tier 0 pair, with no devices, held aside while `shown` is not `machine`, so the sandbox has one set of rules. With `nothing` a board or a part is refused on the hint line ("Fit a case first") and `dropTarget` is null for it; with a bare case any part but a board is refused ("Fit a motherboard first"). A case swap on a bare case keeps it bare. Each is a `swap` step, so Undo walks back: the machine, the bare case, nothing, the machine before. The summary says "No case" or "No motherboard" (`readiness(..., shown)`), the right panel shows a dashed "No case" or "No motherboard" row, and the shelf shows the cases, then the boards, then the processors once a board is in.
- Side panels: a steel panel (Mini, Micro: `DOOR_SEES` false) hides the inside, so the stage opens it for a job and keeps it open while the job runs; a glass panel (Mid, Full) shows the inside shut. Each case has its own swing time and the steps wait for it (`DOOR_SECONDS`, `doorWait` in `casedoor.ts`), by the clock first and then for the panel itself (`Machine3D.doorT`: a slow frame counts for at most 0.1 s, so the panel can lag the clock, and a step must not move a part through a panel still in its way). A case swap shuts the panel before the case lifts and opens a steel one again once the new case stands. See `docs/render/load.md`.
- The stage shows the room's monitor on standby (`Monitor`, props3d.ts).
- Pointer (as the game's board: `board3d.ts`, `drag3d.ts`):
  - **Hover**: the part, the empty socket or the shut side panel under the pointer is outlined (the post chain's outline pass, `Post.setOutlined`) and named in a tooltip (`TooltipBubble`) with a last line of what a click or a drag does. The cursor is a pointer. A step that plays, or a drag, hides both.
  - **Click a part**: it is picked (outlined) and its card opens beside it (`BuildCard`: the game's picker shell with its tail; the numbers, "Take out" with its step count, Focus, and that dropping another of its kind on it swaps). Delete takes it out. Escape, the card's close button or a click on empty space lets go. A click on a row of the right panel does the same; the row outlines its part while the pointer is on it (`BuildStage.glow`).
  - **Click the shut side panel**: it opens (`case.side`; the plate over it takes rays only while no step plays: `setOpen(inside, busy)`). O opens and shuts it. Double-click on a part flies to it; elsewhere it frames the machine again.
  - **Drag from the shelf**: the side panel opens, and every socket that takes the part is outlined while it is held (`fitSlots`: free sockets, and the sockets of parts it would replace). The ghost over the PC is green when the drop fits, red when it fits nowhere (and a drop there says so). Let go on a socket to fit it there.
  - **Drag a part off the PC** (on the stage or by its row): it is outlined, the shelf shows its drop zone, and a drop there takes it out with everything in its way. A drop elsewhere on the stage says to use the shelf.
  - Keys: O side panel, P power view, A airflow view, F focus the picked part, R or Home the whole machine, Delete or Backspace take the picked part out, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z), Escape (lets go of a drag, then the pick, then leaves). The pick follows its part when a swap or a preset rebuilds the machine, and clears when the part is gone.
- HUD: top centre the summary (ready to start, or the reason in amber, from `bootCheck` with the mains on; budget; the draw against the supply with its meter; parts; Benchmark), top right the Power and Airflow lenses (`Lens3D`, the `powerLens` / `airflowLens` signals) and the Graphics dock (`toggleGraphics`), at the bottom the line of what to do now (the step that plays has its Skip), then two bars: the machine (side panel, Undo / Redo: one fit or removal each, or one whole machine for a start (Empty, a preset, Full rig, a saved build: the history keeps the last eight machines, `KEEP_SWAPS`); a new case or board on a machine clears the history, on a bare case or an empty stage it is a step Undo takes back; speed) and the camera (Front, Angle, Side, Top, Focus on the picked part: `BuildStage.preset`, `focusDevice`).
- Drag hint: over the PC, the ghost says which socket the part would fit in, or that it fits none (`BuildStage.dropTarget`), and a pulsing box marks that socket on the board (`showTarget`). A part going in settles: it dips past its seat and comes back (`SETTLE_*` in buildmode3d.ts).
- Right panel (`BuildMachine.tsx`): the header (the part count, and a "Start from" button that opens `BuildStart.tsx`; an empty machine shows it at once). `BuildStart` is one list from nothing to everything: Empty, the presets (Budget, Gaming, Workstation: `BUILD_PRESETS`, built at once by `BuildStage.buildPreset`), Full rig; each row has a picture of the machine (a baked 4:3 thumbnail, see "Start list pictures"; Empty has a dashed frame of the same size, as it puts nothing on the stage), five tier pips and what it fits (`presetBits`). Under it the saved builds, each with the picture taken when it was saved (`ui/savedbuilds.ts`, browser storage; `BuildStage.layout` / `loadLayout`; a delete takes two presses) and "Save this machine…", which opens an inline name field. A start has no confirm: it replaces the machine as one `swap` step in the history, so Undo brings the old machine back (the swap is its own inverse: the step holds the game that was left), and the hint line says what was done with an Undo for eight seconds. Then the case and the board as two rows (a click shows their shelf), the fitted parts grouped by kind with subtotals and 32 px stills (a group of more than three folds to one line; the picked part opens its group), and at the foot one row with the benchmark's overall score, Quick run and Open. "What was done" is for whoever debugs: `?debug` only. The numbers of the summary come from the part tables (`sim/buildspecs.ts`).
- Start list pictures: `public/stills/builds/<key>.webp` and `manifest.json` (the size, a hash per picture that the page asks it by, and the recipe each was taken from). `npm run buildstills` (`tools/buildstills.mjs`) plays each start on the real stage in a headless Chrome on the real GPU and takes `BuildStage.capture`: the whole case from the start view with its side panel off, framed by the pure helpers in `render/three/buildshot.ts` (`pictureWindow`, `fitFactor`, `centred`). Empty has none. `sim/buildpreset.ts` holds the games the starts build (the stage plays the same ones), `recipeOf` (a hash of what a build holds) and `PICTURE_KEYS`. `sim/buildstills.test.ts` fails when a recipe changed since the bake (a preset, a tier, a catalogue part) and runs in `npm run build`: bake again (`npm run buildstills`, or `-- --only <key>`). A change to a part's model is not seen by it. The page loads the manifest once (`ui/buildstills.ts`); a row shows its icon until the picture is there. A saved build keeps its own picture, a data URL that `capture` takes when the player saves (none when the capture fails).
- Benchmark: the screen (`BuildBench.tsx`) runs one almanac task or a set (Quick, a group, the ticked ones, all) on a copy of the machine; the runner is `sandbox/sim/pcbench.ts` and the task list `sandbox/sim/benchtasks.ts` (see `src/features/sandbox/SANDBOX.md` "Benchmark"). `BuildStage.loadBench` loads the runner when the screen opens (it carries the whole sim, so the UI never imports it); `runBenchmark(ids)` runs the tasks; `bench`, `benchWas`, `benching` and `benchIssues` are the stage's signals. Any change to the machine ends a run and clears `bench`; the cleared scores stay in `benchWas` as the deltas. `capsWith` (`economy/sim/clients.ts`) reports the CPU's logical cores, as the threaded jobs need them.
